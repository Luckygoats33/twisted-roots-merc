Carrie & Eric  ·  Siletz, Oregon

Our Roots

A neighborhood mercantile for Siletz.

The short version

We got tired of driving to Newport for one thing.

That's genuinely it. That's the whole business plan.

You're out of dog food on a Sunday. The kid needs poster board for a project due tomorrow. A pipe starts dripping under the sink at 5:40pm. The wind picks up and you realize the flashlight in the junk drawer has been dead since 2019.

Every one of those is a forty-minute round trip and about eleven dollars of gas to buy a seven dollar part. Do that four times a month, for a whole town, for years, and it adds up to something ridiculous — time and money leaving Siletz over batteries and bread.

So we opened the place we kept wishing was already here.

Carrie

Runs the front, the kitchen, and most of the decisions.

Carrie's the one who decides what goes on the shelf, and she reads every single request that comes through this website. She bakes the biscuits, she'll write your item down on a notepad if you don't want to mess with a QR code, and she is the reason the chicken salad recipe is not written down anywhere.

If you ask her for something twice, it usually shows up.

Eric

Runs the yard, the Fix It wall, and the sign out front.

Eric carved the sign. He'll also tell you which flapper fits your toilet, come out and jump your truck in the lot, and talk considerably longer than you intended about what's running in the river right now.

He picked the Fix It inventory the way a person who's actually fixed things picks it: galvanized and stainless over pretty, exterior over interior, one good version of each thing instead of six mediocre ones.

What we decided not to do

Deliberately small.

There's no app. There's no membership. There's no account to make, no points to collect, no "My Roots Dashboard" to log into. You can walk in, call, text, or check this website — that's the whole list.

We're not doing delivery yet either. If it turns out there are folks here who genuinely can't get to the store, we'll start a Tuesday and Friday run. But we're not going to invent a second full-time job for two people before we know anybody needs it.

We also don't special-order everything for everybody. What we do instead is write down what people ask for, and when the same thing comes up enough times, it goes on the shelf permanently — for everyone, not just the person who asked.

That's slower. It's also the difference between a store and an errand service.

The name

Why "Twisted Roots"?

Look at anything that's been growing on this coast for a hundred years — a spruce on a headland, an alder leaning over the river. Nothing out here grows straight. The wind won't let it. What holds it up isn't the trunk, it's the root system, which is knotted and stubborn and goes further sideways than down.

That felt about right for a small town, and about right for a store that intends to still be here in twenty years.
