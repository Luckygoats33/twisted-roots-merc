Deck Screws, Wood Screws and Structural Screws

Three bins that look similar and are not interchangeable. Using the wrong one is how a railing comes off in somebody's hand.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

The fastener wall confuses people because the differences are real but not obvious across a bin. A deck screw and a wood screw look alike. A structural screw looks like a big deck screw. They are engineered for different loads and different environments, and the price difference reflects something.

What each one is for

ScrewBuilt forNot for
Wood screwInterior joinery, trim, cabinets, hardwareAnything outdoors or structural
Drywall screwDrywall to studs, and nothing elseEverything else, it is brittle
Deck screwDecking, fencing, exterior boardsCarrying structural load in shear
Structural screwLedgers, joist hangers, posts, beamsNothing, other than being expensive
Concrete screwInto masonry, with a pilot holeWood to wood

The one to be clear about is drywall screws. They are hardened and brittle, they are not corrosion resistant, and they snap under shear load. They are cheap and they are the wrong screw for every job except drywall. A deck built with drywall screws will come apart, and this is not theoretical.

What makes a deck screw a deck screw

Three things. A corrosion resistant coating or stainless material, a shank designed to pull boards down without splitting, and a head shaped to countersink into softwood without a pilot. Most have an unthreaded section under the head so the threads pull the top board tight against the one below instead of jacking them apart.

The coating matters here more than the shape. Modern pressure treated lumber uses copper-based preservatives that corrode ordinary zinc plating, and the salt in coastal air accelerates everything. See the post on galvanized and stainless, because for anything exposed near the water it is the single most important decision on the shelf.

Structural screws, and where they replace lag bolts

Structural screws are rated by the manufacturer for specific load values, and that is what you are paying for. They have a heat-treated core so they bend rather than snap, threads engineered for withdrawal strength, and they generally need no pilot hole where a lag bolt would.

They belong anywhere the connection is carrying a building load: a ledger, a joist hanger where the manufacturer specifies them, a post to beam connection, stair stringers, a railing post. A railing that a person leans on is a life safety connection, and it deserves the right fastener and usually a bolt through with a nut and washer rather than any screw at all.

What they do not do is replace an engineered connection. If a deck ledger attaches to a house, there are specific requirements about how many fasteners and where, and there is flashing behind it. That is a permit conversation, not a screw choice.

Length and gauge

The general rule for wood to wood is that the screw should go at least twice as deep into the receiving piece as the thickness of the piece being fastened. A three quarter inch board wants at least an inch and a half of penetration, so a two and a half inch screw.

JobTypical screw
Decking to joists2 1/2 in deck screw, coated or stainless
Fence pickets1 5/8 to 2 in exterior
2x to 2x framing3 in structural, or nails
Interior trimFinish nails or 1 1/4 in wood screw

Anything into concreteConcrete screw with a masonry pilot

Driving them without ruining them

Use the right driver bit and seat it fully. Most exterior screws today are star drive or square drive rather than Phillips, and the reason is that a Phillips bit is designed to cam out, which strips the head. If you have a bin of Phillips deck screws, drive them with steady pressure and a fresh bit.

Set the clutch on a drill rather than running it at full torque. Overdriving a screw into softwood strips the hole and the screw holds nothing, and you cannot tell by looking. If a screw spins freely, back it out and put a longer one in a fresh spot rather than leaving it.

Screws, driver bits and pilot bits are on the Fix It wall, and the lumber to use them on is out in the yard. If wood is splitting when you drive, read the pilot hole post.
