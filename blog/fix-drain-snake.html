Fix It

Using a Drain Snake Without Wrecking the Pipe

A hand auger will clear what a plunger cannot, provided you feed it, feel it, and never force it around a bend.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A hand snake is a coiled steel cable with a corkscrew tip on a reel. You feed it down the drain, spin it into the blockage, and pull the blockage back out or chew it up enough that water carries it away. It works on things a plunger will never move, particularly hair and roots. It also punches holes in old pipe if you use it like a battering ram.

What you need

  • A 25 foot hand snake
  • Gloves. The cable will be filthy and it will pinch
  • A bucket and old towels
  • A five gallon bucket of water to flush with afterwards

Where to feed it in

Not through the sink strainer if you can avoid it. Take the P-trap off and feed the cable directly into the wall arm. You skip two tight bends, you keep the cable out of the trap, and you can see what you are doing. On a tub, take the overflow plate off and go in there rather than through the drain. On a toilet, use a closet auger, which is a short snake in a protective sleeve designed so the cable never touches the porcelain. A bare cable in a toilet bowl scratches the glaze and those scratches stain forever.

The technique

  1. Feed about a foot of cable into the pipe and tighten the thumbscrew on the drum.
  2. Crank the handle clockwise while pushing gently. The cable turns as a unit.
  3. When it stops advancing, loosen the thumbscrew, feed another foot or so, retighten, and crank again. Short bites. This is the whole method and it is slower than people expect.
  4. When you hit the obstruction you will feel the resistance change and the cable will start to bind. Keep turning clockwise with light forward pressure. You are trying to screw the tip into the clog, not shove it aside.
  5. Once it bites, pull back slowly while still turning. Hair comes back up on the tip in a mat. This is genuinely disgusting and it is also proof you found it.
  6. Repeat. A clog rarely comes out in one pass.
  7. Reassemble, then dump a full bucket of water down fast. A tap does not have the volume to carry loosened debris out of the line.

What not to do

Never reverse direction under load. Turning counterclockwise while the tip is bound unwinds the cable and it kinks. A kinked cable is scrap.

Never force it around a bend. If the cable stops and will not turn, back it off six inches and try again with slower rotation. Forcing it makes the cable double back on itself inside the pipe, and now you have a steel spring wedged in your drain that a plumber has to remove.

Do not put a powered drum machine into old galvanized or into thin-wall traps unless you know what you are doing. A power snake in corroded steel pipe will happily punch straight through the wall of the pipe. If your drain lines are old steel, hand power only.

How far is far enough

FixtureClog usually withinTool
Bathroom sink2 to 3 ftHand snake, or just clean the trap
Tub or shower2 to 5 ftHand snake through the overflow
Kitchen sink5 to 15 ftHand snake through the wall arm
ToiletUnder 3 ftCloset auger only
Main lineAnywhereNot a hand snake job

When it is not your job any more

If you run 25 feet of cable and never find resistance, the blockage is past where a hand snake reaches and it is in the building drain or the line out to the septic tank. If more than one fixture is affected, same conclusion. Out here that usually means roots, because alder and spruce roots find a clay or old cast iron line and move in. Root cutting is a machine job and it is worth paying for. A camera down the line before anybody digs is money well spent.

Snakes, plungers, gloves and replacement traps are all on the Fix It wall. If your drain is slow rather than blocked, start with the slow drain post.
