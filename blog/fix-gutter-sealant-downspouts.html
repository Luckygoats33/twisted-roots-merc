Fix It

Gutters, Seams and Downspouts on a Coast That Rains Sideways

A gutter that drips at the corner is a five minute fix. A downspout dumping against the foundation is the expensive one.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Gutters matter more here than in most places. It is not the intensity of the rain, it is the duration. Water running down a wall or pooling at a foundation for months on end is what causes the rot, the wet crawlspace and the mildew people spend real money on later. Most gutter failures are small and fixable from a ladder in an afternoon.

What you need

  • Gutter sealant, the flexible kind rated for standing water
  • A wire brush and rags, plus denatured alcohol
  • Gutter spikes or hidden hangers with screws
  • Downspout extensions or a splash block
  • Gloves. Old gutter debris is unpleasant

Clean before you diagnose

Half of gutter problems are just full gutters. Fir needles, moss, and the fine grit that washes off asphalt shingles compact into a mat that holds water and rots the fascia behind it. Scoop it out by hand into a bucket, then flush with a hose and watch where the water goes. Everything below assumes you have done this first, because a clogged gutter overflows at the exact spot you would otherwise blame on a bad seam.

Sealing seams and corners

  1. Find the leak with the hose running. Dry the area completely, inside and out.
  2. Wire brush the inside of the joint. Remove old failed sealant, loose paint and oxidation. This is the step that decides whether the repair lasts.
  3. Wipe with denatured alcohol.
  4. Apply gutter sealant on the inside of the gutter, over the seam, in a generous band. Inside, not outside. Water sits on the inside, and sealing from the outside means water gets into the joint and works behind your patch.
  5. Tool it smooth so debris does not catch on a ridge.
  6. Give it the cure time on the label before it rains, which around here means checking the forecast carefully.

Use gutter sealant rather than general caulk. It is formulated to stay flexible under constant immersion and to bond to aluminum and galvanized steel. Silicone works on metal but will not take paint, and standard acrylic caulk fails quickly sitting in water.

Slope, hangers and sagging

Water should move. A gutter wants about a quarter inch of fall per ten feet toward the downspout. If you find standing water in the gutter after the rain stops, it is either sagging or was hung level.

SymptomCauseFix
Standing water mid-runSag or wrong slopeRe-hang with correct fall
Overflow at one spot in heavy rainClog, or undersized downspoutClean, add a downspout
Water behind the gutter at the fasciaMissing drip edgeNeeds flashing, not sealant
Gutter pulling away from the houseSpikes loosened in rotten fasciaScrews and ferrules, check fascia for rot
Drip at a jointFailed seam sealantClean and reseal from inside

Old spike-and-ferrule gutters loosen because the spike works out of a fascia that has softened. Driving it back in buys a season. Hidden hangers with a screw are the better replacement and they go on without removing the gutter. If the fascia behind it is soft, stop and deal with that first, because a hanger screwed into rot has nothing to hold.

Downspouts are the half that matters

A gutter that catches water perfectly and then delivers it to the base of the wall has achieved nothing. Get the discharge well away from the foundation. Four to six feet minimum on level ground, further if the ground slopes back toward the house.

Extensions, a splash block on a graded slope, or a buried solid pipe running to daylight all work. What does not work is a downspout emptying onto a flowerbed against the wall, which is the setup behind an enormous share of the wet crawlspaces in this county.

Also check the elbow at the bottom is still there and pointed away. They get knocked off by lawnmowers and nobody notices for a year.

Guards, and whether they help

Under fir and spruce, most gutter guards fill with needles rather than keeping them out. Fine mesh guards do work but they still need brushing off, and a guard that makes you feel like you never have to look at the gutter again is the one that lets a clog build up unseen. Whatever you install, look at the gutters twice a year: once after the needles drop and once mid-winter.

Gutter sealant, hangers, downspout parts and roof patch are on the Fix It wall, and if the water problem is bigger than a gutter, there is drainage material out in the yard.
