Fix It

Painting on the Coast, Where Nothing Wants to Dry

Humidity does not just slow paint down. It changes how it cures, and a coat applied in the wrong window will fail early.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Paint is a film that forms as water or solvent leaves it. When the surrounding air is already close to saturated, that departure slows down or stops. On this coast the practical result is that a paint job which would take a weekend anywhere else takes careful timing, and that a coat put on in the wrong conditions can look fine and fail in two years.

The numbers worth knowing

  • Most latex paint wants relative humidity under about 70 percent and surface temperature above 50 degrees
  • The surface must be at least 5 degrees above the dew point, or moisture condenses onto your wet paint
  • Stop painting outside several hours before sunset, because evening dew is the classic ruin

The dew point rule

This is the one that catches people. On a mild coastal afternoon the air can be 60 degrees with a dew point of 57. As the sun drops, the siding cools to below the dew point and condensation forms on paint that has not yet cured. The result is surfactant leaching, which shows as brown or tan streaks running down the wall, or a dull blotchy finish, or in bad cases poor adhesion.

The practical version: paint mid-morning to mid-afternoon. Start once the overnight dew has burned off, and stop with enough daylight left that the film sets before the temperature falls. On a summer day here that can be a five hour window, and on a spring day it might be two.

Preparation is more of the job here than elsewhere

Damp climates grow things. Every north-facing wall out here carries mildew, algae or moss, and paint over any of that fails within a season because the growth keeps going underneath and pushes the film off.

  1. Wash. A bleach and water solution, or a proprietary mildew wash, scrubbed and then thoroughly rinsed. Test for mildew by dabbing bleach on a dark spot. If it lightens in a minute, it is mildew, not dirt.
  2. Scrape all loose and failing paint. If the edges of the sound paint lift when you scrape, keep going.
  3. Sand the edges so the transition is not a visible ridge.
  4. Let it dry. Genuinely dry. Wood that has been washed needs a couple of dry days, and a moisture meter reading under about 15 percent is the target if you have one. Painting over wet wood traps moisture and the paint blisters.
  5. Prime bare wood, all of it, including end grain and the back of any new trim.
  6. Caulk after priming, not before, and use exterior sealant rather than interior caulk.

What to buy for these conditions

SituationChoose
Exterior siding and trim100 percent acrylic latex, with a mildewcide
Bare or weathered woodExterior primer first, always
Bathrooms and kitchensInterior acrylic with mildew resistance, satin or semi gloss
Rooms that never dry outAvoid flat. Sheen sheds moisture and cleans
Metal railings and gatesRust inhibiting primer, then exterior enamel
Painting in cool weatherLook for a low temperature acrylic rated to 35 degrees

Cheap exterior paint is a false economy here. The difference between a mid and a top tier exterior acrylic is the resin content and the mildewcide package, and on a wall facing weather that is the difference between five years and twelve.

Recoat times, and being patient

The recoat time on the can assumes moderate temperature and moderate humidity. In our conditions, double it. A second coat applied over a first that is dry to the touch but not cured underneath traps solvent and moisture, and the result is a soft film that stays tacky, marks easily and peels in sheets later.

Interior work has its own version. A bathroom that has just had a shower in it is not a paintable room. Run the fan for an hour, open a window on a dry hour, and give each coat overnight rather than four hours.

The unglamorous conclusion

Most exterior paint failure out here is not the paint. It is a surface that was damp, dirty or biologically active when it was coated, or a coat applied into an evening dew. Prep and timing are the whole game, and the actual painting is the easy part.

Brushes, rollers, painter's tape, spackle, sandpaper, primer and sealant are all on the Fix It wall. We do not mix custom colors, and there is a good paint counter in Toledo for that.
