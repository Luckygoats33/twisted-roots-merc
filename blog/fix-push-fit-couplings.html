Fix It

Repairing a Pipe Without a Torch

Push-fit couplings put copper and PEX repair inside the reach of anyone who can cut a pipe square and clean a burr off it.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

There was a time when repairing a copper water line meant draining the system, drying the pipe, and soldering in a wall cavity with an open flame, which is a legitimately bad idea in an old house with dry framing. Push-fit couplings changed that. Cut the bad section out, push the fitting on, done. They work on copper, PEX and CPVC, in most cases with the same fitting.

What you need

  • Push-fit couplings in the right size, or a push-fit shutoff valve
  • A tube cutter, or a PEX cutter for plastic
  • Emery cloth or a deburring tool
  • A marker
  • The removal clip that comes with the fittings, or a bag of them

How they actually work

Inside the fitting is a stainless steel ring with teeth that bite into the pipe and hold it, and behind that an O-ring that does the sealing. You push the pipe in past both. The teeth resist pull-out and the O-ring seals against the outside wall of the pipe. Which is why the outside wall of the pipe has to be clean, round, and free of scratches for the last inch. A burr or a deep gouge will run right past the O-ring and give you a permanent slow drip.

The procedure

  1. Shut the water off and open a low faucet to drain the line down. The pipe does not have to be bone dry, which is the whole point, but you cannot push a fitting onto a line under pressure.
  2. Cut the pipe square. A tube cutter, not a hacksaw. An angled cut will not seat.
  3. Deburr the inside and, more importantly, clean and smooth the outside. Emery cloth around the last inch and a half until it is bright and smooth.
  4. Measure the insertion depth. Every brand prints it, usually around an inch for half inch pipe. Mark that depth on the pipe with a marker.
  5. Push the fitting on with a twist until your mark disappears. It takes more force than you expect. If it is not to the mark, it is not sealed, regardless of how solid it feels.
  6. Do the second end the same way.
  7. Turn the water on slowly and watch. Then check again in an hour, and again the next day.

Where they belong and where they do not

SituationPush-fit
Burst or split section in an accessible runIdeal
Replacing a seized angle stop under a sinkIdeal, use a push-fit shutoff
Joining copper to PEXIdeal, no transition fitting needed
Buried undergroundNo
Sealed permanently inside a finished wallAvoid if you can
Old thin, pitted, or galvanized pipeNo

The buried and behind-drywall cases are not about the fitting failing. They are about access. Anything mechanical with a rubber O-ring should be somewhere a person can look at it. Solder or crimp is the right answer for a joint that is going to be sealed up forever.

They also do not belong on galvanized steel. The outside of old galvanized is rough and pitted and the O-ring has nothing smooth to seal against.

Taking them apart

Push-fit fittings are removable, which is a real advantage when you are dry-fitting a repair. You need the small horseshoe clip that came with them. Slide it over the pipe against the collar, press the collar in, and pull the pipe out. Without the clip you will not get it off. Buy a couple of spares and hang them on a nail near the water heater, because the one time you need one is the one time you cannot find it.

Being honest about the limits

Push-fit fittings are excellent for a repair and they are genuinely rated for permanent use. What they do not do is turn an unqualified person into a plumber. If the pipe you are looking at is corroded along its whole length rather than split in one spot, you are patching a system that is failing, and each repair buys less time than the last. If you are re-piping a house, if the leak is in a slab, or if it is on the supply side before the main shutoff, that is a licensed plumber and it is not close.

Couplings, shutoff valves, tube cutters and emery cloth are stocked at the Fix It wall. Before you cut anything, know where the main shutoff is.
