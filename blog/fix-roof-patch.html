Roof Patch, and Being Honest About When It Will Not Stick

Roof sealant works on a clean dry surface in reasonable weather. All three of those are in short supply when you need it.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Roof patch sealant is a genuinely useful product with a narrow set of conditions in which it performs. Almost every complaint about it comes from somebody applying it to a wet, cold, mossy roof in the middle of a storm, which is exactly when a leak announces itself. Knowing what it can and cannot do saves you a wasted trip up a slick ladder.

What you need

  • Roof patch sealant, in a tube or a tub
  • A putty knife or a caulk gun
  • Fiberglass reinforcing mesh for anything bigger than a crack
  • A stiff brush and rags
  • A tarp, rope and something to weight it, for the interim

Tonight, in the rain, the answer is a tarp

If it is dark, wet or windy, do not try to seal a roof. Get a tarp over it, run it up over the ridge so water cannot get under the top edge, and secure it with rope and weights rather than nailing through good roofing. Then wait for a dry window and do the repair properly. Sealant applied over standing water traps the water underneath and gives you a patch that fails and a wet deck that rots.

Tarps, rope, ratchet straps and everything else for that half of the job live over in storm supplies.

Conditions the sealant actually needs

ConditionRequirement
SurfaceDry, or a wet-surface rated product
TemperatureAbove 40 to 50 degrees for most products
CleanlinessNo moss, no loose granules, no dust
Rain afterSeveral hours of dry weather to skin over
Surface materialCheck the label for the roof type

There are wet-surface and cold-weather formulations, and out here they are worth keeping on the shelf specifically because our leaks happen in November rather than July. They still will not bond to moss or to standing water.

The moss problem, which is the local one

North-facing roofs on this coast grow moss thick enough to hold water against the shingles permanently. Nothing sticks to moss. Before any patching, that area has to be brushed off, and moss removal itself needs care: never pressure wash asphalt shingles, because you will blast the granules off and take years off the roof. Brush gently downward with a stiff broom, or use a zinc or copper treatment and let it die back.

If moss is your leak's real cause, patching the symptom accomplishes little. The whole roof needs cleaning and probably a zinc strip at the ridge.

Applying it

  1. Find the actual leak. Water travels along rafters and shows up several feet from the entry point. Go into the attic with a light and follow the stain uphill.
  2. Clean the area thoroughly. Brush, then wipe.
  3. For a crack or a nail hole, work sealant in with a putty knife, forcing it into the gap rather than laying it on top.
  4. For anything bigger, bed a piece of fiberglass mesh in a layer of sealant, then coat over the top so the mesh is fully covered. Extend well past the damage on all sides.
  5. Feather the edges so water runs over the patch rather than into an edge.
  6. Always work with the shingle lap. Sealant belongs under the upper shingle and over the lower one, matching the way water flows.

Where a patch is the wrong tool

A patch is a repair for a discrete failure: a nail pop, a cracked boot around a vent pipe, a lifted shingle, a small hole. It is not an answer to a roof that is generally worn out. If the shingles are curling at the edges, if the granules are gone and the mat shows through, if there are multiple leaks, or if you are patching the same roof every year, you are spending money on delay.

The most common single leak point is the rubber boot around a plumbing vent pipe. Those crack from sunlight long before the roof does, and there is a purpose-made replacement that slips over the pipe and is a genuinely good repair.

The part about ladders

A wet mossy roof on a slope is slippery in a way that is difficult to appreciate until you are on it. If the roof is steep, if it is wet, if you are alone, or if you are not certain about the ladder, hire it. The repair costs a hundred dollars and the fall costs considerably more. There is no version of this where getting on the roof in a storm is the right decision.
