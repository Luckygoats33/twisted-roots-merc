Fix It

Sealing a House Against Mice

A mouse gets through a gap the width of a pencil. Traps deal with the ones inside. Sealing is what stops the next ones.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Every autumn when the rain starts, mice move indoors. Trapping is necessary and it is not a solution, because as long as the route in exists, the supply is unlimited. The only thing that changes the situation is closing the openings, and there are fewer of them than you think.

What you need

  • Coarse steel wool, or copper mesh, or hardware cloth
  • Expanding foam, to hold the mesh in place
  • Sheet metal or hardware cloth for larger openings
  • A flashlight, and the willingness to crawl
  • Caulk and a caulk gun for small gaps

How small a hole they need

A house mouse fits through a quarter inch opening, about the width of a pencil. A young rat needs about half an inch. They do not chew their way in from nothing, they find an existing gap and enlarge it, and they are excellent climbers, so the roofline matters as much as the foundation.

Where the holes are, in order of likelihood

LocationWhat to look for
Under the kitchen and bathroom sinksOversized holes where pipes come through
Crawlspace ventsRusted or torn screen
Where the siding meets the foundationGaps at the sill plate
Dryer ventMissing or stuck damper flap
Around the water line and electrical entryUnsealed penetrations
Under exterior doorsWorn threshold or no sweep
Garage door cornersWorn side and bottom seals
Soffit and roofline gapsAnywhere a tree branch touches the roof
Around the wood stove pipeNeeds fire-rated materials, not foam

Do this from the outside on a bright day, working slowly around the whole perimeter, and then again from inside the crawlspace with a light. Look up as well as down. Rub marks and droppings mark the routes they use.

What actually stops them

Foam alone does not. They chew straight through cured foam, and a foam-only patch will be reopened within a week. The rule is a barrier they cannot chew, held in place by something that seals air.

  1. Pack the opening tightly with coarse steel wool or copper mesh. Copper does not rust, which matters in a crawlspace on this coast, and it is worth the extra.
  2. Seal over and around it with expanding foam or caulk, so the mesh is held and the air gap is closed.
  3. For openings larger than a couple of inches, cut a piece of hardware cloth, quarter inch mesh, and fasten it over the hole with screws and washers, then seal the edges.
  4. For a hole in a wooden surface, a piece of sheet metal screwed over it is permanent.
  5. Replace torn crawlspace vent screens rather than patching them.

The bits people forget

Garage doors. The rubber bottom seal wears at the ends and leaves a gap at each corner that is exactly mouse sized. Replacement seals and side weatherstripping are cheap.

Dryer vents. The flapper sticks open with lint and stays open. A vent hood with a proper damper, cleaned occasionally, closes a highway.

The wood stove and flue penetration. Do not put ordinary foam near it. Use fire-rated sealant and metal.

Overhanging branches. Mice and rats use them like a bridge. Trimming anything touching the roof is part of this job.

Inside, while you are at it

Once the outside is closed, the inside matters less, but sealing the holes under sinks and behind appliances stops mice moving between walls and living space, and it is quick. Escutcheon plates around pipes cost a couple of dollars and cover a hole neatly.

Then remove the reason they are staying. Food in sealed containers including pet food, which is the single most common attractant in a rural house, bird seed off the floor of the shed, and no cardboard boxes of anything edible in the garage.

Being realistic

In a rural house on this coast surrounded by field and forest, you are not going to achieve a permanently sealed building. What you can do is close the easy routes so the pressure drops from constant to occasional. Do a walk around every autumn before the weather turns, because seals wear, screens rust and new gaps open as the building moves.

Steel wool, copper mesh, hardware cloth, foam, sealant and door sweeps are all on the Fix It wall. Doing the door bottoms properly is covered in the sweep post.
