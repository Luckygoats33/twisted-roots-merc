Fix It

Squeaking Hinges, and the Right Thing to Put on Them

Two minutes and no oil dripping down your door frame. There is a method and it works on the first try.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A hinge squeaks because the pin is rubbing dry inside the knuckle. It is not a serious problem and it never gets better on its own. The usual approach, spraying penetrating oil at the closed hinge and swinging the door, works for about a week and leaves an oily stripe down the jamb that will collect dust for years.

What you need

  • A nail or a small punch, and a hammer
  • A rag
  • Grease, petroleum jelly, or a dry lubricant. Not thin penetrating oil
  • Fine steel wool, if there is rust

The method that lasts

  1. Open the door and wedge something under it so it cannot move or drop.
  2. Work on one hinge at a time. Never pull all the pins at once, or the door falls on you.
  3. Put the nail or punch against the bottom of the pin, in the small opening underneath, and tap upward. The pin will rise out of the knuckle.
  4. Pull it out and wipe it clean. If it has surface rust, take it off with fine steel wool.
  5. Apply a thin film of grease or petroleum jelly along the pin. Thin. A heavy coat just squeezes out onto the door.
  6. Slide it back in and tap it home.
  7. Move to the next hinge.
  8. Swing the door a dozen times to distribute it, and wipe any excess off the knuckles.

Done this way it stays quiet for years, because you have put lubricant on the actual bearing surface rather than hoping some ran down there.

Why not penetrating oil

ProductOn hinges
Thin penetrating sprayWorks briefly, evaporates, attracts dust, drips
Petroleum jellyExcellent, stays put, cheap, in the cupboard already
White lithium greaseExcellent, the proper answer
Dry PTFE or graphiteGood where drips would stain, and in locks
Cooking oilWorks today, goes rancid and gummy

Penetrating sprays are solvents with a light oil carrier. They are designed to free seized fasteners, and the solvent flashes off leaving very little behind. That is the opposite of what a hinge wants, which is a film that stays.

The coastal version of a stuck pin

Out here hinge pins on exterior doors seize with corrosion, and tapping from below does nothing. Do not beat on it. Spray the knuckle with penetrating oil, this is the one job it is for, wait fifteen minutes, and try again. Support the door and tap firmly with the punch. If it still will not move, take the hinge screws out instead and remove the leaf with the pin still in it.

If a pin is badly corroded, replace it. Loose pins are sold and they cost very little. On exterior doors near salt air, stainless steel hinges are worth the money. The plated ones rust from the inside of the knuckle where you cannot see it, and the first sign is a squeak.

While the hinge is open

This is a good moment to check the screws. Hinge screws loosen over years, and a loose hinge is the cause of half the doors that rub. If any screw spins, that is worth fixing now with a longer screw into the framing, and the door will close better afterwards.

Also look at the knuckles themselves. If they are worn oval, the hinge is done and the door will sag no matter what you lubricate. A hinge is a few dollars. Replace one at a time, leaving the others in place, and the door stays hung while you work.

The squeak that is not a hinge

If the noise happens at one point in the swing rather than throughout, the door is touching the jamb or the floor rather than squeaking at the pin. Look for a rub mark. That is a fitting problem, not a lubrication problem, and greasing hinges will not touch it.
