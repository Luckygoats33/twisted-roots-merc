Water Around the Base of the Toilet, and the Wax Ring

Wipe it dry and watch. What you learn in the next ten minutes decides whether this is a seven dollar part or a floor repair.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Water on the floor at the base of a toilet is one of three things, and they are wildly different in cost. It is condensation, it is a leak from the tank hardware running down the outside of the porcelain, or the wax ring under the toilet has failed. Only the third one is urgent, and it is the one people ignore longest because the puddle is small and it comes and goes.

Find out which one it is first

  1. Dry the floor and the whole outside of the toilet completely. Every surface.
  2. Put a folded paper towel flat on the floor tight against the base, all the way around.
  3. Do not flush for an hour. If the towel is damp and the porcelain is beaded with water, it is condensation. Common out here in a bathroom with no fan and cold well water.
  4. Now flush a few times and watch the towel. Water that appears only right after a flush, seeping out from under the base, is the wax ring.
  5. Run a dry finger under the tank, under the supply connection and under the tank bolts. If those come away wet, your leak is above the floor and it is a much smaller job.

Why wax rings fail on the coast

A wax ring is exactly what it sounds like: a doughnut of wax that gets squashed between the toilet horn and the flange in the floor. It does not glue anything, it just seals. It fails when the toilet moves. And out here toilets move, because the subfloor around a bathroom in an old coastal house is often soft, the flange is often screwed into wood that has been damp for years, and every time somebody sits down the whole assembly rocks a sixteenth of an inch. Do that a few thousand times and the wax loses its seal.

The leak is sewage water, which is why this one does not wait. It goes into the subfloor first and you smell it before you see the damage.

The repair

  1. Shut off the supply, flush, and sponge both tank and bowl dry. A wet toilet is heavier and it will drip on your floor on the way out the door.
  2. Disconnect the supply line at the tank.
  3. Pop the caps off the two closet bolts at the base and take the nuts off. If they spin without loosening, cut them with a hacksaw blade.
  4. Rock the toilet gently side to side to break the wax, then lift straight up. Bend at the knees. Set it on a towel or a piece of cardboard, not on the tub rim.
  5. Stuff a rag in the open drain so nothing falls in and nothing comes up at you.
  6. Scrape all the old wax off the flange and off the bottom of the toilet. Putty knife. It is unpleasant and it takes longer than you expect.
  7. Inspect the flange. If it is cracked, or the bolt slots are broken out, or the screws spin in soft wood, stop and deal with that. A repair ring or a new flange screwed into solid material is required. A new wax ring on a broken flange will fail again.
  8. Set new closet bolts in the flange slots, press the new wax ring onto the flange, pull the rag, and lower the toilet straight down onto the bolts.
  9. Sit on it. Genuinely. Your weight compresses the wax evenly better than the nuts will.
  10. Tighten the nuts a little at a time, alternating sides. Snug, not tight. Overtightening cracks the base and you have bought a new toilet.
  11. Reconnect the supply, turn it on, and check for a full day before you trim the bolts and cap them.

What tells you to call a plumber

If the floor around the flange flexes underfoot, if a screwdriver sinks into the subfloor, or if the flange sits well below the finished floor, this has stopped being a wax ring job. Rotten subfloor under a toilet on a damp coast is common and it is structural. Setting a new toilet on soft wood just resets the clock on the same leak. Somebody has to cut the floor open, and that is worth paying for if you are not going to enjoy it.

Wax rings, closet bolts and supply lines are all stocked at the Fix It wall. If your toilet also rocks, fix that at the same time and read the piece on a loose toilet first, because shimming after the fact will break the new seal.
