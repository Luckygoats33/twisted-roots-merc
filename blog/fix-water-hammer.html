Fix It

That Bang in the Wall When the Washer Shuts Off

Water hammer is a column of water hitting a closed valve. It is loud, it is fixable, and it is quietly damaging your pipes.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Water in a pipe has mass and momentum. When a valve slams shut, all that moving water stops instantly and the energy goes somewhere: into a shock wave that travels back up the line and shakes the pipe against the framing. That is the bang. It is called water hammer, and beyond being irritating it puts real stress on joints, on fixture connections and on the appliance valves that caused it.

Where you will hear it

  • When the washing machine fills and shuts off
  • When the dishwasher cycles
  • When you close a single-lever faucet quickly
  • When a toilet finishes filling

Try the free fix first

A lot of older houses have air chambers: short capped stubs of pipe above each fixture that hold a pocket of air, and that air cushions the shock. Over time the air dissolves into the water and the chambers fill up, at which point they stop working. Recharging them costs nothing.

  1. Shut the water off at the main.
  2. Open the highest faucet in the house, and the lowest one, and let the system drain completely. Give it a few minutes.
  3. Close the lowest faucet, then turn the water back on at the main.
  4. Let each fixture run until the sputtering stops and it flows clean.

Draining the system lets air back into those chambers. If the banging goes away and comes back in a year, this is your problem and this is your annual chore.

If that does not fix it

Either the house has no air chambers, or the noise is not really hammer. Two other things get mistaken for it.

Series of rapid knocks, fadingValve closesHammer plus loose pipe

A pipe that is not properly strapped will bang against a joist even from mild pressure changes. In an accessible crawlspace, this is the best repair available: find the loose run, and secure it with pipe straps, with a strip of rubber or felt between the pipe and the strap so it can expand without squeaking. Do not clamp it rigid. Copper moves as it heats and a hard clamp trades a bang for a tick.

Arrestors, which are the real fix

A water hammer arrestor is a small sealed cylinder with a piston and a permanent air charge behind it. Unlike an air chamber it cannot waterlog. They screw onto the washing machine hookups, or install on the line near the offending appliance, and they solve the problem properly.

The washing machine is the usual culprit because its solenoid valve closes almost instantly. A pair of arrestors on the hot and cold hose bibs behind the washer is a twenty minute job with a wrench and thread seal tape, and it is where most people should start.

Pressure, and when to get somebody in

High house pressure makes everything worse. Anything over about 80 psi is out of spec, and it wears out fill valves, faucet cartridges and water heaters as well as causing hammer. A gauge that screws onto a hose bib costs very little and tells you in ten seconds. If pressure is high, the fix is a pressure reducing valve on the main, and that is a plumber.

Also call if the banging is severe and you are on old galvanized, or if you have started seeing damp spots after the noise appeared. A hammer strong enough to be alarming is strong enough to work a joint loose, and inside a wall you will find that out the slow way.

Thread seal tape, pipe straps and pressure gauges are on the Fix It wall. If you are going under the house anyway, look at what needs insulating while you are down there.
