Fix It

Wood Filler, Wood Rot, and Knowing the Difference

Filler fixes damaged wood. It does not fix rotten wood, and on a coastal house that distinction is expensive to get wrong.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Out here everything wooden on the outside of a house is in a slow argument with moisture. Trim, sills, thresholds, deck posts, the bottom of door casings, the corner boards on the north side that never see sun. Somewhere in that argument a soft spot appears, and the question is whether you are looking at damaged wood or decayed wood. They need different answers.

The screwdriver test

Push a screwdriver into the suspect area with moderate hand pressure. Sound wood resists. Wood that takes the tip a quarter inch is soft. Wood that swallows the screwdriver to the handle is rot, and it goes further than what you can see, because fungus travels through the grain ahead of the visible damage.

What filler is actually for

Wood filler is for cosmetic and small structural gaps in sound wood: a gouge, an old fastener hole, a chipped edge, a nail head to cover before painting. It has no structural strength worth counting on and it does not stop decay.

ProductUse forLimit
Water-based wood fillerInterior, nail holes, before paintShrinks, not for exterior
Solvent-based fillerInterior and light exterior, small repairsSmells, sets fast
Two-part epoxy fillerReal exterior repairs, sills, trim profilesNeeds sound wood to bond to
Exterior caulkGaps and joints that moveNot for filling holes in a surface

For anything outdoors on this coast, two-part epoxy is the product that lasts. Ordinary filler in an exterior application absorbs water, swells, and pops out in a season, and it leaves you a hole that is now wetter than it was.

Rot has to come out

Decay is a living fungus. If you fill over it, you have sealed moisture in with a food source and it keeps going, quietly, under your nice paint job. Two years later the trim board fails and now the framing behind it is involved.

  1. Cut or chisel back until you reach wood that is hard and takes a screwdriver point with resistance. Go past the discoloration.
  2. Let it dry out. Genuinely dry, which on this coast may mean days with a fan on it or waiting for a dry stretch.
  3. Treat the exposed sound wood with a borate wood preservative. It soaks in, it kills what is left, and it discourages the next round.
  4. Then decide: epoxy consolidant and filler for a shaped piece worth saving, or cut the section out and scarf in new material.
  5. Prime every face of any new wood, including the end grain and the back, before you install it. End grain is where water gets in.

Using two-part epoxy

Consolidant first if the remaining wood is punky at the edges. It is a thin liquid that soaks in and hardens the fibers, and it gives the filler something to bond to. Then mix the filler by the ratio on the tub, not by eye, and work it in with a putty knife. Overfill slightly, because you shape it after it cures.

Epoxy cures by chemical reaction, so unlike everything else on this list, damp weather does not stop it. Cold slows it right down though. Below about 50 degrees it will take much longer, and there is a limit below which it will not cure properly at all.

Once hard, it works like wood. Rasp it, sand it, and it holds a screw. Then prime and paint, because the epoxy itself is not sunlight stable.

Where the real fix is carpentry

Stop and get somebody in if the rot is in a structural member: a deck post, a beam, a header over a window, a rim joist, the bottom plate of a wall. Also if it is in a window sill that carries the window, or if you find the frame behind the trim is involved. Epoxy is astonishingly good at rebuilding a decorative profile. It is not a substitute for a piece of wood that is holding a building up.

And the more important repair is usually not the wood at all. Rot means water is getting somewhere it should not, and if you do not fix the source, you will be back with the same screwdriver in the same spot. Look up: a failed gutter, a missing drip edge, caulk that has split, a deck board that traps water against a post.

Wood filler, epoxy, borate treatment, chisels and exterior sealant are on the Fix It wall, and replacement trim and framing stock is out in the yard.
