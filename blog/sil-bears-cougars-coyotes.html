Around Siletz

Bears, Cougars, Coyotes and Elk as Neighbours

What actually lives out here, how likely you are to have a problem with each, and the one habit that prevents most of them.

4 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

People new to the area tend to worry about the wrong animal. The one that will cost you money is the raccoon. The one that will damage your vehicle is the elk. The bear is a real presence and a manageable one, and the cougar is around and you will probably never see it.

The one habit

Almost every conflict with wildlife out here traces back to food. Rubbish, pet food, bird feeders, fallen fruit, a dirty barbecue, chickens without a proper coop. An animal that finds food at a house comes back to that house, brings its offspring, loses its caution, and eventually becomes a problem that gets solved badly for the animal.

So: secure the bin, feed pets indoors, take feeders down when bears are active, clean the grill, pick up fallen fruit, and keep livestock properly housed. That single list prevents the large majority of everything below. The bin specifically is covered in its own piece because it is the most common failure.

The animals themselves

Black bears

Present throughout this country, and they are black bears — there are no grizzlies in the Coast Range. They are shy, mostly nocturnal around people, and overwhelmingly interested in food rather than in you.

If you meet one: do not run. Make yourself obvious — talk in a normal firm voice, wave your arms, and give it a way out. Back away slowly while facing it. Do not get between a sow and cubs, which is the situation to actually take seriously. If a black bear does become aggressive, the guidance is to fight back rather than play dead.

Around the house, take the food away and they generally move on. A bear that keeps coming back is a call to Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife, and the earlier that call happens the better the outcome usually is.

Cougars

They are here. Almost nobody sees one. They are solitary, extremely cautious, and mostly active at dawn and dusk following deer, which means they follow deer into places deer go, which includes fields and the edges of properties.

Sensible practice: do not hike alone at dusk if you can avoid it, keep children close and dogs leashed on trails, and do not encourage deer to congregate near your house.

If you encounter one: do not run and do not turn your back. Running triggers a chase response. Make yourself look large, hold your ground or back away slowly, speak firmly, and pick small children up without bending down if you can manage it. If one approaches, be loud and throw things. If attacked, fight — they are dissuadable and people have driven them off.

Report sightings near homes to ODFW or the state police non-emergency line.

Coyotes

Common, adaptable, and the animal most likely to actually take something of yours — a chicken, a cat, a small dog. They are not a danger to adults and they should not be treated as one, but they will absolutely take advantage of an unsecured coop.

Keep small pets in at night. Do not leave pet food outside. Secure poultry properly, with a covered run and a buried or skirted wire edge — the chicken piece covers what actually holds. If one is hanging around, be unwelcoming: noise, hazing, no easy food. A coyote that is comfortable around houses is a problem in the making.

Elk

The one people underestimate. Roosevelt elk are large animals and they are around here in real numbers, in fields, on the edges of timber and on the roads. They are not predators and they are not usually aggressive, but a bull in the rut or a cow with a calf will absolutely act on that, and there is a lot of animal behind the decision.

Give them a wide berth. Do not approach for a photograph. Do not let dogs run at them, which is how people get hurt. And the road hazard is not small — hitting an elk is a different event from hitting a deer.

They will also eat a garden to the ground and walk through fencing that was not built for them. That is a fencing problem, covered in fencing out browsers.

The general rule

Be boring. A property with no available food, no easy access to livestock and nothing to investigate is a property animals walk past. Everybody who has a serious wildlife problem out here has, somewhere in the story, something edible that was easy to get at.

Bins, latches, hardware cloth, motion lights and electric fence supplies are all things we keep, and the hunt wall has the field gear if that is what you are up to.
