Fencing Out Deer, and What Elk Do to That Plan

A fence that stops deer is a specific thing, and a fence that stops elk is a considerably more serious one.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

You can have a garden here or you can have deer in it, and the fence is the whole conversation. Everything else — the sprays, the soap, the dog, the hanging pie tins — buys you weeks at most, because animals here are habituated to people and they are hungry through a long winter.

Deer first

Black-tailed deer are not enormous jumpers by the standards of their family, but they are entirely capable of clearing a normal fence and they will go under, through and around anything half-hearted.

  • Height is the main variable. A serious deer fence is tall — around eight feet is the usual recommendation, and shorter fences work only in specific conditions.
  • They will not jump what they cannot see the landing of. A solid fence works at a lower height than an open one, because deer avoid jumping blind.
  • They will not jump wide and high together. Two shorter fences a few feet apart is an old and effective trick where you have the room, and it can be done with lower material.
  • Angle it outward and a fence gets much harder to clear for the same vertical height.
  • Close the bottom. A great deal of deer entry is under a fence rather than over it. Pin the base down.
  • The gate is where they get in. Every time. Whatever the fence is, the gate has to match it and it has to be shut.

Polypropylene deer netting on posts is cheap, nearly invisible and works well where pressure is moderate. Woven wire is stronger and lasts longer. Electric works if it is maintained, and around here maintenance means keeping vegetation off the wire in a climate where everything grows onto everything.

Then elk arrive

Roosevelt elk are a different scale of problem entirely. They are much larger, they travel in groups, and a fence that is merely tall is something they can go through rather than over. Netting is not going to hold them. A light wire fence gets flattened and then it is a hazard to the animal as well as useless to you.

If elk use your ground, you need either serious woven wire on substantial posts, or a properly built and properly maintained electric fence with enough strands and enough power, or you need to accept that the garden gets protected in a smaller enclosure rather than around the whole property.

Talk to neighbours who have elk pressure. The answers are local, they depend on the route the animals use, and somebody a mile away has already spent the money finding out what works.

The cheaper approach that most people end up at

Do not fence the property. Fence the garden.

A small, tall, well-built enclosure around the beds costs a fraction of a perimeter fence and it is the thing that actually gets finished. It also lets you make it good — proper posts, a real gate, wire buried or skirted at the bottom, and a top that keeps birds off the berries.

Fruit trees can be caged individually. New plantings can be sleeved. That is a far better use of money than a long fence with a weak gate.

Building it so it lasts here

  1. Set posts properly and deep. Saturated ground in winter means a shallow post leans and then fails.
  2. Use pressure treated in ground contact, or cedar, and expect anything else to rot.
  3. Galvanised or stainless fasteners. Plain steel bleeds rust down a post inside a season in this air.
  4. Keep the fence line clear. Blackberry pulls a fence over in a couple of seasons — the bramble piece.
  5. Brace the corners. A fence fails at its corners and at its gate, always.
  6. Walk it after every windstorm. A branch across a wire is a doorway.

What does not work

Repellent sprays fade in rain and here it rains constantly, so they need reapplying more often than anybody actually does. Motion sprinklers work for a while and then stop working. Human hair, soap and dog fur are folklore. Dogs work while they are outside and awake, which is not the hours that matter.

The fence is the answer. Everything else is a delay.

Posts, wire, netting, staples, gate hardware and galvanised fasteners are in the yard around back, and if you want to talk through what is walking through your place, ask at the counter — somebody in the line will have the same trail through their property. We are here.
