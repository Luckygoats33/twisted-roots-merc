Around Siletz

The Drive to Town, and What a Trip Actually Costs

Newport, Toledo, Lincoln City and the valley, with honest drive times and the arithmetic nobody does before deciding to go.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Living out here means the phrase going to town is doing a lot of work. There are several towns, they are good at different things, and the difference between a well-planned run and a badly planned one is most of a day.

The map in your head

WhereRoughly how longWhat it is for
ToledoAbout twenty minutesFull hardware, paint matching, closest proper errand town
NewportAbout twenty-five to thirty minutesGroceries, pharmacy, medical, the bay, the big-box run
Lincoln CityLonger, north up the coastDifferent set of stores, the other direction entirely
Corvallis or the valleyOver the hill, well over an hourSpecialists, larger medical, anything nobody on the coast stocks

Those are fair-weather, daylight, nothing-in-the-road numbers. Add time for rain, add more for fog, add an unknown amount for a tree down or a slide. The Coast Range routes in particular are not reliable in winter — see driving the range.

The arithmetic people skip

A round trip to Newport is roughly an hour of driving and the better part of an hour of parking, walking and standing in a line. Fuel on top. So the true cost of going to town for one item is a couple of hours and something like ten or twelve dollars before you buy anything.

That is fine when you need a shop. It is absurd when you need a fuse, a bag of dog food, a gallon of milk or a can of propane, which is more or less the reason this store exists.

Making one trip do the work of four

  1. Keep a running list somewhere everybody in the house can add to. A pad on the fridge beats an app because everybody actually uses it.
  2. Pick a day and stick to it. A fixed town day means the list has time to accumulate and you stop making emergency runs.
  3. Order errands geographically, not by importance. Work along the route and end at the cold stuff.
  4. Cold last, always. Frozen goods first thing means an hour of them thawing while you go to the hardware store.
  5. Keep a cooler in the truck. Not for fish, for groceries. It turns a bad order of errands into a fine one.
  6. Call ahead. Ten seconds on the phone confirming a part is in stock saves the whole trip being wasted.
  7. Fuel on the way home, not on the way out, so you are not making decisions about range with a full truck and a queue.

Prescriptions and appointments

These are the errands worth building the day around, because they cannot be substituted. Ask about ninety-day fills instead of thirty-day if that is available to you — it turns twelve trips into four. Ask whether they will mail. Stack appointments so one drive covers two.

And keep a buffer. Running a prescription down to the last two days when you are half an hour from the pharmacy and the weather can close the road is how a minor inconvenience becomes a real problem.

What we can save you

Not everything, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Real lumber orders go to the lumberyard in town. Deep hardware selection and paint matching are Toledo. Prescriptions and a full grocery shop are Newport.

What we can do is the run-out-of list — the reason people used to make the drive four times a month instead of once. Milk, eggs, bread, coffee, dog food, batteries, propane, a fuse, a flapper for the toilet, a tarp, bait, ammunition, a bag of concrete. If it is the sort of thing that makes you say the words I have to drive to Newport for one thing, it is the sort of thing we are trying to have.

Stock counts on this website come off the register, so you can check before you drive. That is the whole point of them. Hours and directions are here, the phone gets answered, and if we do not have it we will tell you who does rather than sell you the wrong thing.

One more thing

Whatever the trip is, do not leave with less than half a tank in the winter. Pumps need power and the power goes out, and a closed road can turn a thirty-minute drive into a two-hour detour with no warning. The rest of that thinking is in what to keep in the truck.
