Around Siletz

The First Big Blow of the Season Catches Everybody

It is not the worst storm of the winter that causes the most trouble — it is the first one, every time, for entirely predictable reasons.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

By the middle of winter everyone here is competent. Batteries are in the drawer, the generator has been run, the firewood is under cover, the fuel tank does not go below half. Nobody is impressed by a gale in January.

It is the first one that does the damage, and it does it in the same ways every season.

Why the first one is different

The ground is dry-ish and the trees are in full leaf. Alder and maple still carry a canopy, so they present enormous sail area to a wind that the same tree would shrug off once bare. Meanwhile the soil has not yet gone to the full saturated state, but the first big system usually arrives with hours of rain in front of it — so by the time the wind peaks, the ground has just gone soft and the trees are still fully leafed. That is the worst possible combination and it is exactly what an early-season storm delivers.

The second reason is us. Nobody has needed a flashlight since spring. The generator has not been started. The chainsaw has old fuel in it and a chain that needs sharpening. The tarp is somewhere. Half the batteries in the house are dead and nobody knows which half.

What actually fails

  • Generators that have not been run. Stale fuel gums a carburettor and it will not start when you need it. Run it for twenty minutes a few times a year and use fuel stabiliser.
  • Chainsaws, for the same reason, plus a dull chain that turns a ten-minute job into an hour.
  • Flashlights and headlamps with batteries that died quietly in a drawer.
  • Gutters full of summer needles, so the first heavy rain goes over the edge and down a wall instead of down a pipe.
  • Drains and culverts that silted up over the dry months and now back a driveway up.
  • Fuel. Half a tank on the day of the storm, and every pump between here and Newport dark.
  • Firewood that got split late and is not dry, which is a whole separate misery — see seasoning.

The autumn walk-around

Half a day, once, before the weather turns. It is boring and it works.

  1. Clear the gutters and check the downspouts run somewhere sensible.
  2. Clear the culvert and the ditch at the end of the drive.
  3. Start the generator and let it run under a bit of load. Fresh fuel and stabiliser in the cans.
  4. Fresh chain, fresh fuel and bar oil for the saw. Sharpen it now, not by headlamp in the rain.
  5. Test every light in the house and put a fresh set of batteries somewhere you will find in the dark.
  6. Look up. Anything dead, leaning or hanging over the house or the drive is cheaper to deal with now.
  7. Get the loose yard items put away for the season rather than each time.
  8. Firewood under cover with air moving through it.
  9. Water containers filled if you are on a well.
  10. Some cash in the house.

The road part

The first storm is also the first time all season there are trees down across the roads, and the first time the drains fail on the corners. If you have to drive during or after it, keep to the middle of the lane where you can, watch for standing water on bends, and give it a day before deciding a route is clear. There is more on that in the river road and landslides.

What we do about it

We watch the same forecasts everybody else does and we stock the shelf by the door before the front arrives rather than after, because after is too late to be useful. Batteries, lamps, tarps, cord, fuel cans, water jugs and the rest are on the storm page, and the stock counts there come off the register, so it is what is actually in the building.

We stay open as long as we have power, and if the power is out we generally still have coffee, because there is a propane burner behind the counter and that is a hill Carrie is prepared to die on.
