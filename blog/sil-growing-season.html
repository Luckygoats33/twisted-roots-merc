The Growing Season Here Is Long and Cool

Frost is not the limit out here — heat is, and light is — which changes the whole shape of a gardening year.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Gardeners arriving from a continental climate arrive with the wrong mental model. There, the year is a short warm window between two frosts and everything is a race. Here, the frost window is long and the warmth is the scarce resource. Once that flips in your head, everything else makes sense.

What the year looks like

Part of the yearWhat is happeningWhat to do
Late winterWet, mild, soil cold and saturatedPlan, prune, start seeds indoors, stay off wet soil
Early springWarming slowly, still very wetPeas, broad beans, early greens under cover
Mid springSoil finally workablePotatoes, brassicas, roots, main sowings
Early summerCool, often foggy, long daysEverything is growing; plant out warm crops under cover
Late summerThe genuine dry stretch; the warmest weeksWater seriously, harvest, sow autumn and winter crops
AutumnRain returns, growth slows but does not stopPlant garlic and overwintering greens, mulch, tidy
Early winterMild and wet, low lightHarvest hardy greens and roots, cover bare soil

The two things that actually limit you

Soil temperature in spring. Air temperature looks fine long before the soil is ready, and seed sown into cold wet ground rots rather than germinates. That is the single most common early-season failure here. Wait, or warm the ground with black plastic or cloches, or start under cover and transplant. A soil thermometer costs very little and settles the argument.

Accumulated heat in summer. Warm-season crops need not just warm days but warm nights, and the marine influence takes those away. That is why tomatoes flower and never finish, and why a tunnel changes what is possible more than any other single intervention. See what will and will not grow.

Working with a long shoulder season

The compensation for the cool summer is that autumn keeps going. Growth slows in the low light but it does not stop, and hard freezes that would end a garden elsewhere often do not arrive.

So the late-summer sowing is the most underused opportunity in a coastal garden. Kale, chard, leeks, winter cabbage, overwintering brassicas, hardy salads, spring cabbage, garlic in the autumn, and broad beans over the winter. Done properly, a garden here feeds a household through months when other places are looking at frozen ground.

The limit on winter growing is light rather than cold. Things planted too late go into the dark half of the year too small and sit there. Get them established while the days are still long and they will hold and then move again as light returns.

Rain shapes the calendar as much as temperature

The wet season is not a gardening season for soil work. Walking on and digging saturated ground destroys structure and you will feel it for years. Stay off it, work from paths or boards, and cover bare soil over winter with mulch or a cover crop so the rain does not leach it and beat it down.

Then in late summer, remember that this is a place with a real dry spell. Newly planted things and anything in a container will need watering, and wells and rain barrels get low. People are consistently surprised by this in a climate with the reputation this one has.

Frost, since somebody will ask

Frost happens here, especially inland up the valley where cold air pools on clear nights and where it is a different climate from the beach — see coast versus valley. But it is generally light, generally brief, and rarely the thing that ends the season.

Your own place matters more than any published date. A low spot collects cold air and will frost when a slope above it does not. Watch your ground for a couple of seasons and trust that over anything printed.

The practical summary

Start warm crops indoors and be patient about planting out. Sow the cool crops generously because they are what thrives. Sow again in late summer for winter. Protect from slugs from the first day, because seedlings in this climate get eaten before they get cold — the slug piece.
