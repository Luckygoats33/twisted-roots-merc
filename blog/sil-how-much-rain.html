Around Siletz

How Much Rain Actually Falls Out Here

The number is large but the distribution is the interesting part, and it is not what people from wetter-sounding places expect.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

The honest answer to how much rain falls here is that it depends where you are standing, and the difference over a few miles is bigger than anybody expects. A spot on the western slope of the Coast Range and a spot down near the beach can be a long way apart in annual total, and both are within a short drive of this counter.

The rough shape of it

The Oregon coast and the seaward slopes behind it are among the wetter places in the lower states. Annual totals in this part of Lincoln County are generally reckoned in the range of the high sixties to the nineties of inches, with higher ground on the west face of the range running higher still and sheltered valley bottoms running lower. We are not going to put a precise number on Siletz specifically, because the nearest long-running gauge is not in your yard and the terrain here does not average nicely.

For comparison, the Willamette Valley gets roughly half of that. Same storms, one mountain range of difference. That mechanism is explained in why it rains the way it does here.

The distribution is the real story

The total is not what defines the climate. The concentration is.

Part of the yearRoughly what happens
Late autumn and winterThe bulk of the year's rain, in long frontal systems, sometimes for days at a stretch
Early springStill wet, showers and systems, ground fully saturated
Late springTapering, with dry stretches appearing between systems
SummerGenuinely dry for weeks at a time; fog and drizzle rather than rain
Early autumnThe turn, usually with one storm that ends the dry season decisively

So a place with an enormous annual total also has a summer where gardens need watering, wells run low, creeks drop to a trickle and the fire danger is real. That contradiction confuses everybody in their first year.

Counting days rather than inches

The more useful local measure is not depth, it is how many days it rains at all. A great many, in the wet half of the year — more than in cities with a reputation for rain, even though the depth per day is modest. That is what people are reacting to when they say it is relentless. It is not the volume. It is the number of consecutive grey days.

And most of it is light. Serious downpours happen but the standard product is steady, fine, wind-driven water that will soak you completely in twenty minutes and never once look dramatic.

What the number means practically

  • Drainage is a permanent job. Gutters, downspouts, ditches, culverts and the grade around a foundation. Water always wins eventually and the only question is where it goes.
  • Nothing air-dries outdoors for months. Firewood needs cover and a year of lead time.
  • Wood outdoors needs to be the right wood, fastened with the right metal. Galvanised or stainless, exterior grade, or it will not last.
  • The ground stays saturated, which is why fields rut, driveways soften and hillsides move. See landslides.
  • Roofs grow moss, decks grow algae and closets grow mildew. Not neglect, just climate — mud, moss and mildew.

Measuring it yourself

A cheap rain gauge on a post away from the house is worth having, and after a couple of seasons you will know your own place better than any forecast does. People who keep a gauge end up with strong opinions about how much wetter their end of the road is than the other end, and they are usually right.

The other reason to have one: it tells you when the river is going to come up, which tells you when it will be fishable a couple of days later. That is the actual reason half the gauges around here exist.

Rain gear, boots and a gauge live in the outdoors aisle, and the wet season list is over on storm.
