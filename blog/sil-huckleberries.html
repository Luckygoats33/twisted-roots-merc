Huckleberries, and Why Nobody Tells You Where Their Patch Is

Small, slow to pick, better than anything you can buy, and surrounded by the most guarded secrets in any small town.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Ask somebody around here where the huckleberries are and watch their face. You will get a vague gesture at the hills and a change of subject. This is not unfriendliness. It is a tradition, and patches get handed down in families along with the vagueness.

What they are

Huckleberries are the wild relatives of blueberries — same family, smaller fruit, far more flavour. Several species grow in this part of Oregon and they behave differently.

The red huckleberry is the one you meet first around here without looking for it. Bright translucent red berries on a shrub with thin green twigs and small oval leaves, very often growing straight out of an old stump or a rotting log, which is a distinctive habit and a good identification clue. Tart, and excellent in anything cooked.

The evergreen huckleberry has small, tough, glossy dark leaves and black berries that ripen late, often carrying into the cold months. The new growth comes in bronze-red. It is a handsome plant and it is also sold as landscaping, which is a good hint about how well it does here without help.

Blue and black huckleberries of the mountain sort favour higher, more open ground and old burns.

Where to look

Red huckleberry likes shade, rotting wood and the disturbed edges — old logging ground, stump fields, the margins of a road cut, second growth. Evergreen huckleberry likes the coastal forest and does well in a fair amount of shade.

The general principle is edges. Full dark old growth is thin, and open sun-baked ground is thin. Where light gets in but the ground is still forest, that is where you look.

The picking is slow, and that is the point

These are small berries on small stems and there is no way to make it fast. An hour of good picking gets you a modest tub. This is why nobody sells them cheap and why finding a productive patch matters so much.

  • Take a container you can hang, so both hands work.
  • A tarp or sheet under a laden branch, then a gentle shake, gets the ripe ones and leaves the green. Berry rakes exist and they damage plants, so most people around here look down on them.
  • Pick the ripe ones only. Colour is the guide, and taste one before committing to a bush — flavour varies a lot.
  • Leave plenty. Bears, birds and everything else eat these and the patch has to produce again next year.

Land and permits

Know whose ground you are on before you pick. Public forest land generally allows personal harvest and may require a permit depending on the agency and the quantity; timber company land has its own access rules that change; private is private, and tribal land is not open ground. Ask the managing agency rather than assuming, which is the same discipline as everything else out here.

Do not park blocking a gate or a turnout, especially on active haul roads — see sharing a road with a log truck.

Safety, briefly

Bears eat huckleberries and you are in their groceries. Make noise, look around regularly, do not get between a sow and a cub, and do not put your head into a thick patch without announcing yourself. The full version is in the neighbours piece.

Also: brush, ticks, downed timber and getting turned around. Compass, orange during hunting seasons, and tell somebody where you went. Coverage up in the hills is negligible — dead zones.

Keeping them

Freeze on a tray in a single layer, then bag. They keep beautifully and they hold their flavour better than most fruit. A cup of huckleberries in something in the middle of winter is worth the hour it took to pick them.

Pancakes, muffins, a pie, or cooked down into syrup for the whole year. The recipes have a couple of ideas, and if you bring a tub in and ask nicely, Carrie has opinions.

The etiquette

If somebody does show you a patch, that is a real thing. Do not take anybody else there, do not post it, and do not strip it. That is the entire social contract and it is the reason the vagueness exists in the first place.

Buckets, gloves and boots are on the outdoors shelf.
