Mud, Moss and Mildew Are Not Failures, They Are the Climate

Everything here is trying to grow on everything else — what to fight, what to accept, and what quietly does real damage.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

New arrivals spend their first year scrubbing. Then they work out which of it matters and which of it is just what a wet forest does to a building, and they relax considerably.

The triage

Three categories. Sort everything into one of them and you will stop wasting weekends.

  • Cosmetic. Green film on a north wall, moss in the lawn, algae on siding. Ugly, harmless, comes back. Deal with it when you feel like it.
  • Slowly damaging. Moss on a roof, moss in gutters, mildew inside a closet, algae on a deck. Left alone for years these cost real money.
  • Actually dangerous. A slick deck, a slick ramp, a slick set of steps. People break bones on these every winter and it is the most underrated hazard on any property out here.

Moss on the roof, which is the expensive one

Moss holds water against the roof and lifts the edges of shingles, and once water is getting under a shingle you are on a timer. On a wood or composition roof this is the maintenance item that determines how long the roof lasts.

What works: keep it clear of debris, particularly the needles that collect in valleys and behind chimneys, because moss establishes in the organic muck rather than on clean roofing. Zinc or copper strips near the ridge release enough metal in runoff to suppress growth downslope. Moss killer applied in the wet season, then let the rain wash the dead material off over the following months.

What does not work: a pressure washer. It strips the granules off composition shingles and drives water up under everything, and it takes years off the roof to fix a cosmetic problem. Scraping hard does much the same.

And do not go up there in the wet season unless you actually know what you are doing. A mossy pitched roof in the rain is one of the slickest surfaces available.

Gutters

Twice a year is the honest minimum here, and if you are under conifers it is more. Autumn needle drop is heavier than leaf drop and it packs down into a mat that holds water. A gutter that overflows sends water down a wall and into the ground right at the foundation, which is how the expensive problems start.

Check the downspouts actually discharge somewhere useful and not into a puddle against the house.

Decks, steps and ramps

Anything horizontal, in shade, gets a film of algae that turns lethal when wet. The fix is regular cleaning with a deck cleaner, decent drainage between boards, and grit tape or grit-added finish on steps and ramps. Do the steps even if you do nothing else. A fall on wet wooden steps in the dark is the single most common serious injury around here that nobody talks about.

Mildew inside

Indoor mould is a humidity problem wearing a costume. It shows up on north walls, in closets against exterior walls, behind furniture pushed tight, and in bathrooms with weak fans.

  1. Ventilate. A bathroom fan that actually vents outside, run for a decent stretch after showers, is the highest-value fix in the house.
  2. Move furniture a couple of inches off exterior walls so air can pass.
  3. Run a dehumidifier in the wet months. In a crawlspace or a basement it pays for itself.
  4. Leave closet doors ajar, and use moisture absorbers in the ones that stay shut.
  5. Check the crawlspace has a decent vapour barrier over the dirt. A lot of the damp in an older house out here is coming up from underneath.

Clean visible mildew with a proper cleaner rather than just wiping. And do not mix bleach with anything containing ammonia, which is a genuinely dangerous combination and easier to do by accident than people think.

Mud

Mud is not a problem to be solved, it is a season to be managed. Gravel in the traffic areas, a boot tray inside every door, rubber boots by the door and a stiff brush outside it. Do not drive on saturated ground unless you like ruts that will be there in July, and keep a shovel and a tow strap in the truck — the truck kit covers the rest.

Moss killer, deck cleaner, grit tape, boot trays and rubber boots are all things we keep on the shelf, and the Fix It wall is by the front door. If we do not have it, we will tell you who does.
