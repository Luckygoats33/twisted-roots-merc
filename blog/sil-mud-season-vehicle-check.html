Around Siletz

A Mud-Season Vehicle Check

Half an hour in the autumn on the things that only matter when it is dark, wet and you are a long way out.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Vehicles out here have a harder life than they would in town: gravel, mud, salt air, steep grades, long wet months and a lot of miles on roads with no shoulder. None of that requires anything exotic. It requires the basic checks actually getting done before the weather turns rather than after something happens.

Tyres, which is most of it

Tread depth is the difference between stopping and not on a wet road. The old trick with a penny is crude but it works — if you can see the top of the head, the tread is down where wet grip falls off badly. Better is a proper depth gauge, and better still is looking at the wear bars moulded into the grooves.

Check pressures cold, including the spare, and check them again when the temperature drops because pressure falls with it. Look at the wear pattern: wear on one edge means alignment, wear in the middle or at both edges means pressure, and cupping means suspension. Any of those is worth fixing before winter rather than replacing tyres early because of it.

All-season tyres are fine here for most people because we do not get much lasting low-elevation snow. If you cross the range regularly in winter, carry chains and know how to fit them before you need to fit them in the dark on a grade.

Everything you see with

  • Wiper blades. Replace them every autumn as a matter of course. They are cheap and they are the thing standing between you and a smeared windscreen with oncoming headlights in it.
  • Washer fluid. Full, with a decent fluid, because road film here is constant and a dry wipe just smears it.
  • Every bulb. Headlights, tail lights, brake lights, indicators, reverse. Walk around with somebody in the driver's seat, or reverse up to a wall and watch the reflections.
  • Cloudy headlight lenses. Restore or replace. It makes a bigger difference on unlit roads than anything else you can do for the money.
  • Defrost and heater. Including whether the air conditioning still works, because it is what actually clears fogged glass quickly.

Under the hood and underneath

  1. Battery. Cold weather finds weak batteries. Clean the terminals and get it load tested if it is more than a few years old — a test is usually free and a no-start in the rain is not.
  2. Coolant level and condition, and check the hoses for softness or bulges.
  3. Belts for cracking and glazing.
  4. Oil, and whether the service is actually due.
  5. Brakes. Listen for noise, feel for pulsing, and look at pad thickness if you can see it. Wet grit is hard on brakes.
  6. Suspension and steering play, particularly if you run washboard gravel.
  7. Undercarriage. Salt air plus road grit rusts things quietly. Look at brake and fuel lines on an older vehicle.
  8. Wash the underside occasionally. It sounds fussy and it adds years.

The body and the interior

Check the door and window seals, and clear the drain holes at the bottom of doors and the sunroof channels if you have one. Blocked drains are how water ends up inside a vehicle and how the carpet starts smelling, which in this climate never fully goes away.

Get the carpets and mats out and dry once before winter. Wet carpet plus a cold cab equals fogged glass on every trip, and no amount of defroster fixes the source.

The recovery items

Jack and lug wrench present and matching the vehicle, spare with air in it, tow strap, shovel, gloves and a light. That is the difference between a delay and a night. The full version is in what to keep in the truck.

The gravel-road extras

If you drive forest roads, check the air filter more often than the manual says because dust loads it fast, watch for stones wedged in brakes and shields, and look at the tyre sidewalls for cuts — sidewall damage from sharp rock is the most common flat out here and it is not repairable.

Do it in the autumn, before the first real storm, alongside clearing the gutters and running the generator. There is a full seasonal list in the first blow piece, and the winter supplies are laid out on the storm page.
