Around Siletz

What a General Store Is Actually For

In a town this size the store is not really about the shelves, and it took us a while to be comfortable saying so out loud.

4 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

People sometimes ask what the business plan is. The honest answer is that we got tired of driving to Newport for one thing, and we suspected everybody else was too. That is the whole of it. There is no second act.

But a general store in a small town does a few things that are not obvious from the outside, and after a while you notice you are doing them.

It stops the fourth trip

The arithmetic is simple and unforgiving. A forty-minute round trip and about eleven dollars of fuel to buy a seven dollar part. Do that four times a month, for a whole town, for years, and it adds up to something ridiculous — time and money leaving Siletz over batteries and bread.

So the shelf is chosen around that. Not the weekly shop, which belongs in town. The things that make somebody say the words I have to drive to Newport for one thing. Milk, eggs, coffee, dog food, propane, a fuse, a flapper for a toilet, a tarp, batteries, bait, a bag of concrete. One good version of each thing instead of six mediocre ones.

It is where information lives

This is the part nobody plans for and it turns out to be half the job.

Which road is closed. Whether the river is fishable. Who has a tractor and would probably help. Whether the power is out on your road or just at your house. Whether that is the right part for your sink. Who to call about a leaking roof, and who not to. Which stretch of the coast had the slide.

None of that is on a website and none of it is stocked. It accumulates because people come through, and it gets handed out over the counter free because that is what it is for. On a bad weather day the store becomes a notice board with coffee, and that is not a nuisance, that is the point.

It is a place to stand around in

Out here you can go a long while without being in a room with people you did not plan to see. Rural life is good in a lot of ways and thin in that particular one, and it is thinner for the people who are older, or on their own, or new and do not know anybody yet.

So there is coffee going all day and nobody is going to hurry you out of the way. Some mornings the most useful thing this building does is give four people a reason to be in the same place at the same time. Bakery rack is thin by nine and gone by eleven, and the conversation lasts considerably longer than the pastry.

It is a small piece of resilience

When the wind takes the power out and it stays out — which it does, because of how the lines run — the store is a building in town with supplies in it, a propane burner behind the counter and somebody who knows what is happening. We stay open as long as we have power and generally a while after.

That is not heroism. It is just what a shop in a small place is, and it is why the batteries and the lamps and the jugs are stocked before the front arrives rather than after. The storm shelf exists for one weekend a year and earns its keep that weekend.

The shelf gets written by the neighbours

We do not special-order everything for everybody, because that is an errand service rather than a store. What we do is write down what people ask for, and when the same thing comes up enough times it goes on the shelf permanently — for everybody, not just the person who asked.

That is slower. It is also the difference between a store and a delivery app, and it means the inventory is a reasonably honest record of what this town actually needs.

What we are not

Not a lumberyard, and there is a proper one in town. Not a hardware store with a paint counter, which is Toledo. Not a pharmacy or a supermarket, which is Newport. Not a gun shop, because that takes a federal licence we do not hold. We would rather point you right than sell you wrong, and knowing where a store stops is the useful part.

The name, since it comes up

Look at anything that has been growing on this coast for a hundred years. Nothing out here grows straight — the wind will not allow it. What holds a tree up is not the trunk, it is a root system that is knotted and stubborn and goes further sideways than down.

That seemed about right for a small town, and about right for a store that intends to still be here in twenty years. We are on N Gaither Street, the ovens go on at six, and there is a biscuit jar at the register for the dogs. Come see us.
