Cash, Because the Card Readers Do Not Work Either

An outage is a cash economy for a day or two, and the ATM in Newport is also a device that needs electricity.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

People plan for the lights and forget that the entire payment system is electric. Card readers need power and a network. ATMs need both. The fuel pumps need power to move gas, and the register at every store between here and the highway needs at minimum a working phone line or a hotspot.

For a day or two after a big blow, the coast runs on cash. It is not dramatic, it is just how it goes, and the people who have some are fine and the people who do not are driving around looking for a machine that works.

How much, and in what

AmountCovers
$100 in the houseThe minimum. A tank of fuel and a few days of groceries
$300-500 in the houseA week for a family, including a motel night if you have to leave
$50-100 in the gloveboxFuel and food if you are away from the house when it happens

Small bills. This is the part that matters more than the total. Fives, tens and twenties, and a roll of quarters. When the power is out nobody has a working till, the change drawer runs down within hours, and a hundred dollar bill for a nine dollar purchase is a problem for both of you. A stack of fives is worth more than the same money in fifties.

Where to keep it

Not all in one place, and not somewhere obvious. Split it: some in the household kit, some in the go bag, some in the vehicle, some in a wallet. Somewhere fireproof and waterproof if you have that, or at minimum in a sealed bag so a wet kit does not ruin it.

Do not keep it in the bank envelope it came in and do not keep it with your passport and the deed in one folder that walks out the door if somebody gets in.

Get it before, not during

The window is when the watch goes up, not when the warning does. Once the wind arrives the ATMs in Newport have queues, and once the power goes they are ornaments.

The easy version is to draw a bit extra when you are already at the bank and put it away, rather than making a special trip. Then check it twice a year when you check the rest of the kit and put back whatever you borrowed from it, which everybody does and which is fine as long as you replace it.

What cash actually buys in an outage

  • Fuel, at whichever station has a generator, which is usually one and usually cash only
  • Groceries and supplies from stores running on backup power with a paper till
  • A motel room inland if the house gets too cold or too wet to stay in
  • A meal somewhere warm, which on day three is worth more than it sounds
  • Somebody with a chainsaw and a truck who can clear your drive today rather than Thursday
  • Ice, when it is available, which is the thing that saves a freezer full of elk

The rest of the money question

Two related things worth doing in the calm:

  1. Keep paper copies of the account numbers, the insurance policy numbers and the agent's phone number, on the same page as the phone list. If your phone is dead and the internet is out, you cannot look any of it up.
  2. Know that a long outage delays things. Direct deposits, bill payments and card holds do not care about the weather, but your ability to check them does. Nobody has ever been ruined by an outage, but plenty of people have had an overdraft they did not see coming.

What we do here

When the power goes out, the merc runs on generator and the register goes to a paper pad and a calculator. Cash is easiest. We will take a card if the hotspot is holding, and we will write down what you owe if you are a neighbor and short, because that is what a small store in a small town does and it has worked for a very long time.

What we cannot do is give change out of thin air, so the small bills genuinely help. More on what the store can and cannot manage in what a store can do in an outage, and the shelf status before you drive over is on the storm page.
