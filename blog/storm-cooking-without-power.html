Cooking Without Power, and Eating Better Than You Expect

A woodstove, a camp stove and a kettle cover almost everything, and the rest is deciding what to eat in which order.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

People plan their outage food around cold cans and then eat cold cans for four days and feel awful. A hot meal on the second night does more for a household's mood than anything else you can buy, and it is not difficult.

The line between what can come inside and what cannot is not about how careful you are, it is about how much carbon monoxide the burner produces and whether it is designed for enclosed use. That distinction is worth understanding properly before you commit, and it is spelled out in when a camp stove can come inside.

The order to eat things

  1. Fridge perishables, day one. Milk, eggs, leftovers, cut fruit, anything opened. This is the food on the clock.
  2. Freezer, day two and three. Cook the biggest thing first while it is coldest. A pot of something from the freezer feeds four people twice.
  3. Pantry, day four onward. Canned, dry, shelf-stable. This is where you are living if it goes long.
  4. The emergency bars, last. They are the floor, not the plan.

Cook once, eat twice

Fuel is finite and so is your patience for doing dishes with heated water. Cook big, single-pot things: soup, chili, a pot of rice and beans, oatmeal for four. One pot means one pot to wash, and washing is the part everybody underestimates when the tap is a jug.

Boil water in a full kettle every time you have the stove going, even if you only need a cup. Pour the rest into a thermos. Hot water on demand for tea, oatmeal, washing and a hot water bottle at bedtime is worth more than the fuel it costs.

A stocked shelf that does not need a stove

Even with a stove, half of what you eat should need nothing. Aim for real food, not novelty food:

  • Peanut butter, crackers, tortillas, hard cheese that keeps a few days out
  • Canned tuna, chili, beans, soup — with a manual can opener that you have tested, not one still in the packaging
  • Dried fruit, nuts, jerky, granola
  • Shelf-stable milk boxes, which make coffee and cereal possible
  • Instant coffee, or ground coffee and a way to pour hot water through it
  • Something sweet. On night three this is not frivolous

Buy the food your household already eats. An outage is not the moment to discover that nobody likes the emergency lentils.

Doing dishes on a jug

Two basins: one with a little hot water and dish soap, one with cool water and a capful of bleach as a rinse. Scrape first, wash least-greasy to most-greasy, and reuse the wash water for the whole meal. A roll of paper towels and some paper plates for the worst of it is a completely legitimate way to save water, and this is the one week it is fine.

Camp stove fuel, 1 lb propane cylinders, tank exchange, manual can openers and the shelf-stable groceries are all stocked — check the storm wall before you drive over. The kitchen at the merc keeps running on the woodstove and the gas range when the power is out, which is a policy, not a coincidence — come see us.
