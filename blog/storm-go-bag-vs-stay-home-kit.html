Storm Ready

Going Bag Versus Stay-Home Kit

They are two different things for two different emergencies, and building one and calling it both is how people end up with neither.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Almost every emergency out here is a shelter-in-place emergency: the power is out, a tree is across the drive, the road is closed, and the answer is to stay put and be comfortable. A small number are the other kind: fire, a slide, a flood coming up, an earthquake, and the answer is to be gone in ten minutes.

The two need different things, and the failure mode is trying to make one bag do both. It ends up too heavy to carry and too small to live on.

The difference in one line

The stay-home kit is heavy, bulky and measured in weeks. The go bag is light, portable and measured in three days.

PropertyStay-home kitGo bag
DurationTwo weeksThree days
WeightDoes not matterEverything. You may be walking
Water14 gallons per person, stored2 liters per person plus a filter
FoodGroceries you rotate and eatBars and dense calories, no cooking
LightLanterns, headlamps, sparesOne headlamp, lithium cells
ShelterYour houseEmergency bivvy, poncho, mylar blanket
SanitationBucket, liners, absorbentWipes, a few bags, sanitizer
DocumentsIn a fire box at homeCopies, in the bag, in a waterproof pouch
Where it livesA closet or a corner, low, near a doorBy the door, or in the truck

What actually goes in a go bag

Aim for something you can carry a couple of miles without hating it. Twenty pounds for an adult, ten for a child, and children carry their own small one because a bag with their own things in it helps them enormously.

  1. Water — two liters and a small filter or purification tablets.
  2. Food — three days of bars, nuts, jerky. Nothing that needs cooking or a can opener.
  3. Light — one headlamp with lithium batteries, plus a spare set.
  4. Warmth and shelter — a poncho, an emergency bivvy, a wool hat, dry wool socks, gloves, a mylar blanket.
  5. First aid — a compact kit, plus three days of every prescription and a copy of the medication list.
  6. Communication — a small crank or battery radio, a charged power bank, the right cable, and the paper phone list.
  7. Documents — copies of ID, insurance, deed or lease, medical and vaccination records, in a sealed pouch. A copy on a thumb drive too.
  8. Cash — a hundred or two in small bills.
  9. Tools — a multi-tool, a whistle, duct tape wrapped around a pencil, paracord, a ferro rod or a lighter.
  10. Personal — glasses or contacts, sanitation wipes, a toothbrush, any comfort item for a child.
  11. Sturdy shoes — either in the bag or beside it. This is the item people leave without.

The five-minute add-ons

Keep a short written list taped inside the bag of what you grab on the way out if there is time. Nobody thinks clearly at that moment and a list means you do not have to.

  • Prescriptions in current use, from the bathroom
  • Phone and its charger
  • Wallet, keys, glasses
  • The pet carrier and the pet kit — see pets in an emergency
  • Laptop or the hard drive with the photographs
  • One irreplaceable thing per person, chosen in advance so nobody is deciding in the hallway

Where each one lives

Go bags by the door you actually use, or in the vehicle. One per person. A go bag in a basement store room is a go bag you will not have.

The stay-home kit goes low, in one place, near an exit, in totes with the contents written on the outside. Water separate and off the concrete. Everything in one place so anybody in the house can find it in the dark.

A third one in each vehicle covers being away from home when something happens, which is covered in the car kit piece.

Then keep them honest

Both drift. Batteries go flat, food expires, children grow out of the clothes in the bag, and somebody borrows the headlamp and does not put it back. Twice a year, tie it to something you already do — see rotating and maintaining a kit.

Bags, bivvies, ponchos, filters, headlamps, first aid kits and the rest are on the storm wall and the Hunt & Field side, which is where the light, packable versions of most of it live.
