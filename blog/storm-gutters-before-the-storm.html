Storm Ready

Clean Your Gutters Before the Wind, Not After

Fir needles are the specific problem here, and a blocked downspout can put water in a wall for weeks before you notice.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Gutters are the most boring item on any storm list and the one with the best return. A roof on this coast sheds a genuinely large volume of water — an inch of rain on a typical house roof is over a thousand gallons — and gutters are the only thing deciding whether that goes down a pipe and away or down the wall and into the crawlspace.

The coastal problem is needles, not leaves

Deciduous leaves are easy. They arrive over a few weeks, they sit on top, and they come out in handfuls. Douglas fir and Sitka spruce shed needles all year, a little at a time, and needles do something different: they mat down into a dense felt in the bottom of the gutter, they wash into the downspout and jam at the elbow, and they do it invisibly. A gutter can look perfectly clear from the ground and be completely plugged eight feet down the pipe.

Then add moss, which grows on north-facing roofs here with real enthusiasm and slides down in sheets in the first hard rain, and the grit that comes off asphalt shingles, and you have a gutter full of what is essentially soil. Ferns growing out of a gutter is not a joke around here, it is a Tuesday.

When to do it

Twice, if you can. Once in early autumn before the wet season really starts, and once mid-winter after the first few big blows have brought down whatever the wind was going to bring down. If you only do it once, do it in autumn.

Pick a dry-ish day, because wet needle mat is heavier and nastier and wet roofs are slick.

Doing it

  1. Set the ladder properly. One foot out for every four feet up, on firm level ground, tied off or footed by somebody. Most gutter injuries are ladder injuries.
  2. Do not lean the ladder on the gutter itself. It will dent and the dent will hold water forever. Use a ladder standoff, or set it against the fascia between hangers.
  3. Move the ladder rather than reaching. Every time.
  4. Scoop the debris into a bucket hooked to the ladder, not onto the ground where you will rake it later and not into the flowerbed.
  5. Flush with a hose from the far end toward the downspout and watch the outlet run.
  6. Check the downspout separately. If it does not run freely, tap down its length listening for the dead thud where the blockage is, then either flush it upward from the bottom or take the elbow apart.
  7. While you are up there, look: rusted-through sections, loose hangers, joints that have opened, and the slope. A gutter should fall about a quarter inch every ten feet toward the outlet. Standing water in a gutter means it has sagged.

Where the water lands

The other half of the job and the half people skip. A downspout that discharges at the foundation is just delivering the whole roof to the base of your wall in a neat concentrated stream.

DischargeVerdict
Splash block at the foundationBarely adequate. Better than nothing
Flexible extension, 6-10 ft out, sloped awayGood, cheap, do this
Buried solid pipe to daylight downhillBest. Solid pipe, not perforated
Into a rain barrel with an overflow that goes awayGood, and it gives you flush water
Into the ground with no outletYou have built an underground pond next to your footing

Gutter guards, honestly

Mesh guards help with leaves and do very little for fir needles, which stand on end and thread through mesh, or lie on top and mat until water sheets over the front. Fine screens clog with shingle grit. Foam inserts fill with needles and grow moss and are worse than nothing.

The best of them turn a twice-yearly job into a once-yearly job. None of them turn it into never. If somebody quotes you a lot of money on a guarantee of never cleaning gutters again on the Oregon coast, they have not been here through a winter.

The five minute version

If a storm is coming tomorrow and you have no time: walk the outside of the house and check that every downspout is running and every discharge point is clear. That alone catches most of the damage. The rest can wait for a dry weekend.

Gutter sealant, hangers, extensions, gloves and buckets are all on the Fix It wall, and if what you actually need is to move water away from the house at ground level, the piece on diversion and water intrusion is the next one to read.
