Landslides on Coastal Slopes: Signs and Sense

Weeks of rain, a steep cut and a bit of fill is the recipe, and slopes give warning signs for days before they go.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Coast range slides are not mysterious. Water gets into the soil, the soil gets heavy, the friction holding it to the slope drops, and gravity does the rest. The reason they cluster at the end of a long wet stretch rather than during the heaviest hour of rain is that it takes weeks for water to work its way down and saturate the layer that matters.

Where they happen

  • Steep cuts. Anywhere a road or a driveway was cut into a hillside, the cut bank above it is the most likely place on the property.
  • Fill slopes. The other half of that cut — the material pushed downhill to make the bench. Fill is looser than native ground and it goes first.
  • Old slide scars. Ground that has moved before moves again. A hummocky, lumpy slope with trees leaning at odd angles is telling you its history.
  • Below recent clearcuts and burns. Root strength holds slopes together, and root systems decay over the several years after harvest.
  • Where water is concentrated. A culvert outfall, a downspout, a ditch that overtops, a road that sheds all its water into one spot. This is the one people create themselves.

The warning signs

Slopes usually creep before they run. Look for these on your own ground, and take them seriously:

SignWhat it means
New cracks in the ground, a driveway or a foundationSomething is pulling apart. The most direct warning there is
Trees, fences or poles tilting, or curved trunksThe ground under them has moved and they have grown to compensate
Doors and windows sticking that never didThe structure is being racked
A bulge or new hummock at the bottom of a slopeMaterial is piling at the toe. The slide has already started
Water appearing where it never did, or a spring going muddyGroundwater is finding new paths through disturbed soil
A spring or a seep suddenly going dryEqually bad. The path has been pinched off by movement
Broken water lines, tilting utility poles, taut or sagging wiresGround movement
A low rumble, cracking, or trees knockingGet out now. This is the sound of it going

What to do about it

Most of what helps is water management, and it is the same work that keeps your crawlspace dry.

  1. Get roof water away from the top of any slope. Downspouts piped to daylight below the slope, not spilling over its edge.
  2. Keep ditches and culverts clear so water follows the path it was designed to. A plugged culvert redirects a whole road's runoff into one point on a hillside.
  3. Do not add water at the top — no irrigation, no septic drainfield, no pool, no pond above an unstable slope.
  4. Do not add weight at the top or remove support at the toe. Cutting into the bottom of a slope for a shed pad or a parking spot removes exactly what was holding it.
  5. Keep vegetation. Roots are the reinforcing steel. Clearing brush off a steep slope for a view is a decision with consequences three winters later.
  6. If you see cracks or movement, get a geotechnical engineer to look before you do anything else. This is not a thing to figure out yourself.

During a long wet stretch

Be more alert after the ground is saturated than during any single storm. Listen at night — most slides that reach houses do it at night, and the sound of trees cracking, a rumble, or boulders knocking is your only warning.

If you are on or below a slope you do not trust, sleep on the uphill side of the house, keep a bag by the door, and if something feels wrong, leave. Nobody has ever regretted spending a night at a friend's house.

On the road

Slides onto roads are the version most of us actually meet. Around a blind corner on a wet night, a pile of mud and rock in the lane with no warning at all. The only defence is speed, which is covered in driving the coast range. If you come on one, do not try to drive over it or around it on the shoulder — the shoulder is the part most likely to have gone.

Shovels, gravel, culvert-clearing tools and rain gear are out in the yard and on the storm wall.
