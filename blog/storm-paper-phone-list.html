The Paper Phone List, and Why You Do Not Know Anyone's Number

Nobody has memorized a phone number since flip phones, which is fine right up until the phone is a brick.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Try it now. Without looking, write down your spouse's number, your mother's, and the number of the person who would come get you if your truck died on 229 at ten at night.

Most people cannot do it. That is not a character flaw, it is what happens when the device remembers for you. It becomes a problem the moment the device is dead, lost, wet, or in the truck you just walked away from.

What goes on it

One page. Printed or handwritten, in a sandwich bag or laminated. One in the kit, one in each vehicle glovebox, one in a wallet, one at work.

The out-of-area contact

This one is genuinely useful and almost nobody does it. Pick one person who lives well away from the coast — a relative in Idaho, a friend in Boise, anywhere outside whatever weather or earthquake would affect us here.

Everybody in the family knows that number and everybody checks in with that person. They become the switchboard. If the family is scattered — one at work in Newport, one at school, one at home — nobody has to reach each other, they each just have to reach one person out of the area, and that person can tell each of them where the others are.

It works because local calls saturate first. Long distance and text messages get through when local calls will not, and one person answering a phone somewhere dry is a lot more reliable than four people trying to find each other.

Write down more than numbers

While you are making the page, add the things you would need and could not look up:

  • Your own address as emergency services would find it, including the mile marker or the fire number on your driveway post
  • Insurance policy numbers, home and vehicle
  • Blood types and serious allergies for everyone in the house
  • The location of the water main shutoff, the gas or propane shutoff, and the electrical panel
  • The bank's number, and your account number, if a card gets swallowed somewhere
  • Where the spare keys are, and who has one

Teach the kids their numbers

A child who knows a parent's phone number by heart is a child who can be reunited with you. Make a game of it, put it to a tune, and check they still have it a few months later. For younger ones, write it on a card that lives in their backpack or a jacket pocket.

The rest of the communication plan

Three things that go with it and cost nothing:

  1. Text, do not call. When a tower is loaded, a short text will keep retrying and get through when a call will not connect at all.
  2. Agree a check-in time rather than checking in constantly. Once in the morning and once in the evening saves everybody's battery and stops the low-grade panic when somebody does not answer immediately.
  3. Agree a meeting place. One near the house for a fire, one outside the immediate area for something bigger. Say it out loud with everyone in the room once a year.

Then put the list somewhere and forget about it, which is the whole point.
