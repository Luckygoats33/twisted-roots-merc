Poly Sheeting and Window Plastic: The Cheap Fix for a Broken Opening

Six mil poly and a staple gun will close a hole in a wall, and the shrink film kit will make a drafty window stop being one.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Poly sheeting is the most useful thing in the storm section that nobody thinks to buy until they need it. It closes broken windows, seals a hole a limb made, splits a house into a heated zone and an unheated one, and keeps rain off everything you had to carry out of a wet room.

Which thickness

ThicknessUse it forLasts
1 milDrop cloth, dust barrierOne job
4 milInterior partitions, covering furniture, vapor barrierWeeks indoors
6 milBoarding a window, exterior temporary cover, ground coverA season outdoors
Shrink film window kitInsulating an existing window you can still see throughAll winter

Six mil is the one to keep. It is stiff enough to take a staple without tearing out and thick enough to survive being flogged by wind for weeks. Four mil is fine inside and will not last outside.

Closing a broken window

  1. Clear the glass. Gloves on, and get the loose shards out of the frame with pliers rather than fingers.
  2. Cut the poly at least six inches oversize all round.
  3. Sandwich it, do not staple it bare. Staple through the poly into the trim, then run a lath strip, a length of 1x2, or even a strip of cardboard over the staple line and fasten through that. Staples alone tear out of plastic in one windy night.
  4. Work from the top down and pull it tight as you go, so any water that gets behind runs down and out rather than pooling.
  5. On the exterior side, tape the top edge with a waterproof repair tape so water sheds over it rather than behind it. Bottom edge stays untaped so it can drain.
  6. If the opening is structural or big, put plywood over the outside and poly on the inside. Plywood stops things coming through; poly stops the weather.

Duct tape directly onto painted siding or trim in cold wet weather will not hold and will pull paint when it does let go. Fasten mechanically wherever you can.

Splitting the house

This is the underrated use. If the power is out and you are heating one room with a woodstove, a sheet of 4 mil poly stapled across a hallway or a stairwell opening stops the heat wandering into rooms nobody is in. Same idea as hanging a blanket in a doorway, but it lets light through and it seals better.

Also useful for closing off a room that got wet, so you are not living with the damp air from it.

The window film kit

Different product, different job. The shrink film kits are for windows that work but leak heat — single pane, old aluminum frames, anything with a draft you can feel. The film goes on double-sided tape around the frame, then you run a hair dryer over it and it shrinks drum-tight and clear.

It genuinely works. A single-pane window loses heat several times faster than a double, and the film adds a still air gap, which is the whole mechanism behind insulated glass in the first place. The catch is that you need power for the hair dryer, so this is a job for the calm before, not the outage during.

  • Clean and dry the frame first or the tape will not stick. Wipe with rubbing alcohol.
  • Put the tape on the frame, not the glass, and leave the backing on until the film is up.
  • Cut generously, stick the top first, then the sides, then the bottom, keeping it loose. Wrinkles are fine at this stage.
  • Heat from the middle outward and stop as soon as it goes tight. Overheating melts holes.
  • Trim the excess with a sharp blade against the tape edge.

Keep some on hand

A roll of 6 mil, a box of staples, a roll of waterproof repair tape and a few furring strips is about the cheapest insurance in the house. A tree branch through a window at ten at night is a two-hour problem if you have that and an overnight disaster if you do not.

Poly sheeting, window film kits, staples, repair tape and lath are stocked — poly on the storm wall, tape and film on the Fix It side. If the hole is in your roof rather than your wall, go read tarping a roof leak instead.
