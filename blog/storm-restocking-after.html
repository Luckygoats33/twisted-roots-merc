Storm Ready

Restocking After, Before You Forget It Happened

The week after a storm is the only week of the year everybody knows exactly what they were missing.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

There is a short window after an outage — maybe ten days — where the whole household remembers precisely what went wrong. The lantern that took a battery nobody had. The saw that would not start. The fact that there was no can opener that did not plug in.

Ten days later everybody has moved on and the same list is waiting for you next winter. So do it while it stings.

Write the list on the last dark night

Literally. Get a pen out while the power is still off and write down every single thing you wished you had, including the small stupid ones. Especially the small stupid ones — a book of matches, a second lighter, a bottle opener, a longer cord, a bucket, a pair of dry gloves.

The big items you will remember without help. It is the two-dollar items that repeat every year because nobody writes them down.

The restock walk-through

Once things are back, go through the kit properly rather than shoving it back in the closet.

  1. Water. Refill or replace everything you drank. Sanitize the jugs while they are empty, which is the only time it is easy.
  2. Batteries. Count what is left and replace what you used, plus a bit. Pull the alkalines out of anything going back into storage.
  3. Food. Replace what you ate. Check the dates on what you did not, and note what nobody would eat so you do not buy it again.
  4. Fuel. Refill the gas cans with fresh stabilized fuel, top off the propane, get a spare 1 lb cylinder. Run the generator dry or fill it, per the fuel piece.
  5. Charge everything. Power banks, rechargeable lights, the jump pack, the tool batteries.
  6. First aid. Replace what came out of it, and check dates on anything you did not touch.
  7. The saw. Sharpen or replace the chain, clean the air filter, top up the bar oil, and buy the spare chain you wished you had.
  8. Tarps and rope. Whatever went on the roof is now used. Buy the replacement now, not next October.
  9. Cash. Put back what you spent out of the emergency envelope.
  10. Prescriptions. Refill anything that got low, and re-establish the two-week buffer.

The upgrade list

The other half is the things you did not own at all. Every outage produces two or three, and they are worth buying now while the reason is fresh.

Fix the thing that broke

The other week-after job. The gutter that overflowed, the downspout that dumped at the foundation, the gate that would not latch, the shed door that blew open, the limb that is now resting on something. All of it is easier to deal with in the calm and all of it is guaranteed to do the same thing again.

And the tree. If a tree came down and it made you think about the other one, that is the moment to get somebody out to look at it, while the memory is doing the arguing for you — see the leaning tree.

Then put a date on the next check

Not a date on the calendar — a habit. Tie the kit check to something you already do twice a year: smoke alarms, clock changes, the first weekend the heat comes on. That is what turns a kit into a system, and it is covered in rotating and maintaining a kit.

We restock hardest in the week after a blow, so the shelf is deep right when you are motivated. Check the storm page before you come, and tell us what we did not have.
