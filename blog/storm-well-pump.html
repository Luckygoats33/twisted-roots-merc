Storm Ready

When the Power Goes and So Does the Water

A well pump is an electric appliance, which means half the houses out here lose their plumbing the moment the lights do.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

If you are on town water, an outage is an inconvenience. If you are on a well, an outage is also a plumbing failure, because the thing that moves water out of the ground is an electric motor two hundred feet down and it has no backup.

What you actually have left

When the pump stops, the pressure tank still has whatever was in it. A typical residential tank holds forty to eighty gallons of total volume but only eight to twenty gallons of usable drawdown, because most of the tank is a pressurized air bladder. That is a toilet flush and a couple of hand washes, and then the tap sputters and stops.

Two things add to that reserve and both are already in your house: the water heater holds forty to eighty gallons that can be drawn from its drain valve, and each toilet tank holds a couple of gallons of clean water. Turn the power or gas off to the heater before you draw from it, and know that the first gallon out of a heater that has never been drained will be sediment.

Backing it up

OptionCostReality
Store water ahead of timeLowThe right answer for almost everybody. See water storage
Generator plus a transfer switch or interlockModerateWorks, but a submersible pump has a big starting surge. Size the generator for it
Hand pump alongside the submersibleModerateOnly practical on shallower wells. Ask your well driller about your static level
Gravity tank uphill of the houseHigherExcellent if your land allows it. Fills when power is on, feeds by gravity when it is not
Creek or rain barrel plus a filterLowFine for flushing. Treat before drinking — see purification

If you are sizing a generator for a well pump, the number that matters is the starting current, not the running current. A pump that runs on eight amps may pull thirty for a fraction of a second on start, and a generator that cannot supply it will simply refuse. Get the pump's nameplate figures and ask somebody who knows.

What to do the moment the power drops

  1. Fill containers immediately, before the pressure tank is gone. Pots, jugs, the kettle, drinking bottles.
  2. Fill the bathtub for flushing water. This is the biggest single reservoir in the house and it is free.
  3. Tell everyone in the house that the tap is now finite. This is the step that matters, because one person doing dishes uses the whole reserve.
  4. Set up the flushing system — a bucket by each toilet and a source to refill it.
  5. Turn off the pump breaker if you expect power to return in a surge or with a rough restoration. See below.

When the power comes back

Do not just walk away. A well system that has been off has some things to check:

  • Let it run and repressurize before you use anything. Listen — a pump that runs continuously and never builds pressure means the tank has lost its bladder charge or the pump has an issue.
  • Run a tap until it clears. Air in the lines and a bit of sediment is normal, and it will spit.
  • If the well head was flooded, or floodwater got anywhere near it, do not drink the water until it has been shocked and tested. Surface water reaching a well casing is a genuine contamination path, and it does not change how the water tastes.
  • If your neighbourhood got a boil notice, follow it even on your own well until you know why it was issued.

The well head itself

Walk out and look at it after a storm. The casing should stand well above grade with an intact, sealed cap, and the ground should slope away from it. A cap knocked loose by a limb, or a well head sitting in a puddle, is a direct path from the surface into your drinking water.

While you are out there, note the well's depth and the pump's specs somewhere you can find them. When you are calling a pump company on day three of an outage, being able to read them the numbers moves you up the list considerably.

Jugs, buckets, hose, filters and bottled water are on the storm wall, and the pipe fittings and push-fit couplings are on the Fix It side for whatever the freeze or the pressure surge broke.
