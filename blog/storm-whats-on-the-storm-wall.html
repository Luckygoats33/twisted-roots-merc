Storm Ready

What Is on the Storm Wall, and Why Each Thing Is There

Front left as you come in, arranged in the order you will need it, chosen by two people who have lost power for four days.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

The storm wall is the first thing on your left when you come in the front door of the merc. It is there rather than in a back corner because when somebody comes through that door in a wind advisory, they are not in the mood to hunt for it.

Everything on it was picked the same way Eric picked the Fix It wall: one good version of each thing rather than six mediocre ones, and nothing on it that neither of us would use at our own house.

How it is arranged

Left to right, roughly in the order the problems arrive.

The items people are surprised by

  • Bar and chain oil, by the case. Because on the morning after a blow every saw in the valley is running and nobody has any. It is the most reliably sold-out item on this coast.
  • The manual can opener. The most forgotten object in emergency preparedness. It costs a few dollars and its absence ruins a pantry.
  • D cells. Nobody owns D cells anymore until they find the old lantern in the garage that takes four of them.
  • Wool socks. Not glamorous. Genuinely one of the highest-value items on the wall — dry feet is most of being warm.
  • The emergency toilet kit. Which people avoid looking at and then thank us for, because a bucket takes the entire load off a saturated septic system.
  • Fuel stabilizer. Two dollars of prevention against the single most common reason a generator will not start.

What Storm Mode does to it

When the forecast turns, Carrie hits one button behind the counter and the reorder minimums jump. AA goes from ten packs to a hundred. Tarps go from ten to twenty-five. Bar oil goes from six to twenty-four. Propane cylinders go from eight to thirty-six. The wall gets refaced, the water gets stacked by the door, and the tarp pallet comes out onto the floor.

The same button rescales the bars on the storm page, so when it says low, it means low for a storm week rather than low for an ordinary Tuesday. That is worth knowing before you drive over, because it is the difference between a wasted trip and a useful one.

What is not on it, and where it is instead

Lumber, plywood, gravel and sand are out in the yard. Sealants, fasteners, cords, tape, tools and the CO alarms are on the Fix It side. The packable, light versions — bivvies, ferro rods, pump filters, compasses — are over on Hunt & Field, which is where the go-bag things naturally live.

The wall is written by the neighbors

Most of what is on it got there because somebody stood at the counter in a windstorm and asked for something we did not have. Carrie writes those down and the ones that come up more than once go on the shelf permanently, for everybody.

So if you come in during a blow and we do not have the thing you needed, that is the single most useful thing you can tell us. Come see us, or leave a note. It is a small store and it gets better one item at a time.
