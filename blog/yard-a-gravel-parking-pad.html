The Yard

Putting In a Gravel Parking Pad

Fabric, base, top course and edges. Skip the fabric and you will be adding rock every year for the rest of your life.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A gravel pad for a truck, a trailer or a second vehicle is one of the better returns on a weekend out here. Done right it drains, it stays put, and it does not turn into ruts in December. Done wrong it sinks into the clay and you top it up forever.

Sizing it

UseSize
One vehicle10 x 20 ft
One vehicle with doors and walking room12 x 22 ft
Two vehicles side by side20 x 20 ft
Truck and a small trailer12 x 35 ft
TurnaroundAdd 10 ft in one direction

The layers

  1. Strip the topsoil. All of it. Organic material compresses and decomposes, and anything you build on it settles unevenly. Dig to firm subsoil, usually 6 to 10 inches down.
  2. Grade and slope. One to two percent fall in one direction. A flat pad holds water and a pad sloped toward a building is a problem you built on purpose.
  3. Lay woven geotextile fabric. Overlap seams by at least a foot. On clay soil this is not optional and it is the single biggest determinant of how long the pad lasts.
  4. Base course. Four to six inches of 3/4 inch minus crushed rock, placed in two lifts and compacted each time. Damp rock compacts better than dry.
  5. Top course. Two inches of 1/4 inch minus or crushed surfacing, compacted. This is what fills the voids and gives you a surface that does not roll under a foot.
  6. Edges. Something to hold the rock in: treated 4x4 timbers pinned with rebar, or a shallow trench, or a berm. Without an edge, the gravel migrates outward and you lose an inch a year.

How much rock

A cubic yard covers 81 square feet at 4 inches. A 12 by 22 foot pad is 264 square feet.

Eight tons is a dump truck load, not a pickup load, and definitely not bags. We sell gravel by the 50 lb bag for post holes and small work. A parking pad is a call to a rock supplier and we will happily give you a name. Buying 320 bags from us would be a very expensive way to do this and we will say so at the counter.

Compaction is the job

Rent a plate compactor. It is the difference between a pad and a pile. Compact each lift separately, in overlapping passes, in two directions. Damp the rock with a hose first, because dry rock does not lock up. You will know it is done when the plate stops sinking in and starts riding on the surface.

Driving over it with the truck a few times is not compaction. It compacts two strips and leaves the rest loose.

Coastal maintenance

  • Moss and weeds will colonize a gravel pad within a couple of years. Raking it annually disturbs them and keeps it draining.
  • Fir needles are the real enemy. They mat, hold water and turn the top inch into soil. Blow or rake them off before winter.
  • Top up the surface course every few years. That is normal, not failure.
  • Ruts mean the base failed, usually because the fabric was skipped or the subgrade was soft. Dig it out and fix it, do not add rock on top.

Gravel or concrete

For a parking pad, gravel wins on cost, drains far better, is easy to repair, and does not crack. Concrete wins on cleanliness, on wheeled equipment, and on anything you want to work on a vehicle on. On the wet north side of a house, gravel also does not grow the sheet of moss that concrete does. See the concrete pad piece if you decide the other way.

Bagged gravel, treated 4x4 edging and fabric are in the Yard. Compactor rental and bulk rock are town calls, and we will make the recommendation.
