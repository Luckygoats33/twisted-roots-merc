The Yard

A Dog Kennel That Holds and Drains

Size it to the dog, roof half of it, and put something under the surface so it does not turn into a mud pen by December.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

An outdoor kennel is a fence problem, a drainage problem and a roof problem in about equal measure. People solve the fence and skip the other two, and by the middle of winter they have a mud pen with a wet dog in it.

Size

DogMinimum runComfortable
Under 35 lb4 x 8 ft5 x 10 ft
35 to 60 lb5 x 10 ft6 x 12 ft
Over 60 lb6 x 12 ft8 x 16 ft or larger
Two dogs togetherAdd half againOr separate runs

Height depends on the dog. Four feet holds most, six feet holds a jumper, and a determined climber needs a top or an inward-angled kick rail. A hunting dog that has decided to leave will leave, and there is no fence height that beats a dog with a reason. See the hunt and field shelf for what people around here run.

Framing it in wood

  1. 4x4x8 treated posts at every corner and every 6 to 8 feet along the sides, set 24 to 30 inches deep in concrete on a gravel base.
  2. 2x4 rails top, middle and bottom, fastened to the post faces with structural screws.
  3. Welded wire fencing, 2x4 inch mesh or smaller, stapled and then trapped under a 1x4 batten screwed over the top. Staples alone pull out.
  4. Gate framed from 2x4 with a diagonal brace running from the bottom hinge corner up to the top latch corner. Read the gate piece before you build it, not after it sags.
  5. Bury a wire apron or a run of gravel-filled trench along the fence line if you have a digger.

Chain link panel kits are the other route and they go up in an afternoon. They also do not last as well on wet ground unless you set them on something solid, and they are a Toledo Do it Best purchase, not ours.

The floor, which is the whole thing

Bare dirt becomes mud. Grass becomes mud. There are three surfaces that work.

SurfaceProsCons
Compacted gravel over fabricDrains, cheap, easy to refreshHard on some feet, needs topping up
Concrete pad, sloped and broomedCleanest, hoses offCost, cold, hard on joints
Coarse wood chips over gravelSoft, drains, warmNeeds replacing yearly, can hold odor

Whichever you use, slope the ground about a quarter inch per foot so water leaves in one direction, and put landscape fabric under the gravel so it does not sink into the clay. Fine sand does not work here, it holds water.

Roof at least half of it

Dogs need a dry place to stand that is not the inside of the house. A shed roof over one end, sloped away from the door, with 12 inches of overhang, is enough. Metal roofing on 2x4 rafters. Put the shelter under it, raised off the ground on a treated frame with the opening facing away from the prevailing weather.

Do not put the dog house directly under the run of the roof edge unless you want the drip line landing on the doorway, which is exactly what happens.

Details people wish they had done

  • A hose bib within reach, or at least a hose that reaches. You will rinse this.
  • The gate opening in from the side, not swinging into the run where a dog can rush it.
  • A double latch. Same reasoning as the coop, but for the opposite direction.
  • Shade in summer. A tarp or a panel on the west side.
  • Nothing chewable at nose height. Exposed board edges get chewed and then they hold water.

Posts, rails, gravel and concrete are in the Yard. Wire, staples, hinges, latches and hardware are inside on the Fix It wall. Fence panels and chain link kits are a town trip and we will say so.
