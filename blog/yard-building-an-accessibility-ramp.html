The Yard

Building a Ramp Somebody Can Actually Use

One inch of rise per foot of run, a level landing at both ends, and a rail. The slope people guess at is usually twice too steep.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Ramps get built in a hurry, usually because somebody came home from the hospital and the front steps became a problem overnight. Built in a hurry, they are almost always too steep, and a ramp that is too steep is not usable by the person it was built for and is dangerous for whoever is pushing.

The slope number

One inch of rise for every twelve inches of run. That is the standard, written as 1:12, and it is the number to build to. It sounds gentle until you do the arithmetic.

Rise to overcomeRamp length at 1:12Landings needed
6 in6 ftTop and bottom
12 in12 ftTop and bottom
18 in18 ftTop, bottom, one intermediate
24 in24 ftTop, bottom, one intermediate
30 in30 ftTop, bottom, two intermediate
36 in36 ftTop, bottom, two intermediate

Thirty-six feet of ramp for a three foot porch surprises everybody. That is why real ramps switch back. A single run should not rise more than 30 inches, which means 30 feet, before it hits a level landing.

The other dimensions

  • Clear width: 36 inches minimum between the rails. Build the deck at 40 inches wide and you have room for the rail posts.
  • Landings: 60 by 60 inches at the top, at the bottom, and at every turn. A turn landing needs to be big enough to pivot a chair, which is why 60 inches is not negotiable.
  • Cross slope: as close to zero as you can manage. A ramp that tilts sideways steers a chair into the rail.
  • Handrails: both sides, 34 to 38 inches above the ramp surface, graspable, continuous, extending 12 inches past the ends.
  • Edge protection: a 2 inch curb or a bottom rail so a wheel cannot roll off the side.
  • Surface: non-slip. This is a coastal ramp. It will be wet and it will grow algae.

Framing it

A ramp is a deck on a slope. Joists at 16 inches on center, decking across, posts and footings under the beams, same as any deck. Treated ground contact material everywhere, because the underside of a ramp is the definition of a place that never dries.

Run the decking perpendicular to the direction of travel, with the gaps at 1/8 to 1/4 inch. Wider gaps catch small caster wheels and cane tips. Run the boards crown up so water sheds.

The surface, which matters most here

Wet wood on a slope in the shade is genuinely slippery, and by the second winter it will have a film of algae. Options, roughly in order of effectiveness: adhesive grit strips on every board, a grit additive mixed into an exterior paint or deck coating, roofing shingle strips fastened down, or expanded metal mesh. The grit-in-paint approach is the most common and needs redoing every few years.

Whatever you choose, budget for cleaning it. See the moss piece.

Where to be honest

Permanent ramps are permitted construction in Lincoln County and they should be. There are also good alternatives worth considering first: a modular aluminum ramp that can be rented or bought and reconfigured, or a threshold ramp if the rise is only a few inches. For a temporary situation, rental is often the right call and nobody at a lumber counter is going to mention it.

And this is a case where a bad build is worse than none. If you are not confident, ask around. There are volunteer groups and church crews in Lincoln County that build ramps, and local contractors who have done a dozen of them.
