Deck Railing That Does Its Job

Heights, gaps and the post connection that decides whether a railing catches somebody or comes off in their hand.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A railing is not trim. It is a safety device, and the failure mode is somebody leaning on it and going over. The good news is that the numbers are simple and the hard part is one connection.

The numbers

DimensionResidential target
Railing height, deck surface to top of rail36 in minimum, 42 in common
Guard required when deck is above30 in from grade
Maximum gap between balustersLess than 4 in. A 4 in ball must not pass
Gap under the bottom railLess than 4 in
Post spacing6 ft maximum, closer is better
Load the guard must resist200 lb concentrated in any direction at the top
Stair handrail graspable size1.25 to 2 in diameter, or equivalent

Check with Lincoln County before you build, because local amendments happen and the inspector's copy of the number is the one that counts. But those figures will not lead you badly astray.

The four inch rule, in practice

The gap limit exists so a small child cannot get through or get a head stuck. Four inches is the maximum anywhere in the assembly: between balusters, under the bottom rail, and at the triangle formed where a stair rail meets the tread.

Practical spacing with 2x2 balusters works out to about 5 inches on center, which puts the gap right around 3.5 inches. Cut a spacer block that width and use it for every baluster instead of measuring. Faster and more consistent.

The post connection is the whole thing

Two hundred pounds applied at the top of a 36 inch post is a serious moment at the base. A guard post bolted through a rim joist with two lag screws will not reliably hold it. That is the connection that fails.

What works:

  • Two through-bolts with washers, vertically separated as far as the post allows, into a rim joist that is itself blocked and fastened.
  • Blocking between joists behind the post so the load goes into more than one member.
  • A proper hold-down tension device designed for guard posts. These exist, they are not expensive, and they are what an engineer specifies.
  • Notching the post around the rim joist. Common, and it removes material exactly where the stress is highest. If you do it, keep the notch shallow and add a hold-down.

What does not work: deck screws, lag screws into the end grain of a joist, or a post sitting on top of the decking and fastened down through it.

Build order that saves grief

  1. Set and secure all the posts first, plumb, before any rail goes on. Check plumb both ways.
  2. Run a string from end post to end post at the finished top rail height. Trim posts to that line.
  3. Install the bottom rail, then the top rail, both attached to the posts, not just to each other.
  4. Cut all balusters at once to the same length. If the deck is not perfectly flat, measure the worst bay first.
  5. Set balusters with a spacer block, checking plumb every fourth one and adjusting the spacing gradually rather than all at once.
  6. Add a cap rail on top if you like. A flat cap catches water, so slope it slightly or accept the maintenance.

Coastal notes

Cedar for anything you put your hand on. Treated splinters worse and leaves copper on your palms. Stainless or hot-dip fasteners everywhere, because a railing failure that starts with a rusted bolt is exactly the failure mode described above. Predrill cedar near ends, it splits easily.

Horizontal cable and horizontal board railings look good and give kids a ladder. That is a real consideration and some jurisdictions restrict it. Think about who uses the deck.

2x2 balusters we do not stock. 2x4, 1x4 and 1x6 to eight feet we do, and 1x4 balusters set on edge look fine. Check the racks. If you are building a full railing run, that is a lumberyard order, and we will say so.
