Why Your Gate Sags and the Four Ways to Fix It

Diagnose it before you buy anything. A gate that drags is either a post problem, a hinge problem or a frame problem.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A gate that drags on the ground is one of the most satisfying things to fix, because the repair takes an hour and you notice it every single day afterward. But you have to figure out which of three things is actually wrong, and people almost always guess the frame when it is the post.

Diagnosing it in five minutes

  1. Open the gate to about 45 degrees and look at the hinge post from the side. Is it plumb? Put a level on it.
  2. Lift the free corner of the gate with your hand. If it lifts easily and the post moves with it, the post is the problem.
  3. Close the gate and look at the frame. Is the rectangle still a rectangle? Measure both diagonals. If they differ by more than about a quarter inch, the frame has racked.
  4. Look at the hinges. Are the screws pulling out? Is there play in the barrel?
SymptomCauseFix
Hinge post leans toward the gatePost not set deep or wide enoughReset the post, or brace it back
Frame diagonals do not matchFrame has racked out of squareAnti-sag cable or a diagonal brace

The fixes, in order of likelihood

Fix one: the hinge post

This is the usual culprit and the least fun answer. A gate hangs its whole weight off one post as a cantilever, and it is a long lever. A gate post needs to go deeper and wider than the line posts around it. Thirty inches deep, twelve inches across, gravel in the bottom, and set in concrete. If yours was set at 18 inches in a narrow hole, no amount of hardware on the gate will fix what the post is doing.

Read the post setting piece before you redo it, because doing it the same way twice is a common outcome.

Fix two: an anti-sag cable

If the frame has racked, run a cable diagonally from the top hinge corner down to the bottom latch corner, with a turnbuckle in it. Direction matters and people get it backwards constantly. The cable must go from the top of the hinge side to the bottom of the latch side. That way tightening it pulls the sagging corner up. Backwards, it pulls it down harder.

Snug it until the frame is square by the diagonal measurement, then stop. Cranking further will bow the frame the other way and bind the latch.

Fix three: a wood diagonal brace

Same geometry, but in compression instead of tension. A 2x4 run from the bottom of the hinge side up to the top of the latch side, cut to fit tight between the frame rails. In compression the brace runs the opposite direction from a cable. It is stronger, it is permanent, and it is the right answer on a heavy gate.

Fix four: better hinges

Three hinges instead of two. Longer lag screws that reach 2 inches into solid post, not 1 inch of screw into a picket. Heavy strap hinges rather than butt hinges on anything wider than about three feet. And hot-dip galvanized, always, because we are on the coast and plain zinc plating gives up fast. See the hardware piece.

The one that is not a fix

Cutting the bottom of the gate off so it clears the ground. Everybody does it, it works for a season, and then the gate sags another half inch and now you have a short gate that still drags. It also cuts through the treated shell of the pickets and opens end grain to the ground.

Cable kits, turnbuckles, hinges and lag screws are on the Fix It wall. Posts, 2x4s and fast-set are out back.
