Framing a Small Shed, Start to Sheathing

Floor, walls, roof, in that order, with a cut list you can check against the truck before you leave.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

An 8x8 shed is the largest thing most people build alone and the smallest thing that teaches you the whole sequence. Floor, then walls, then roof, then sheathing, then roofing, then trim and door. Do it in that order and it goes smoothly. Do it out of order and you will be shimming things.

Before anything, the boring questions

Check whether you need a permit. In Lincoln County a small accessory building under a certain size often does not require one, but setbacks from property lines apply regardless, and if you put power or water in it, that is a permit no matter the size. Call the county before you dig. Ten minutes on the phone beats moving a shed.

The floor

  1. Level a gravel pad, larger than the shed footprint by a foot on each side. Compact it in lifts. See the pad piece.
  2. Set treated 4x4 skids running the short direction, spaced about 4 feet apart, level to each other in both directions.
  3. Build the floor frame from treated 2x6 with joists 16 inches on center, ends squared by checking the diagonals.
  4. Sheath the floor with treated plywood, seams landing on joists, screwed down.

The floor must be square and it must be level, because everything above it inherits both. Measure both diagonals of the frame before you sheath. They should match within about an eighth of an inch.

The walls

Build each wall flat on the floor deck, then stand it. This is why the floor comes first.

  1. Cut all studs to the same length at once, off one stop block, not one at a time off a tape.
  2. Lay out the plates together, marking stud positions on both at once so they cannot disagree.
  3. Nail or screw the frame together flat, check the diagonals, then sheath the wall while it is still flat if you can. A sheathed wall is square and stays square.
  4. Stand it, brace it, nail the bottom plate to the floor frame.
  5. Repeat for the other three, lapping the top plates at the corners.

The roof

For a shed this size, a simple shed roof, one slope, is far easier than a gable and sheds our rain fine. Make the back wall shorter than the front by enough to give you at least a 3-in-12 slope, more if you can. Rafters run front to back, 16 or 24 inches on center, birdsmouth cut where they sit on the top plates, overhanging 8 to 12 inches on all sides.

Overhangs are not decoration here. They keep water off the walls and off the door, and they are the cheapest weatherproofing you can build. Do not trim them flush to save plywood.

What we can and cannot supply

Our racks stop at 8 feet. For an 8x8 shed that mostly works out, and for anything bigger it does not. Full 4x8 sheathing sheets we do not carry either. We stock 2x4 and 2x6 in 8 foot lengths, 4x4x8 treated, 1x4 and 1x6 boards, and 2x4 foot project panels.

So: build the shed with a load from Siletz River Lumber, and come to us for the four studs you miscut, the box of screws you ran out of, and the one panel you need to close a gap. That is genuinely the arrangement and we are comfortable with it. See the racks, and read the roof sheeting piece before you get to that stage.
