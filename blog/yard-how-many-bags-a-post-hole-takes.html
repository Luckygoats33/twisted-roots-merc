How Many Bags a Post Hole Actually Takes

A table you can do arithmetic against, plus the honest note that the hole always ends up bigger than you dug it.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

The single most common question at the dry shed door. Somebody is putting in six posts and wants to know whether to buy six bags or twelve. The arithmetic is easy and the answer is usually more than people want to hear.

The math, if you want it

The volume of concrete you need is the volume of the hole minus the volume of the post. A 10 inch hole 24 inches deep is about 0.91 cubic feet. A 4x4 running through that same 24 inches takes up about 0.21 cubic feet. You need about 0.7 cubic feet of concrete, less whatever the gravel base takes.

A 50 lb bag of fast-set yields about 0.37 cubic feet. A 60 lb bag of standard mix yields about 0.45. An 80 lb bag yields about 0.6. So that standard hole is right around two bags of fast-set, and this is where people get surprised.

The correction nobody prints on the bag

Those numbers assume a clean cylindrical hole the size you meant to dig. In clay with a hand digger, working around a root, on a slope, in the rain, you do not get a clean cylinder. You get a funnel with a flare at the top and a gouge on one side where the digger slipped. That funnel is real volume and it eats bags.

Our rule at the counter, and it has not embarrassed us yet: figure the table, then add one bag for every three posts, then add one more bag total. If you set the whole fence and have two bags left, you have two bags. Bagged concrete keeps indefinitely if you keep it dry and off the concrete floor. A bag you drove back for costs you forty minutes and a gallon of gas.

Things that change the count

  • Gravel base. Four inches of gravel in a 10 inch hole removes about 0.15 cubic feet, which is roughly half a bag over four posts. Worth it anyway.
  • Gate and corner posts. Dig them bigger on purpose. Budget an extra bag each and do not feel clever about saving it.
  • Rocky ground. Up the hill toward Logsden you will hit rock and end up with an oversized, irregular hole. Add half again.
  • 6x6 posts. The post displaces more, so you need less concrete, but the hole has to be wider, so you need more. It roughly cancels. We do not stock 6x6, so this is theoretical for us.

What we can put in your truck

Everything in the dry shed is 50, 60 or 80 lb, and we will help you load it. Eight bags of 80 lb is 640 pounds, which is fine in a half-ton and worth thinking about in a small truck with old springs. If you need thirty bags, call Siletz River Lumber or Toledo Do it Best and let somebody deliver it on a pallet. That is not us. We are the place you come when you are halfway through and short two bags.

Check what the dry shed has before you hitch up. And read how to set the post if you have not done one in clay before.
