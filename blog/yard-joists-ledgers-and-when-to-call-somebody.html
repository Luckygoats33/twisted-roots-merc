The Yard

Joists, Ledgers, and Knowing When to Call Somebody

Most of a deck is carpentry you can learn. The connection to the house is not, and it is the one that hurts people.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

We will happily sell you lumber and talk through a deck board or a railing. We are going to be direct about this one: the ledger connection, where a deck attaches to a house, is the number one cause of deck collapse, and deck collapses hurt people. It is not a place to improvise.

What the ledger does

On an attached deck, one edge of the whole structure hangs off a board bolted to the house rim joist. Everything on that half of the deck, plus everybody standing on it, is carried by that connection. A deck collapse almost never starts with a broken joist. It starts with the ledger pulling away from the wall, usually because it was nailed instead of bolted, or because water got behind it and rotted the house framing it was bolted to.

Ledger detailRightWrong
FastenersThrough bolts or approved structural lag screws, staggered patternNails, deck screws, or lags on a whim
Attached toSolid house rim joist or blockingSiding, sheathing, brick veneer, or a cantilever
FlashingMetal flashing over the ledger, tucked behind housewrapCaulk. Caulk is not flashing
Lateral loadHold-down devices per codeNothing
Spacing behindWashers or a drainage gap so water passesTight to the siding, trapping water

Joist basics you can use

Joists are the boards running from the ledger to the beam, carrying the decking. Two numbers matter: how far apart they are, and how far they span. Closer spacing and shorter spans are always safer.

Joist16 in on center12 in on center
2x6About 9 ft max spanAbout 10 ft
2x8About 12 ftAbout 13 ft
2x10About 15 ftAbout 16 ft

Treat those as rough orientation only. Actual allowable spans depend on species, grade, wet service conditions and what is on top, and the county has the real table. Our racks stop at 8 feet, so anything with a real span is coming from Siletz River Lumber anyway.

The things worth knowing regardless

  • Joists get hung in galvanized joist hangers, fastened with joist hanger nails, not deck screws. The screws are not rated for that shear.
  • Blocking between joists at midspan keeps them from twisting and stiffens the whole deck noticeably.
  • Crown up. Every joist has a slight arc. Sight it and install the crown facing the sky.
  • Joist tape on the top edge of every joist keeps water out of the fastener holes and is the cheapest life extension available.
  • A freestanding deck with its own posts on the house side avoids the ledger question entirely, and on a small deck that is often the better plan.

Where the honest line is

Here is what we tell people at the counter, and we mean it.

If it holds up people, spans more than a few feet, attaches to your house, or sits more than about 30 inches off the ground, get a permit and get it looked at.

Lincoln County requires permits for most decks. A permit gets you a plan review and an inspection by somebody whose job is to catch exactly the ledger detail nobody thinks about. It is not expensive relative to a deck, and it matters when you sell the house.

Repair work you can absolutely do yourself: replacing decking, replacing a railing picket, adding blocking, taping joists, sistering a joist alongside a soft one, rebuilding stairs. Read the deck board piece and the stairs piece.

Work to hire out or get inspected: the ledger, the beams, the posts and footings, anything cantilevered, and any deck that already sags, bounces noticeably, or has movement at the house wall.

Hangers, hanger nails and structural screws are on the Fix It wall. Framing lumber to 8 feet is in the Yard. Anything longer, we will give you the lumberyard's number rather than pretend.
