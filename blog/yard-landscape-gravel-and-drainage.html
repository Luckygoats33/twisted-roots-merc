The Yard

Landscape Gravel, Drainage, and Where Your Water Goes

Angular gravel locks and drains, round rock does not, and fabric is the difference between five years and twenty.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Almost every outdoor problem out here is a water problem wearing a different hat. Rotted posts, heaving pads, mud in the dog run, moss on the deck, a soggy corner of the yard that never dries. Understanding gravel and where water goes fixes more of these than any product will.

Angular versus round

Crushed gravel has fractured faces that lock together when compacted. It forms a stable base you can build on. Round river rock rolls, never locks, and stays loose forever. The two look similar in a pile and behave completely differently.

The word minus means the material includes everything from the stated size down to dust. Those fines are what let it compact into a hard surface. Clean means the fines are screened out, which leaves the voids open for water to move through. You want minus under things and clean around drains, and mixing them up is the usual mistake.

Landscape fabric, and when it is worth it

Under gravel on clay soil, always. Without a separation layer, the clay migrates up into the gravel and the gravel sinks into the clay, and in five years your four inch base is a three inch base with mud in it. A woven geotextile fabric stops that, and it costs almost nothing relative to the gravel.

Where fabric is not worth it is as a weed barrier in a planting bed. It clogs, weeds root on top of it in the mulch anyway, and eventually you have plastic fibers all through the soil. Use cardboard and mulch instead.

The four inch base rule

Under a shed pad, a landing, a parking pad or a path, the standard is 4 inches of compacted crushed gravel, placed and compacted in two lifts of 2 inches each. Compacting 4 inches all at once does not work. The bottom stays loose.

Rent a plate compactor if you can. A hand tamper works for small areas and it is a genuine workout. Water the gravel lightly before compacting and it will lock up considerably better.

Where your water goes

Before you build anything, stand outside in a hard rain and watch. That single half hour tells you more than any amount of planning. Look at where the downspouts discharge, where the ground slopes, where puddles form and how long they last.

  • Grade should fall away from every structure at least 6 inches over the first 10 feet.
  • Downspouts should discharge at least 6 feet from a foundation, or into a solid pipe that carries the water away.
  • A gravel path that runs across a slope becomes a creek. Cross it with a shallow swale or a drain.
  • Water that has nowhere to go will find the lowest point and stay there, and that is where your rot problem will be.

If the low spot is persistent, you want a drain, not more gravel. See the french drain piece.

How much gravel

Volume in cubic feet is length times width times depth in feet. A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet and covers 81 square feet at 4 inches deep. Gravel weighs roughly 2,700 pounds per cubic yard, so a yard is about 54 of our 50 lb bags.
