Mixing Concrete Without a Mixer

A wheelbarrow, a hoe and about four minutes a bag. The trick is adding water in two stages and stopping before it goes soupy.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

You do not need a mixer to place a couple hundred pounds of concrete. You need a wheelbarrow with a flat bottom, a mortar hoe or a garden hoe, a bucket you have marked, and the willingness to do it a bag at a time instead of three bags at once. Most bad small pours are not bad concrete. They are two bags dumped in together, too much water thrown at them, and a hoe that never reached the corners.

What you need

  • A contractor wheelbarrow, or a mixing tub if the pour is small
  • A hoe. A mortar hoe with the two holes in it is better but a garden hoe works
  • A five gallon bucket with the water line marked on the inside
  • Gloves. Wet cement is caustic and it will take the skin off your knuckles by dinner

How much water

Bags print a range. The bottom of the range is almost always the right answer. Extra water buys you easy mixing and costs you strength, and it costs a lot more than people believe. Concrete gets weaker roughly in proportion to how much water you add past what the cement needs.

The two-stage method

  1. Dump one bag in the barrow. One. Not two.
  2. Pull the dry mix into a ring with a crater in the middle, like you are making bread.
  3. Add about two thirds of the water into the crater.
  4. Work from the outside in, folding dry into wet with the hoe. Chop, do not stir. Get the corners and the front lip of the barrow, which is where the dry pockets hide.
  5. When it is uniformly damp and still crumbly, add water in small splashes until it will hold a ridge without slumping.
  6. Dump it, then load the next bag. Do not let a barrow of mixed concrete sit while you mix the next one.

How to tell when it is right

Drag the hoe through it and pull it up into a peak. The peak should stand and the sides of the trench should hold their shape without water pooling in the bottom. If a puddle forms in the groove, you went too far. If it crumbles and will not hold a ridge, it needs a little more.

Too wet is a much bigger problem than too dry. You can always add a splash. You cannot take it back out without adding dry mix and throwing your proportions off.

If you did overdo the water

Add a couple of scoops from the next bag and mix again. This works fine if you are one bag into a five bag pour. It works badly if that was your last bag, which is the real argument for buying one extra.

Cleanup, which is not optional

Hose the barrow and the hoe out before you sit down, not after. Cured concrete comes out of a wheelbarrow with a hammer and a bad attitude. Rinse the water out somewhere it will not run into the river or a storm drain. Let it settle in a low spot in the gravel and it will just be gravel.

We stock 60 and 80 lb concrete mix, 60 lb sand and topping mix, and Type S mortar, all kept dry and off the ground in the shed out back. Mixing tubs, hoes and buckets live inside on the Fix It wall. Anything past about ten bags, and you should think seriously about whether you want a rented mixer from town instead of a sore back.
