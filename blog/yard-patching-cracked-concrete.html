Patching Cracked Concrete So It Stays Patched

Most patches fail because nobody cleaned the crack or figured out why it cracked. Both take longer than the patching.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Concrete cracks. All of it, everywhere, eventually. The question is never whether a slab has cracks, it is whether the crack is doing anything. Answer that before you buy a bag, because half the cracks people patch did not need patching and a quarter of them needed something bigger than a patch.

Read the crack first

What you seeWhat it usually isWhat to do
Hairline, wandering, both sides levelShrinkage from curingNothing, or a flexible sealant to keep water out
Straight, at a control jointWorking as designedNothing. Keep the joint sealed
Wide, one side higher than the otherSettlement. The base movedFix the base or replace the section
Crumbly, flaking surface, no real crackSpalling from a weak surface layerResurface with sand mix
Crack that grows season to seasonSomething is still movingFind the cause before patching anything

A crack with a lip you can catch a boot on is a settlement problem, and mortaring over it means you get the same crack again in the same place next winter. That is a base problem, and often a drainage problem, which usually means water is getting under the slab. Look at where the downspouts go before you look at the concrete.

Preparing the crack, which is the whole job

  1. Chase the crack out with a cold chisel or a grinder until you have sound concrete on both sides. Do not just widen the top.
  2. Undercut the edges if you can, so the groove is wider at the bottom than the top. That gives the patch a mechanical key and it is the single biggest thing you can do for longevity.
  3. Get all the dust and loose material out. Shop vac, then blow it, then vacuum again. Dust is a bond breaker.
  4. Dampen the concrete so it does not steal water out of your patch, but leave no standing water.
  5. Brush on a concrete bonding adhesive if the patch is thin or the surface is old. This is on the Fix It wall and it costs less than doing the job twice.

Which bag

For anything under about two inches thick, sand and topping mix. It has no coarse aggregate so it works into a narrow groove and feathers at the edge. For a hole deeper than two inches with a real footprint, concrete mix. For a crack that needs to move, a flexible urethane crack sealant, not cement at all.

That last one is not a compromise. A control joint or a slab edge that expands and contracts will crack any rigid patch you put in it. Sealant keeps water out and lets the joint work, which is the actual goal.

Placing it

Mix stiffer than you think, press it hard into the groove with a margin trowel, and work it against the sides rather than just filling the middle. Overfill slightly, then strike it flush and match the surrounding texture while it is still workable. A broom finish next to a smooth patch looks worse than the crack did.

Then cover it. A patch is a thin section with a lot of surface area, which means it dries fast and cracks. Damp burlap, a scrap of plastic, or in our weather just letting the rain do it, but keep it wet for two or three days.

Resurfacing a whole spalled area

If the surface is flaking over several square feet, patching individual spots is a losing game. Clean the whole area to sound concrete, pressure wash it, and skim the entire slab with sand mix or a proper resurfacer at about a quarter to half inch. One 60 lb bag of sand mix at half an inch covers roughly 12 square feet. It is more work up front and it lasts.

When it is not a patch job

Structural cracks in a foundation wall, cracks with active water coming through, cracks wider than about a quarter inch that keep growing, or a slab that has dropped an inch. Those are somebody's professional problem, and doing a good-looking patch over them just hides the evidence. Get a look from a foundation contractor and be glad it was a phone call.

Sand mix, concrete mix and bonding adhesive are all here. Check the shed count, and read the bag comparison if you are unsure which one your repair wants.
