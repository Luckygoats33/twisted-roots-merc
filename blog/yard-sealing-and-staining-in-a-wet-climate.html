The Yard

Sealing and Staining Wood in a Climate Like This

Film finishes fail here and penetrating finishes do not. Also, the wood has to actually be dry, which is the hard part.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Exterior wood finishes on the Oregon Coast have a hard job and a short window to be applied in. Most finish failures out here come down to two things: the wrong kind of product, and applying it to wood that was not dry.

Film versus penetrating

A film finish, meaning a solid stain, a paint or a varnish, sits on top of the wood as a layer. When water eventually gets underneath it, which it will, the film blisters and peels, and now you have to strip it before you can refinish. On horizontal surfaces in a wet climate this happens fast.

A penetrating finish, meaning an oil or a semi-transparent stain, soaks into the wood. When it wears out, it fades and thins rather than peeling, and you clean and reapply. No stripping.

Nothing. Let it grayIndefiniteNone. It just goes silver

That last row is a real option and it is what a lot of people out here choose. Untreated cedar and treated fir both weather to gray and neither one is structurally worse for it. The finish is about appearance and about slowing surface checking, not about whether the deck survives.

Is the wood dry enough

This is where most jobs go wrong. Finish applied to damp wood does not penetrate, and it seals moisture in.

  • New pressure treated lumber arrives saturated. Wait until it has dried, which around here means months, not weeks.
  • After washing a deck, wait at least two or three dry days, more if it has been wet all season.
  • The sprinkle test: put a few drops of water on the board. If they soak in within a minute, the wood is ready. If they bead, it is either still wet or there is old finish on it.
  • A moisture meter reading under 15 percent is the reliable answer.

The weather window

You need a stretch of dry days: at least one to prepare, and 24 to 48 hours after application with no rain. On this coast that window mostly exists between midsummer and early fall, and it is narrower than people plan for.

Also watch the temperature and the sun. Most finishes want 50 to 90 degrees. Applying in direct sun on a hot board makes the finish flash off before it penetrates. Work in the shade or follow the shade around the deck.

Application

  1. Clean thoroughly and let it dry. See the cleaning piece.
  2. Sand any fuzzy or splintered areas lightly. Do not sand a whole deck smooth, it closes the pores and the finish will not soak in.
  3. Stir the product thoroughly and keep stirring. Pigment settles and an unstirred can gives you two different colors.
  4. Work board by board, full length, and keep a wet edge. Stopping in the middle of a board leaves a lap mark that will show forever.
  5. Do not overapply. A penetrating finish takes what the wood will absorb and the excess sits on top and gets sticky. Wipe off anything still glossy after fifteen minutes.
  6. Two thin coats beat one thick one, applied wet on wet if the label allows.

What to do about the north side

The shaded side of anything is the side that fails first, and it is worth accepting that it will be on a shorter cycle. Rather than refinishing the whole structure when one face fails, plan to touch up the wet side more often. With a penetrating finish this is easy. With a film finish it is not, which is another argument for penetrating.
