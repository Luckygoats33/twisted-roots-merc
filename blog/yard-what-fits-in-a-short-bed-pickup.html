What Actually Fits in a Short-Bed Pickup

Bed lengths, tailgate math, weight limits, and the reason our racks stop at eight feet.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

The whole Yard is sized around one question: will it go home in the truck that is parked out front. Around here that truck is usually a half-ton with a short bed, often with a canopy, and often already carrying a toolbox.

An 8 foot board rides flat in a standard bed with the tailgate down and a flag on the end. In a short bed it sticks out about two and a half feet, which is legal with a marker but means you should tie it properly. In an SUV with the seats down and the passenger seat forward, an 8 footer will go in at an angle. People do it every week.

The payload sticker in the driver's door jamb is the number that matters, and on a modern half-ton crew cab it is often lower than people assume, sometimes around 1,400 pounds including passengers. Twenty bags of 80 lb concrete is 1,600 pounds. That is over the limit on a lot of trucks, and it is why we will ask before we load thirty bags.

Loading order

  1. Heavy things forward, against the bulkhead, over the axle. Weight behind the rear axle unloads the front wheels and the truck steers badly.
  2. Long lumber flat on the bed floor, running fore and aft, tight to one side.
  3. Bags on top of nothing that can shift. A bag of concrete sliding into the tailgate at a stop sign will break the bag.
  4. Fill the void spaces so nothing can move. A shifting load is worse than an overweight one.
  5. Red flag on anything overhanging more than 4 feet past the tailgate, and a light at night.

The eight foot rule

Everything on our racks is 8 feet or shorter. That is not a limitation we apologize for, it is the point. An 8 foot board fits in a truck without a rack, a trailer, or a strap arrangement that takes twenty minutes. It also fits most of the small jobs people actually do: fence bays, raised beds, a woodshed, a small shed wall, a repair.

If you need twelves, fourteens, sheet goods, siding or a real load, call Siletz River Lumber. They deliver, they will get you the grade you need, and they do it properly. We will give you the number without making it awkward. See the piece on why we stop there.

Things worth having in the truck

  • Two ratchet straps, minimum. Bungees are not tie-downs.
  • A red flag or a rag that will not tear off in the wind.
  • A moving blanket or a piece of carpet to keep lumber off the bedliner.
  • Gloves. Wet treated lumber and bare hands do not mix well.

We will help you load. Always, without being asked, and we will tell you honestly if we think the load is too much for the truck. Read the tie-down piece, then come find us and pull around back.
