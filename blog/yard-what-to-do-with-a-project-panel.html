The Yard

What People Actually Do With a Project Panel

Two feet by four feet of plywood, in the truck without a rack, and a list of the jobs it turns out to be exactly right for.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A project panel is a quarter of a sheet of plywood, cut 2 feet by 4 feet. It fits flat in a truck bed, it fits in the back of a wagon, one person can carry it, and it costs a fraction of a full sheet you would have to have cut down anyway. We stock it in plain and pressure treated because it turns out to be the answer to a surprising number of problems.

The storm one

Keeping one plain panel in the garage is cheap insurance. A branch through a window at eleven at night on a Sunday is not the moment to find out that nobody within twenty miles is open. A panel, a handful of screws and a cordless drill turns a broken window into a nuisance instead of an emergency, and you can deal with glass on a weekday.

Screw into the sash frame or the trim, not into the glazing stop. If it is a rental or you care about the trim, screw into the frame from the outside face and accept the holes. Read the storm page for the rest of the list.

Getting more out of one

A 2x4 foot panel yields useful pieces if you plan the cuts. Two 2x2 squares. Four 1x2 rectangles. A 2x4 with a 6 inch strip ripped off. Sketch it before you cut, because the offcut you throw away is the one you need next week.

Cutting it straight without a table saw is easier than a full sheet. Clamp a straight board as a guide and run a circular saw against it. See the cutting piece.

Plain or treated

Treated for anything that touches ground, sits in a wet location, or lives outdoors permanently. Plain for anything under a roof, inside, or that you will paint and keep dry. The price difference is real and so is the performance difference. Do not put plain plywood on a shed floor and do not put treated plywood in your kitchen.

Every cut edge of treated plywood should get end-cut preservative, same as treated lumber. Plywood edges are the most absorbent part of the panel and the first place delamination starts.

The honest limitation

Project panels are not a substitute for sheet goods. If you are sheathing a shed, roofing anything, or building cabinets, you want full 4x8 sheets and you want them from Siletz River Lumber, who will also get you the grade you actually need. Buying twelve project panels to avoid a lumberyard trip costs more and gives you a wall full of seams.

Check panel stock before you drive over. They move in ones and twos most weeks and by the pallet the week after a windstorm.
