Why a 2x4 Is Not Two Inches by Four Inches

Nominal versus actual, with a table you can hold up against a tape, and why the whole thing is not a scam.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

A 2x4 measures 1.5 by 3.5 inches. A 2x6 measures 1.5 by 5.5. A 4x4 measures 3.5 by 3.5. People discover this at the worst possible moment, usually with a saw in one hand and a plan in the other, and they are reasonably annoyed about it.

Where the missing half inch went

The nominal size is the rough size of the board when it comes off the saw at the mill, green and unfinished. It then gets dried, which shrinks it, and planed on all four faces, which removes more. What arrives at the rack is the finished, surfaced dimension. The name stuck to the rough size and the board kept shrinking.

It is standardized, not arbitrary. Every 2x4 in the country is 1.5 by 3.5, which means framing details, hangers, connectors, insulation batts and drywall spacing all work out. If mills had kept full dimensions, everything would cost more and nothing would be interchangeable.

Note the pattern breaks at 8 inches. Boards up to 6 inches nominal lose half an inch. Boards 8 inches and wider lose three quarters. That inconsistency catches people who thought they had the rule figured out.

Where it actually bites

  • Two 2x4s laid flat are 3 inches, not 4. Stacking to fill a gap almost never works out to a round number.
  • A wall framed with 2x4s is 3.5 inches thick, plus drywall or sheathing. Plan openings from the actual number.
  • A 4x4 post is 3.5 inches, which matters when you buy a post cap or a bracket. The hardware is sized to the actual dimension and labeled with the nominal one.
  • Cedar fence pickets are usually 5.5 inches wide. Eighteen of them with a small gap covers roughly 8 feet. That is the arithmetic behind our fence section table.
  • Treated lumber is wetter and may measure slightly over, then shrink to standard as it dries. That is normal.

Rough sawn is different

Lumber sold rough sawn, often locally milled, actually is close to its nominal size, because it has not been planed. A rough 2x6 might genuinely measure 2 by 6, or 1.875 by 5.75 depending on the mill. It is not interchangeable with surfaced lumber and you cannot mix them in the same wall without shimming.

People around here get rough sawn fir from small mills and it is good material. Just measure it yourself rather than assuming.

Board feet, while we are here

Lumber is sometimes priced by the board foot, which is 144 cubic inches, calculated from the nominal dimensions, not the actual. Thickness in inches times width in inches times length in feet, divided by 12. A 2x6x8 is 2 times 6 times 8 divided by 12, which is 8 board feet.

You will not need this at our counter, because everything on our racks is priced by the piece. You will need it if you call Siletz River Lumber for a real order.

The practical habit

Measure the board, not the label. Especially on anything old you are matching, because dimensional standards have changed over the decades and a 2x4 out of a house built a long time ago might genuinely measure 1.625 by 3.625. Matching new lumber to old framing usually requires a shim and it is better to find that out with a tape than with a saw.
