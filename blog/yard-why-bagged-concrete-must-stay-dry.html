Why We Keep the Concrete Up Off the Ground

A paper bag on a damp slab absorbs moisture from underneath and turns into a rock. Here is how to store yours so it does not.

3 minute read  ·  Twisted Roots Merc, Siletz

Every bag in our dry shed sits on a pallet, under a roof, with air moving underneath it. That is not tidiness. Bagged cement products are hygroscopic, meaning they pull moisture out of the air, and paper bags are not a vapor barrier. Set a bag of concrete directly on a garage slab on this coast and in a few months you will have a very heavy, very useless brick.

How a bag goes bad

Two ways. The obvious one is liquid water: a leak, a splash, rain blowing sideways under an eave. The bag gets wet, the cement hydrates in place, and you get lumps that will never break up.

The subtler one is vapor. Concrete slabs wick ground moisture upward continuously. A paper bag sitting on that slab is a wick with a sponge in it. The bottom two inches harden first and people do not notice until they cut the bag open and find a crust. That crust is cement that has already reacted. It contributes nothing to strength and it will not mix in.

Storing your leftovers

  1. Get it off the floor. Two scraps of 2x4 and a project panel is a pallet. That is enough.
  2. Slide each bag into a contractor bag and tape it shut, or dump the bag into a five gallon bucket with a lid.
  3. Keep it away from exterior walls, which are the coldest surfaces and where condensation forms.
  4. Label the bucket. Fast-set, standard mix, sand mix and mortar all look identical once they are out of the bag and you will not remember.

The bucket method is the one we actually recommend. A 60 lb bag fits in a five gallon bucket with room to spare, it stacks, it does not tear, and rodents leave it alone.

How to tell if a bag is still good

Pick it up and drop it flat from about a foot. A good bag thumps and shifts like a sandbag. A bad one lands solid, like a paver. Then squeeze it in a few places. Lumps that break under thumb pressure are fine, they are just compacted powder. Lumps that do not break are cured cement and they are gone.

If the bag is mostly good with a hard crust on one face, you can cut it open, break out the hard part, and use the rest. Do not try to crush the hard lumps and mix them in. They are aggregate now, and weak aggregate at that.

What this means for buying

Buy what the job needs plus a bag, not a pallet you will store for a year in a damp shed. That is genuinely the argument for a small yard like ours existing. If you need forty bags, Siletz River Lumber and Toledo Do it Best will get you a pallet and you should use it inside of a month. If you need three bags because you underestimated a post hole, drive over and we will load them.

Everything in the shed stays under cover and off the deck, and we rotate it. If you get a bag from us with a hard corner in it, bring it back. See what is stocked, or come find us and Eric will walk you out back.
