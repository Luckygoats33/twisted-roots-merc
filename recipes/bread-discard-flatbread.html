Bread & Sourdough

Skillet Flatbread From Discard

A firm, oiled dough from the starter you were about to throw away, cooked dry in a hot pan.

Six flatbreads, about 70 g each
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 30 minutes
Start to finish

Discard has no lift left in it and plenty of flavour, which makes it exactly right for something cooked flat and fast. This dough is firm on purpose. A soft flatbread dough sticks to the pin, rolls out unevenly, and puffs in patches; a firm one rolls thin, releases cleanly, and balloons in the pan. They cook in a dry skillet in about three minutes and they are best within about ten of that, so make them while whatever they are going with is nearly ready rather than in advance.

What you need

Dough

  • 200 g sourdough discard, straight from the fridge (about 3/4 cup)
  • 170 g all-purpose flour (about 1 1/3 cups)
  • 40 g water (about 3 tbsp)
  • 25 g olive oil (about 2 tbsp)
  • 5 g fine sea salt (about 1 tsp)
  • Extra flour for rolling, about 3 tbsp

To finish

  • 2 tbsp melted butter or olive oil
  • A pinch of flaky salt
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley or chives, optional

How

  1. Mix everything to a stiff, slightly tacky dough and knead it on the counter for three minutes until smooth. Total flour comes to 270 g and total water to 140 g, which is near 52% — firm, and deliberately so.

  2. Cover and rest one hour at room temperature. The rest is not for rising, it is for relaxing the gluten so the dough will roll thin instead of springing back.

  3. Divide into six pieces of about 70 g, roll each into a ball, and rest them another 10 minutes under a cloth.

  4. Heat a cast iron skillet or a heavy pan over medium-high heat, dry, for a good five minutes. It is hot enough when a drop of water skates across it and vanishes rather than sitting and hissing. If you would rather not guess, an instant-read laid flat on the surface should read around 205°C.

  5. Roll one ball at a time on a lightly floured counter to about 2 mm, roughly the size of a side plate. Roll the next one while the first cooks.

  6. Lay it in the dry pan. It is ready to turn when the surface goes from wet-looking to matte, bubbles rise across it, and the underside is spotted brown — about 60 to 90 seconds.

  7. Turn it and cook the second side until it puffs, or at least blisters, and takes on its own brown spots, another 45 to 60 seconds. Press the edge lightly with a folded towel to encourage the puff.

  8. Stack them in a clean tea towel as they come off the pan. The trapped steam is what keeps them pliable; left on a rack they turn into crackers within minutes.

  9. Brush with melted butter or oil, scatter flaky salt and herbs, and serve warm.

  10. If they come out dry and leathery, the pan was not hot enough and they cooked too long. If they scorch before the middle sets, it was too hot. The window is narrower than it sounds and the second one is always better than the first.

Notes

  • Cold discard from a well-fed starter is mild. Discard that has been in the fridge a fortnight is sharp and makes a more interesting flatbread.
  • Half a teaspoon of baking powder in the dough gives a softer, more tender result if your discard is very old and flat.

Keeping it

They keep a day in a bag and reheat for 30 seconds a side in a dry pan until soft and flexible again.
