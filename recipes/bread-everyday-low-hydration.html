Bread & Sourdough

The Everyday 65% Loaf

A firm, easy-handling sourdough with a tight even crumb, for people who want bread rather than a project.

One loaf, about 950 g baked
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
About 20 hours
Start to finish

There is a quiet assumption that wetter is better, and for a certain kind of loaf it is. But a sixty-five percent dough is easier to shape, holds its form without a banneton, cuts into slices that hold butter instead of dropping it through a hole, and makes better toast. It is also the hydration most working bakeries used for most of history. This one is deliberately plain: one flour, one fold schedule, no inclusions, and a crumb that is even enough to build a sandwich on without an engineering degree.

What you need

Levain

  • 20 g mature starter
  • 60 g bread flour (about 1/2 cup)
  • 60 g water (about 1/4 cup)

Dough

  • 600 g bread flour (about 4 3/4 cups)
  • 365 g water (about 1 1/2 cups)
  • 12 g fine sea salt (about 2 tsp)
  • 140 g levain, all of it

How

  1. Build the levain and use it at peak.

  2. Mix everything together, including salt, and knead on the counter for six to eight minutes. At this hydration kneading is pleasant rather than a fight, and the dough should end up smooth, firm and springing back when pressed.

  3. Bulk with two folds in the first two hours, then leave it alone. Totals are 670 g flour and 435 g water, so 65% hydration, with salt at 1.8%.

  4. A stiffer dough rises more slowly and shows it less obviously. It is done when it has grown by about two-thirds and a poke leaves a dent that fills in halfway, five to seven hours warm.

  5. Preshape, rest 20 minutes, shape into a tight round or a batard. This dough holds a shape well enough to proof on a floured tray under a cloth if you do not own a banneton.

  6. Refrigerate 10 to 14 hours, or proof at room temperature for 90 minutes and bake the same day. Both work; the cold version tastes better and scores more cleanly.

  7. Heat a Dutch oven at 500°F (260°C) for 45 minutes. Score a cross or three parallel cuts a little deeper than you would in a wet dough — stiff dough resists opening.

  8. Bake covered 22 minutes at 480°F (250°C), then uncovered 22 to 26 minutes at 450°F (230°C), until the crust is deep brown and the loaf reads 205°F (96°C) inside.

  9. Cool 90 minutes.

  10. If the crumb is dense and heavy rather than tight and even, that is under-fermentation rather than hydration. A stiff dough needs longer, not more water.

Notes

  • This is the dough to hand somebody who is intimidated by wet sourdough. It behaves like the bread they have seen made.
  • It also takes an egg wash and a seeded crust better than a slack dough does, since the surface stays where you put it.

Keeping it

Four days on the counter, cut side down. Low hydration loaves stale more slowly than high ones at the same weight.
