Bread & Sourdough

Pan Focaccia

Eighty percent hydration, a cold night in the fridge, and more olive oil than feels reasonable.

One 9 x 13 inch pan, 12 pieces
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
About 24 hours
Start to finish

Focaccia is the most forgiving bread there is, because you never shape it and nobody expects an even crumb. What it does need is enough oil in the pan to effectively shallow-fry the base, and a dough slack enough to spread into the corners under its own weight. Eighty percent hydration handles that. The overnight cold rest is not strictly required, but it turns a decent flatbread into something with actual flavour, and it means the fiddly part happens the night before rather than an hour before people arrive.

What you need

Levain

  • 20 g mature starter
  • 80 g bread flour (about 2/3 cup)
  • 80 g water (about 1/3 cup)

Dough

  • 610 g bread flour (about 5 cups)
  • 470 g water (about 2 cups)
  • 14 g fine sea salt (about 2 1/2 tsp)
  • 60 g olive oil, for the dough (about 1/4 cup)
  • 180 g levain, all of it

Pan and top

  • 40 g olive oil for the pan (about 3 tbsp)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil for the dimples
  • 1 tsp flaky salt
  • 1 tbsp rosemary needles, or a handful of halved cherry tomatoes

How

  1. Build the levain to peak.

  2. Mix flour and water and rest 30 minutes, then squeeze in the levain, salt and the 60 g of oil. It will be sloppy and will look like it will never hold together. Keep folding it in the bowl for two minutes and it will.

  3. Four sets of folds in the bowl at 30 minute intervals, using a wet hand. Totals are 700 g flour and 560 g water, so 80% hydration, with salt at 2%.

  4. After the last fold, cover the bowl and refrigerate 12 to 18 hours. The dough should roughly double and be full of large bubbles by morning.

  5. Pour the 40 g of oil into a 9 x 13 inch pan and spread it up the sides. Tip the cold dough in, turn it once to coat, and leave it 2 to 3 hours to warm and relax.

  6. Stretch it gently toward the corners with flat hands. It will pull back. Wait 20 minutes and stretch again — two or three rounds of that gets it to the edges without tearing, and forcing it in one go tears the gluten and gives you a flat corner.

  7. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C). Oil your fingers and press dimples right down to the bottom of the pan, all over. Scatter the flaky salt and rosemary and drizzle the last of the oil into the hollows.

  8. Bake 22 to 28 minutes, until the top is deep gold, the edges are crisp and pulling away from the pan, and the base is browned and crackling when you lift a corner with a spatula. An instant-read in the middle reads 205°F (96°C).

  9. Slide it straight out onto a rack. Left in the pan it steams and the fried base goes soft, which loses the best part of it.

  10. If the base is pale and greasy rather than crisp, the pan was underheated or there was not enough oil. Use metal, not glass, and do not economise on the oil.

Notes

  • For a yeasted same-day version, use 5 g instant yeast in place of the levain and give it four hours warm instead of the overnight fridge.
  • Anything wet on top — tomato, onion, olives — should be pressed into the dimples so it does not slide off, and it will add a few minutes to the bake.

Keeping it

Best the day it is baked. Day two, split it and use it as sandwich bread, or reheat at 350°F (175°C) for six minutes until the base crisps again.
