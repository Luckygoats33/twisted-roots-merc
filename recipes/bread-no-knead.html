Bread & Sourdough

No-Knead Bread

One gram of yeast, eighteen hours, no work at all.

One round loaf, about 620 g baked
Makes
10 minutes
Hands on
18 to 20 hours
Start to finish

The reason this works is that gluten will organise itself given enough time and enough water. Kneading is just a way of hurrying that along. Leave a slack dough alone overnight with a trace of yeast and it develops the same structure while you sleep, and the long ferment gives it more flavour than a fast kneaded loaf would have. It is the least demanding bread in this collection and the most reliable one to hand somebody who says they cannot bake. Eighty-two percent hydration, which is why you never touch it with your hands until the end.

What you need

Dough

  • 400 g bread flour (about 3 1/4 cups)
  • 330 g cool water (about 1 1/3 cups)
  • 8 g fine sea salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 1 g instant dried yeast (about 1/4 tsp)
  • Semolina or extra flour for the towel, about 2 tbsp

How

  1. Stir the flour, salt and yeast together dry, then add the water and mix with a spoon or a hand for 30 seconds until there is no dry flour left. It will be a shaggy, wet, unpromising mess. Do not knead it and do not tidy it up.

  2. Cover the bowl and leave it at cool room temperature, around 20°C, for 16 to 18 hours. It is ready when the surface is covered in bubbles, the dough has more than doubled, and it smells yeasty and faintly of beer.

  3. Flour the counter well and scrape the dough out. Fold it over on itself twice with a scraper — that is the entire amount of handling it gets. Rest it 15 minutes under the bowl.

  4. Shape it roughly into a ball by tucking the edges under, and set it seam side down on a well-floured tea towel. Cover with a second towel and prove for two hours at room temperature, until it has spread and puffed and a finger pressed into it leaves a mark that fills in slowly.

  5. Half an hour before the end, put a Dutch oven with its lid on into the oven and heat to 475°F (245°C). Let it sit at temperature at least 30 minutes.

  6. Tip the dough into the hot pot seam side up — it will look wrong and it does not matter. Lid on, bake 30 minutes.

  7. Uncover and bake 15 to 20 minutes more, until the crust is deep brown all over and an instant-read through the base reads 208°F (98°C). Pale bread is underbaked bread; go darker than feels comfortable.

  8. Turn it out and cool on a rack for an hour at minimum. In a damp house this loaf softens quickly once cooled, so if the crust matters to you, eat it the first day.

  9. If the dough is soupy and will not hold any shape at all, the flour was thirstier than expected or the room was too warm and it over-fermented. Next time use 310 g water and check it at 14 hours.

Notes

  • One gram of yeast is roughly a quarter teaspoon and it really is that little. More yeast makes it faster and blander.
  • A cast iron pot, an enamelled one, or a heavy lidded casserole all work. The lid is doing the job, by trapping steam.

Keeping it

Two days in a paper bag. Plastic keeps it soft but turns the crust leathery by morning.
