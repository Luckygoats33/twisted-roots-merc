Bread & Sourdough

Sourdough Sandwich Loaf

A soft square-shouldered pan loaf with milk, butter and honey, built for slicing.

One 9 x 5 inch loaf, about 900 g baked
Makes
40 minutes
Hands on
About 20 hours
Start to finish

A round hearth loaf makes a bad sandwich. The slices are different sizes, the crust fights the filling, and half the loaf is the wrong shape for a lunchbox. This is the other kind of bread: milk and butter for softness, a little honey for colour and keeping quality, and a tin to force it into square slices. It is still naturally leavened, so it has flavour, but the enrichment tames the sourness. This is what the sandwiches at the counter would be on if they were not on the hearth loaf.

What you need

Levain

  • 20 g mature starter
  • 60 g bread flour (about 1/2 cup)
  • 60 g water (about 1/4 cup)

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 320 g whole milk, cool (about 1 1/3 cups)
  • 40 g water (about 3 tbsp)
  • 30 g unsalted butter, soft (about 2 tbsp)
  • 25 g honey (about 1 tbsp)
  • 11 g fine sea salt (about 2 tsp)
  • 140 g levain, all of it

How

  1. Build the levain and use it at peak. Because this dough is enriched and slow, a sluggish levain shows up badly here.

  2. Mix flour, milk, water and honey to a shaggy mass and rest 30 minutes. Add levain and salt and work through, then rest 15 minutes.

  3. Add the soft butter in three lots, squeezing each in until the dough stops looking greasy and comes back together. It will fall apart and reassemble each time; that is what butter does to gluten and it is temporary.

  4. Knead or slap and fold for six to eight minutes until the dough is smooth, glossy and pulls cleanly off the counter. This one wants real development — a pan loaf with weak gluten collapses into a wedge.

  5. Bulk with two folds in the first hour, then leave it. Counting the milk as water, hydration is about 65% — the true figure is nearer 58% since milk is roughly 87% water, and that is close enough to plan by. It is done when risen by two-thirds and domed, four to six hours warm.

  6. Degas gently, shape into a tight cylinder the length of the tin, and set it seam down in a buttered 9 x 5 inch pan.

  7. Cover and refrigerate overnight, 10 to 14 hours. Take it out in the morning and let it finish at room temperature until the crown stands about 2 cm above the rim and a floured finger leaves a dent that fills slowly. In a cold kitchen that final stretch takes two hours.

  8. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Brush the top with milk for a soft crust, or with egg for a shiny dark one.

  9. Bake 40 to 48 minutes, until the top is deep gold, the sides have set firm enough that the loaf releases from the tin without tearing, and an instant-read in the centre reads 200°F (93°C). Tent with foil at the 30 minute mark if it is colouring fast.

  10. Turn out immediately onto a rack. Leaving it in the hot tin steams the sides soft and then soggy.

  11. Cool fully, at least three hours, before slicing. Warm enriched bread tears rather than slices, and this loaf is worth a bread knife and some patience.

Notes

  • For a flat top for square sandwiches, use a lidded Pullman tin and fill it no more than two-thirds full.
  • Butter goes in after the gluten is partly built, never at the start, or you are kneading a paste that will not develop.

Keeping it

Four days in a bag on the counter — the honey and butter keep it soft far longer than a lean loaf. Freezes well sliced.
