Bread & Sourdough

Walnut Levain

Toasted walnuts, a little honey, three flours, and a crumb that comes out lilac-grey and is supposed to.

Two loaves, about 620 g each baked
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
About 20 hours
Start to finish

Walnut bread turns purple-grey inside and every time somebody bakes it for the first time they assume something has gone wrong. Nothing has. Walnut skins are full of tannins, they leach into an alkaline wheat dough, and the colour is the proof the nuts were in there long enough to flavour it. Toast them first, which drives off the raw bitterness, and use a little honey and rye to meet the tannin halfway. This is the loaf to have in the house when there is good cheese, and it is worth baking for that reason alone.

What you need

Levain

  • 20 g mature starter
  • 70 g bread flour (about 1/2 cup plus 1 tbsp)
  • 70 g water (about 1/4 cup plus 1 tbsp)

Dough

  • 450 g bread flour (about 3 2/3 cups)
  • 100 g whole wheat flour (about 3/4 cup)
  • 50 g whole rye flour (about 1/3 cup)
  • 420 g water (about 1 3/4 cups)
  • 12 g fine sea salt (about 2 tsp)
  • 20 g honey (about 1 tbsp)
  • 160 g levain, all of it
  • 160 g walnut halves (about 1 1/2 cups)

How

  1. Toast the walnuts at 350°F (175°C) for 8 to 10 minutes, until they smell nutty and the skins have darkened and started to flake. Cool them completely, then rub off the loose skins in a towel and break them into rough halves.

  2. Build the levain to peak.

  3. Mix the flours with the water and honey, rest 45 minutes, then work in the levain and salt.

  4. Two sets of folds 40 minutes apart, then laminate the walnuts in: stretch the dough out on a wet counter into a rectangle, scatter the nuts, roll it up, and fold it twice more over the following hour.

  5. Bulk until the dough is up by half and feels alive rather than slack, four to five hours. Totals are 680 g flour and 500 g water, so about 73% hydration, with salt at 1.8%.

  6. Divide, preshape, rest 25 minutes, shape into rounds. Nuts tear a dough, so shape with a firm hand and expect the surface to be less than perfect.

  7. Seam up in floured bannetons, refrigerate 10 to 14 hours.

  8. Heat a Dutch oven at 500°F (260°C) for 45 minutes. Score cold in a cross or a simple square, bake covered 20 minutes at 475°F (245°C), then uncovered 20 to 24 minutes at 450°F (230°C), until the crust is dark brown and an instant-read reads 205°F (96°C).

  9. Any walnut sticking out of the crust will char. Pick the worst offenders out before it goes in the pot, or accept a few bitter bits.

  10. Cool two hours. The colour deepens as it cools and it will be more purple in the morning than it was at noon.

Notes

  • Untoasted walnuts make a bitter loaf. This is the step to not skip.
  • Swap 60 g of the walnuts for dried cranberries or chopped dried figs if you want it sweeter — add them at the same stage.

Keeping it

Three days wrapped. The nuts keep the crumb moist longer than a plain loaf of the same hydration would stay.
