Breakfast & The Griddle

Buttermilk Pancakes

Lumpy batter, rested twenty minutes, cooked on a griddle at a temperature you actually measured.

About 14 pancakes, 12 cm across
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
45 minutes
Start to finish

Two things separate a good pancake from a rubber disc, and neither is the recipe. The first is stopping the mixing while lumps remain. Flour and liquid build gluten the moment they meet, and every extra stroke past just-combined makes the finished pancake tougher and flatter. The second is griddle temperature, which is worth actually measuring once so you know what medium means on your own stove. Too cool and the pancake dries out before it colours; too hot and it is dark outside and raw in the middle. There is also a rest, which does more than people expect.

What you need

Dry

  • 300 g all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt

Wet

  • 500 ml buttermilk, well shaken
  • 2 large eggs
  • 60 g butter, melted and slightly cooled
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional

For the griddle

  • 2 tablespoons butter or neutral oil
  • Maple syrup and butter, to taste, for serving

How

  1. Whisk the dry ingredients together thoroughly. Baking soda in particular clumps and, undistributed, gives you bitter metallic pockets in an otherwise fine pancake.

  2. Whisk the buttermilk and eggs together in a second bowl, then whisk in the melted butter. If the buttermilk is straight from the cold, the butter will seize into small flecks. That is cosmetic and harmless.

  3. Pour the wet into the dry and fold with a spatula about ten strokes, stopping while the batter is still lumpy with visible streaks of dry flour the size of a grain of rice. It will look wrong. Trust it.

  4. Let the batter rest twenty minutes at room temperature. The flour hydrates, the lumps dissolve on their own, the gluten you did build relaxes, and the baking soda gets a head start on the buttermilk. Rested batter makes taller, more tender pancakes than fresh batter, which is the closest thing to a free improvement in this recipe.

  5. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet to 375°F (190°C). Without a surface thermometer, use the water test: flick a few drops on and they should form beads that skate across the surface and evaporate in two or three seconds. If they vanish instantly with a crack, it is too hot. If they just sit and steam, too cool.

  6. Grease lightly and wipe most of it off with a paper towel. A visible film of fat fries the outside into a crisp mottled skin instead of an even brown.

  7. Ladle about 60 ml per pancake. Cook until bubbles rise and break across the whole surface, not just the rim, and the edges look dry and matte rather than glossy — around two minutes. Bubbles that form and immediately close mean the batter is too thick; add a splash of buttermilk.

  8. Flip once. The second side takes about half as long and is done when the pancake springs back when pressed lightly in the centre and no longer feels dense. Flipping more than once knocks the air out and gives you the flat ones nobody picks off the stack.

  9. Hold finished pancakes in a single layer on a rack in a 200°F (93°C) oven. Stacking them on a plate traps steam and softens the surface within minutes.

Notes

  • No buttermilk: stir 1 tablespoon of vinegar or lemon juice into 500 ml of milk and let it stand five minutes. It curdles slightly and is a fair substitute for the acid, though not for the thickness. Cut the milk by 60 ml to compensate.

  • The soda and powder do different jobs. Soda reacts with the buttermilk immediately for lift and browning; powder is double-acting and holds some of its rise for the heat of the griddle. Dropping either changes the pancake.

  • Blueberries and chocolate: scatter them onto the wet side after ladling, rather than stirring them into the batter. Stirred in, they streak the batter and sink to the bottom of the bowl.

Keeping it

Batter holds cold for a day, though it loses lift after the first few hours as the soda spends itself. Cooked pancakes freeze well: cool completely, stack with parchment between them, and reheat straight from frozen in a toaster or a 350°F (175°C) oven until hot through and no longer bendy in the middle.
