Breakfast & The Griddle

Corned Beef Hash from Scratch

Real brisket, potatoes with edges, and a crust on the bottom. Nothing here comes out of a tin.

6 servings
Makes
40 minutes
Hands on
3 hours 30 minutes with the brisket, 40 minutes with leftovers
Start to finish

Hash out of a tin is a paste. Hash made properly is chunks of braised brisket and cubed potato pressed into a hot pan and left there until a crust forms, then broken up and pressed again. The two changes that matter most are cutting the meat in pieces you have to chew rather than shredding it to threads, and parboiling the potatoes so the interiors are already tender and the pan only has to handle browning. If you are starting from a leftover corned brisket, skip straight to the potatoes and you have breakfast in forty minutes.

What you need

The brisket, if you are cooking one

  • 1.4 kg corned beef brisket with its spice packet
  • 1 onion, halved
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Water to cover, about 3 litres

Hash

  • 900 g russet or Yukon gold potatoes, cut in 1.5 cm cubes
  • 1 tablespoon fine salt, for the potato water
  • 700 g cooked corned beef, cut in 1.5 cm cubes
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 3 tablespoons butter or beef fat
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, to taste
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley

To finish

  • 6 eggs, fried or poached
  • A pinch of flaky salt

How

  1. If you are starting with a raw brisket, put it in a pot with the onion, bay and the spice packet, cover with cold water, and bring it to a bare simmer — surface barely moving, a bubble every second or two. A rolling boil squeezes the muscle fibres tight and gives you stringy dry meat.

  2. Hold it there until a fork slides into the thickest part and turns with no resistance, roughly three hours for a piece that size. Doneness in a braise is about texture, not temperature; a tough brisket is undercooked, not overcooked. Cool it in its liquid, then cut it in cubes against the grain.

  3. Put the potato cubes in cold salted water, bring to a boil, and cook until the point of a knife goes into a cube with slight resistance still at the centre, about six minutes from the boil. Drain and spread on a tray to steam dry for five minutes. Wet potatoes will not brown.

  4. Heat the oil in a large heavy skillet over medium-high. Add the onion and cook until soft and beginning to brown at the edges. Scoop it out and set it aside.

  5. Add the butter to the same pan and, once it foams, tip in the potatoes in an even layer. Press them down and then leave them completely alone for four or five minutes, until the undersides have set into a brown crust and release from the pan without tearing.

  6. Turn the potatoes over in sections, add the beef and the cooked onion, season with the pepper and salt, and press the whole thing flat again. Leave it alone once more. What you are building is a crust on the bottom that you fold up into the rest.

  7. Repeat the press-and-wait two or three more times, over about ten minutes, until there are dark crisp edges through the hash and the whole pan smells of browned beef rather than boiled. Stir in the parsley.

  8. Serve with a fried or poached egg on each portion, so the yolk runs into the hash and does the job a sauce would otherwise have to do.

Notes

  • It turned to mash: the potatoes were boiled all the way through before they hit the pan, or you stirred instead of pressing. Parboil to resistance, not tenderness.

  • Nonstick is the wrong pan. You want the fond — the browned layer that sticks — and a nonstick surface will not build one. Cast iron or carbon steel.

  • Leftover roast beef, braised chuck or a smoked brisket end all work in place of corned beef. Add a teaspoon of pickle brine or vinegar at the end to stand in for the missing tang of the cure.

Keeping it

The cooked brisket holds cold in its liquid for five days and freezes for three months, and honestly the meat is better on the second day. Assembled hash holds cold for three days and reheats well in a hot skillet, which crisps it further. Do not reheat it in a microwave unless you have made peace with steamed potato.
