Breakfast & The Griddle

Breakfast for a Crew of Ten

Two sheet pans, one skillet, one timeline. Everything lands hot at the same moment, which is the only hard part.

10 hungry people
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 30 minutes
Start to finish

Cooking breakfast for ten is not a recipe problem, it is a scheduling problem. Nobody has ten burners, and a fried egg made at the start is cold and leathery by the time the tenth one is done. So the oven does the volume work — bacon, sausage and potatoes all cook flat on sheet pans without supervision — and the only thing that gets made to order is the eggs, in two big pans, at the very end. What follows is a timeline as much as a recipe. Read it through once before you start, because the order is doing most of the work.

What you need

Sheet pan one, potatoes

  • 2.5 kg russet or Yukon gold potatoes, cut in 2 cm dice
  • 80 ml neutral oil
  • 2 large onions, cut in wedges
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 tablespoon fine salt
  • 2 teaspoons black pepper

Sheet pan two, meat

  • 1 kg thick-cut bacon, about 20 slices
  • 900 g breakfast sausage links, about 20

Eggs

  • 24 large eggs
  • 125 ml half and half
  • 2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 60 g butter

The rest of the table

  • 2 loaves bread, sliced, for toast
  • 225 g butter, softened
  • 300 g grated cheddar
  • 2 litres coffee, brewed
  • Hot sauce and jam, to taste

How

  1. Ninety minutes out: heat the oven to 425°F (220°C) with racks in the upper and lower thirds. Cut the potatoes and put them in cold water so they do not brown while you deal with everything else.

  2. Seventy-five minutes out: drain the potatoes and dry them on a towel — genuinely dry, because wet potatoes steam instead of browning. Toss with the oil, paprika, salt and pepper and spread over two sheet pans in a single layer. Two pans, not one heaped one. Roast on the lower rack.

  3. Sixty minutes out: lay the bacon flat on a rimmed sheet pan and put it on the upper rack. Cook until the fat has rendered translucent and the slices are stiff enough to hold their shape on tongs, about twenty minutes. They crisp further as they cool. Drain on paper and hold them on a rack.

  4. Forty-five minutes out: turn the potatoes with a spatula. They should be browning on the underside and releasing from the pan. Add the onion wedges now, so they soften and colour without burning in the time remaining.

  5. Forty minutes out: cook the sausage links on a fresh sheet pan on the upper rack, turning once, until browned all over. These are ground meat: check the thickest link with an instant-read thermometer and pull them at 160°F (71°C). Colour is not a reliable indicator with sausage.

  6. Twenty minutes out: the potatoes are done when the edges are dark and crisp and a cube crushes easily under a spatula. Drop the oven to 200°F (93°C) and hold everything in there, meat on racks so it does not sit in its own fat, potatoes spread out so they do not steam.

  7. Ten minutes out: start toast. Bread laid flat on a sheet pan under a broiler, watched constantly and turned once, does ten slices at a time and is faster than any toaster. Pull each side when it is evenly golden — the gap between golden and black under a broiler is about twenty seconds.

  8. Five minutes out: beat the eggs with the half and half, salt and pepper. Split between two wide skillets over medium-low with the butter. Stir constantly and slowly, and pull them off the heat while they still look glossy and slightly wet in the seams. They finish in the pan and again in the serving dish. Fold in the cheddar off the heat.

  9. Serve family style from the pans, with everything on the table at once. Coffee should have been made twenty minutes ago and be sitting in a thermal carafe, not on a hot plate turning bitter.

Notes

  • The eggs are the only thing you cannot make early, so build everything else around them. Twenty-four eggs across two pans takes about six minutes and needs your full attention.

  • Quantities per head: two eggs, two slices of bacon, two sausage links, 250 g of potato, two slices of toast. For a working crew going out into weather, add half again on the potatoes.

  • Holding at 200°F (93°C) works for about forty minutes for potatoes and meat. Past that the potatoes go leathery. Nothing holds eggs.

Keeping it

Leftover potatoes and meat hold cold for four days and make hash or burrito filling. Leftover scrambled eggs are best written off. If you know in advance that ten will become six, cook the full sheet pans anyway — roasted potatoes and cooked bacon are far more useful cold than half a pan of anything else.
