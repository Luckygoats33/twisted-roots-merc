Breakfast & The Griddle

Breakfast Burritos Built to Freeze

A dozen at a time, wrapped tight, reheated in four minutes. The trick is what you leave out.

12 burritos
Makes
1 hour
Hands on
1 hour 45 minutes, plus cooling
Start to finish

Almost every ingredient that makes a fresh burrito good makes a frozen one bad. Salsa, sour cream, tomato and fresh avocado all hold water, and water turns to ice crystals that shred the tortilla from the inside and leave you with a wet grey tube. What survives freezing is starch, fat, protein and cheese, so this build is potatoes, eggs cooked slightly under, sausage, beans and a lot of cheddar, with the wet things served alongside on the day. Wrap them cold, freeze them flat, and reheat them from frozen. A dozen of these in the freezer covers two weeks of mornings.

What you need

Potatoes

  • 900 g russet potatoes, cut in 1 cm dice
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 2 teaspoons smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Filling

  • 700 g bulk breakfast sausage or chorizo
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 16 large eggs
  • 60 ml half and half
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 1 can black beans, about 400 g, drained and rinsed
  • 450 g sharp cheddar or pepper jack, grated

Wrapping

  • 12 flour tortillas, 25 cm, at room temperature
  • 12 sheets parchment, 30 cm square
  • 1 roll aluminium foil

How

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the potatoes with the oil, paprika, salt and pepper, spread them on a sheet pan in one layer, and roast, turning once, until the edges are dark brown and crisp and a cube crushes with no resistance under a spatula, about thirty minutes. Let them cool completely.

  2. Brown the sausage in a wide skillet over medium-high, breaking it up, until no pink remains and the crumbles are firm. Ground meat is not judged by colour: check the thickest crumble with an instant-read thermometer and take it to 160°F (71°C). Scoop it out, leaving the fat.

  3. Cook the onion and pepper in the sausage fat until soft and browning at the edges and any liquid they released has cooked away. Wet vegetables are the second most common cause of a soggy frozen burrito, after salsa. Combine with the sausage and cool.

  4. Beat the eggs with the half and half and salt. Scramble them in the butter over low heat, stirring constantly, and stop while they are still noticeably looser and wetter than you would serve them — they get a second cooking in the microwave later and will finish then. Spread them on a tray to cool fast.

  5. Cool every component to cold before you build. Warm filling steams the tortilla, and a steamed tortilla freezes into something that tears the moment you unwrap it. This is the step people skip and regret.

  6. Warm each tortilla for a few seconds on a dry griddle until it is pliable and no longer stiff. A cold tortilla splits along the fold every time.

  7. Build in a line down the lower third: cheese first as a moisture barrier against the tortilla, then potatoes, sausage mix, beans, eggs, and a last pinch of cheese. About 250 g of filling per burrito. Overfilling is why burritos burst.

  8. Fold the sides in first, then roll from the bottom, pulling back against the filling with your fingertips as you go to compact it. A loose burrito has air pockets, and air pockets are where freezer burn starts.

  9. Wrap each one in parchment, then in foil, and label the outside. Freeze them in a single layer on a tray until firm before stacking, so they hold their shape.

  10. To reheat from frozen: strip off the foil and parchment, wrap in a damp paper towel, and microwave for about two minutes, then turn it over and go another one to two minutes until the centre is steaming hot the whole way through and no cold spot remains at the middle. If you want the outside crisp, finish it in a dry skillet over medium until the tortilla is blistered and golden.

Notes

  • Do not put salsa inside. Serve it on the side. The same goes for sour cream, fresh tomato, lettuce and avocado — all of them are mostly water and all of them ruin the tortilla in the freezer.

  • Vegetarian version: replace the sausage with 700 g of mushrooms cooked hard until browned and dry, plus a second can of beans. Everything else is the same.

  • The cheese-first rule matters. A layer of cheese against the tortilla melts into a seal and keeps the potato and egg moisture from soaking into the wrap.

Keeping it

Three months in the freezer wrapped in parchment and foil, and they are noticeably better in the first month. They will keep cold for four days unfrozen. Do not thaw on the counter — go straight from freezer to heat, both for texture and because a burrito full of egg and meat sitting at room temperature for hours is exactly the situation food safety rules exist for.
