Breakfast & The Griddle

Granola That Actually Clusters

Low oven, egg white, and no stirring. Every rule here exists to protect the clumps.

About 1.2 kg
Makes
15 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes, plus cooling
Start to finish

Loose granola and clustered granola are made from identical ingredients and separated entirely by process. Clusters form when the sugar and binder in the mixture melt, flow between the oats, and then set hard as everything cools — which means the mixture has to be pressed into contact, baked low enough that the sugar never scorches, and then left completely undisturbed until it is cold. Stirring halfway through, which nearly every recipe tells you to do, is exactly the thing that prevents clusters. A whipped egg white is the reliable binder; it sets into a brittle protein scaffold around the oats.

What you need

Dry

  • 500 g rolled oats
  • 150 g nuts, roughly chopped — almonds, pecans or hazelnuts
  • 100 g seeds — pumpkin, sunflower or a mix
  • 50 g unsweetened shredded coconut
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon

Wet

  • 160 ml maple syrup or honey
  • 120 ml neutral oil or melted coconut oil
  • 50 g brown sugar
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1 large egg white

After baking

  • 150 g dried fruit, chopped, optional

How

  1. Heat the oven to 300°F (150°C) and line a half sheet pan with parchment. A low oven is the point — at higher heat the syrup caramelises past bitter and the outside of the oats burns before the inside dries.

  2. Combine all the dry ingredients in a large bowl and toss until evenly mixed.

  3. Whisk the syrup, oil, brown sugar and vanilla together in a separate bowl. In a third small bowl, whisk the egg white until frothy and opaque with soft peaks just starting to hold, then fold it into the syrup mixture.

  4. Pour the wet over the dry and mix thoroughly with your hands until every oat is coated and the mixture feels uniformly damp with no dry patches at the bottom of the bowl. Dry patches are where the loose granola comes from.

  5. Tip onto the sheet pan and press it down firmly and evenly with a spatula or the flat of your hands, into a slab about 1.5 cm thick. Press harder than feels necessary. Contact between the oats is what allows the melted sugar to bridge them.

  6. Bake for about forty-five minutes without stirring, without shaking, without opening the door more than once. Rotate the pan halfway if your oven runs uneven, but do not disturb the slab.

  7. It is done when the surface is an even deep gold, the edges are noticeably darker, and the whole slab smells strongly toasted. It will still feel soft and slightly tacky when you press it. That is correct — granola crisps as it cools, not in the oven, and a batch baked until it feels crisp in the pan will be burnt and bitter when cold.

  8. Set the pan on a rack and leave it completely alone until fully cold, at least an hour. This is the step that decides everything. Move it, stir it or break it while warm and you have made loose granola.

  9. When it is cold and hard, lift the slab off the parchment and break it into clusters the size you want. Toss with dried fruit now, after baking — dried fruit in the oven turns to leather.

Notes

  • Vegan binder: 3 tablespoons of aquafaba, the liquid from a tin of chickpeas, whipped to a froth, works in place of the egg white with the same result.

  • It is bitter: the oven was too hot or it went too long. Honey burns sooner than maple syrup, so if you use honey, start checking at thirty-five minutes.

  • Salt and cinnamon are both doubled compared to what a lot of recipes call for, on purpose. Granola is eaten in small handfuls with cold milk and it needs to be seasoned aggressively to taste of anything at all.

Keeping it

Three weeks in a sealed container at room temperature, and it stays crisp as long as it is properly cold before you seal it. Warm granola in a closed container steams itself soft within a day. It freezes for six months and needs no thawing.
