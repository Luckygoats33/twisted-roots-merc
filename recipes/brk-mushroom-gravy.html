Breakfast & The Griddle

Mushroom Gravy, No Meat Required

A vegetarian gravy with enough savoury weight to sit on a biscuit and not apologise for itself.

About 1 litre, enough for 6 split biscuits
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
35 minutes
Start to finish

The problem with a meatless breakfast gravy is not thickening, it is depth. Take the meat out and you have taken out browning, rendered fat and a great deal of salt, and a white sauce with mushrooms in it is not a substitute for any of that. The way back is to build browning deliberately: cook the mushrooms far past the point where they look done, until the water has gone and the pan is sticky brown, then deglaze and put that stuck-on layer back into the sauce. Soy sauce and a little tomato paste do the rest.

What you need

Mushrooms

  • 450 g mushrooms, mixed if you have them, sliced thick
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves, or 1/2 teaspoon dried
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce

Gravy

  • 45 g butter
  • 45 g all-purpose flour
  • 750 ml whole milk, warmed
  • 125 ml vegetable stock or mushroom soaking liquid
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, to taste
  • 1 teaspoon cider vinegar
  • A pinch of grated nutmeg

How

  1. Heat the oil in a wide heavy skillet over medium-high and add the mushrooms in a single crowded layer. They will immediately release water and appear to be boiling. Leave them. Stir only occasionally.

  2. Keep cooking past the point that looks finished. The liquid goes, the pan starts to hiss instead of bubble, the mushrooms shrink to about a third of their volume and take on brown edges, and a sticky brown film builds on the pan bottom. That film is the flavour of this recipe. Give it eight to twelve minutes.

  3. Add the onion and a good pinch of salt and cook until soft and golden at the edges. Add the garlic and thyme and cook until fragrant and no longer raw-smelling, about thirty seconds.

  4. Stir in the tomato paste and cook it until it darkens from bright red to brick and smells sweet rather than tinny, about a minute. Add the soy sauce; it will hiss and lift some of the brown film off the pan.

  5. Push everything to one side, add the butter to the empty half, and when it foams, sprinkle in the flour. Stir it into the butter and cook, scraping, until it smells like toasted crackers and turns the colour of pale straw, about two minutes. If you skip this the gravy will taste chalky no matter how much salt you add.

  6. Pour in the stock first and stir hard to lift everything off the bottom of the pan, then add the warm milk in three additions, stirring smooth between each. Lumps are easy to break up while the mixture is thick and nearly impossible once it thins.

  7. Bring to a bare simmer and hold, stirring often, until it coats a spoon and a finger drawn across the back leaves a track that stays open, about five minutes.

  8. Off the heat, add the pepper, salt to taste, nutmeg, and the vinegar. The vinegar is not optional — a dairy and mushroom sauce goes flat and heavy without a little acid, and you will not taste it as vinegar. Serve over split hot biscuits, toast, or potatoes.

Notes

  • Dried mushrooms make it better. Soak 15 g of dried porcini or shiitake in 125 ml of hot water while you work, chop them into the pan with the fresh, and use the strained soaking water in place of the stock.

  • Crowding the pan is fine here. You have plenty of time and the goal is not searing, it is driving off water and then browning what is left. A crowded pan just takes longer.

  • To make it vegan: swap olive oil for the butter and unsweetened oat milk for the dairy. Oat milk is starchy already, so use about 40 g flour instead of 45 g or it will set up too thick.

Keeping it

Holds cold for four days and thickens hard; loosen with milk or stock over low heat. Like most dairy gravies it separates when frozen. The mushroom base without the milk freezes well for three months, so make a double batch of that and finish the gravy fresh.
