Breakfast & The Griddle

Overnight Oats, Six Containers at a Time

No cooking, no stove, and breakfast handled for the week. The ratio is the whole recipe.

6 servings
Makes
15 minutes
Hands on
8 hours, all of it in the cold
Start to finish

Overnight oats are not cooked at all. Rolled oats sitting in liquid in the cold absorb water and soften over several hours, the same process that happens in a pot but slower and without heat, so the starch never fully gelatinises and the texture stays loose and slightly chewy rather than creamy. Which means the ratio matters more than in cooked oatmeal, because nothing is evaporating and nothing thickens on the stove. One part oats to one part liquid by volume gives a spoonable result; add chia and it firms up considerably overnight. Everything else is a decision about breakfast, not about technique.

What you need

Base, multiplied by six

  • 270 g rolled oats, about 6 portions of 45 g
  • 750 ml milk, or oat, almond or soy milk
  • 300 g plain yoghurt
  • 3 tablespoons chia seeds
  • 3 tablespoons maple syrup or honey
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Per container, choose one

  • 2 tablespoons peanut butter and 1/2 sliced banana
  • 80 g berries and 1 tablespoon toasted almonds
  • 1 grated apple, a pinch of cinnamon and 1 tablespoon raisins
  • 2 tablespoons cocoa powder and 1 tablespoon chopped dark chocolate

Added in the morning

  • 2 tablespoons milk per container, to loosen
  • 1 tablespoon toasted nuts or granola per container, for crunch

How

  1. Stir the milk, yoghurt, chia, syrup, salt and vanilla together in a large bowl until the chia is fully dispersed and not clumped at the bottom. Clumped chia turns into a gluey lump overnight and never redistributes.

  2. Add the oats and stir until every oat is wet. Use rolled oats, not instant — instant oats are pre-cooked and rolled thin, and they collapse into sludge by morning. Steel cut oats do the opposite and stay gritty.

  3. Divide between six containers with lids, about 220 ml each, leaving room at the top. Do not fill to the rim; the mixture swells by about a fifth.

  4. Add the flavourings now if they are sturdy — peanut butter, cocoa, cinnamon, raisins, grated apple. Hold back anything that browns or goes soft, like banana and most berries, and add those in the morning.

  5. Cover and refrigerate at least eight hours. At four hours the oats are still chalky at the centre; at eight they are evenly soft with a slight chew. They keep improving until about day two and then start to go pasty.

  6. In the morning, stir in a splash of milk to loosen — they always set firmer than you expect — and add fruit and something crunchy on top. Without the crunch it is a bowl of one texture, which is the most common complaint about this whole category of breakfast.

  7. Eat cold. If you want them warm, thirty seconds in a microwave takes the chill off, but heating them fully turns the chia gel slimy.

Notes

  • Without chia, use a ratio closer to 45 g oats to 100 ml liquid, because chia absorbs several times its weight in water and you are removing that.

  • Salt is not optional. A cold sweet breakfast without salt tastes flat and slightly chemical. A quarter teaspoon across six portions is enough to notice its absence and not enough to taste.

  • Yoghurt does two jobs: it adds acid, which keeps the whole thing from tasting like wet cereal, and its thickness stops the oats from separating out of the milk overnight.

Keeping it

Five days in the cold, at their best on days one through three. They do not freeze — the dairy separates and the oats go grainy on thawing. Fruit added at the start will be brown and soft by day three, which is why it goes on in the morning.
