Breakfast & The Griddle

Sheet Pan Frittata for a Crowd

Eighteen eggs, one half sheet pan, twenty-four squares. The way to feed a room without standing at a skillet.

24 squares, or 12 generous servings
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes
Start to finish

A skillet frittata feeds four and demands your attention. A sheet pan frittata feeds twelve and demands nothing after it goes in the oven, which is the entire argument for it. The two things that go wrong are both about water: vegetables that were not cooked down first leach into the eggs and leave grey weeping pockets, and an oven run too hot puffs the whole thing up like a soufflé, then drops it into a rubbery slab as it cools. Moderate oven, dry fillings, and pull it while the centre still has the faintest wobble.

What you need

Eggs

  • 18 large eggs
  • 250 ml whole milk or half and half
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Fillings

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 450 g mushrooms, sliced
  • 300 g spinach, or 250 g frozen spinach, thawed
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 200 g sharp cheddar, grated
  • 100 g feta, crumbled

The pan

  • 1 tablespoon butter or oil, for greasing
  • 1 half sheet pan, 33 by 46 cm, with a rim

How

  1. Heat the oven to 325°F (165°C) and set a rack in the middle. This is lower than most recipes suggest, on purpose. High heat inflates a frittata and then deflates it into something bouncy.

  2. Grease the sheet pan thoroughly, corners included, and line it with parchment if you have any doubts about the pan. Grease the parchment too. Eighteen eggs welded to a sheet pan is a bad hour.

  3. Cook the onion in the oil over medium in a wide skillet until soft and translucent with no raw bite left. Add the mushrooms and raise the heat. They will release a lot of liquid; keep going until that liquid has evaporated entirely and the mushrooms squeak and start to brown at the edges. This step is the difference between a frittata and a wet sponge.

  4. Add the garlic, cook until it smells sweet rather than sharp, about thirty seconds, then add the spinach and toss until it collapses and any standing water has cooked off. If you used frozen spinach, squeeze it dry in a towel first — hard, until no more drips come out.

  5. Spread the vegetables evenly over the greased sheet pan and let them cool for a few minutes. Scatter the cheddar and feta over the top.

  6. Whisk the eggs, milk, salt and pepper together until fully uniform with no streaks. Do not aerate it — you want it smooth, not frothy. Pour it slowly over the sheet pan and nudge the fillings so everything sits level.

  7. Bake until the edges are set and lightly puffed, the surface has lost its raw shine, and a gentle shake gives only the slightest tremor at the very centre. An instant-read thermometer pushed into the middle should read 160°F (71°C), which is the food-safety number for an egg dish and, conveniently, also the point where the texture is right. Depending on your oven this lands somewhere around thirty-five minutes.

  8. Let it rest at least ten minutes before cutting. Carryover heat finishes the centre and the whole slab firms up enough to lift out cleanly. Cut into a grid of four by six.

Notes

  • If it wept: either the vegetables went in wet or it was overbaked. Both squeeze water out of the egg. There is no fixing it after the fact, but it still eats fine in a tortilla.

  • Swaps that work: cooked and crumbled bacon, roasted potatoes, sun-dried tomatoes, sauteed peppers, leftover braised greens. Anything raw and watery — fresh tomato, zucchini, raw onion — needs cooking down first.

  • Scaling down: nine eggs and 125 ml milk in a quarter sheet, same oven, and start checking around twenty-two minutes.

Keeping it

This is one of the few egg dishes that is genuinely good cold and genuinely good the next day. Wrap cut squares and hold cold for four days. They reheat in a covered pan over low heat or thirty seconds in a microwave. Freezing works but the texture goes a little spongy; wrap squares individually and use within a month.
