Breakfast & The Griddle

Sourdough Discard Pancakes

What to do with the cup of starter you were about to throw away. Tangy, thin-edged, faintly chewy.

About 12 pancakes
Makes
20 minutes
Hands on
30 minutes, or overnight
Start to finish

Anyone keeping a starter has the same problem: a container of discard in the cold that is too good to bin and too sour to bake with. Pancakes are the honest answer, because everything discard is bad at — weak leavening, aggressive acidity, unpredictable hydration — turns into an advantage here. The acid reacts with baking soda for lift, so you need almost no chemical leavening. The long fermentation has already broken down some of the starch, which gives a thinner batter that spreads into lacy edges. And the sour is a genuine flavour rather than a defect once there is butter and syrup involved.

What you need

Batter

  • 250 g sourdough discard, unfed, any hydration near 100%
  • 180 g all-purpose flour
  • 250 ml milk or buttermilk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 45 g butter, melted
  • 2 tablespoons sugar or honey
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt

Added at the griddle

  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 2 tablespoons butter for greasing

How

  1. Whisk the discard, flour and milk together until smooth. If you have the time, cover this and leave it on the counter for four hours or overnight in the cold. The batter will loosen, bubble faintly and smell distinctly sourer. That step is optional, and it is also most of the reason to make these.

  2. Whisk in the eggs, melted butter, sugar and salt until uniform. The batter should pour off a spoon in a ribbon that sinks back in a couple of seconds. If it is thicker than that, loosen with milk a tablespoon at a time.

  3. Wait until you are ready to cook before adding the soda and powder. Sprinkle them over the surface, then fold in gently — no more than a dozen strokes. The batter will visibly foam and rise within seconds as the soda hits the acid. That reaction is finite, and stirring it flat or letting it sit for twenty minutes spends it for nothing.

  4. Heat a griddle to 375°F (190°C), or until a drop of water beads and skates rather than vanishing. Grease it lightly and wipe most of it away.

  5. Ladle 60 ml portions. These spread thinner than a standard pancake, so leave room. Cook until the edges are set and dry-looking, bubbles have opened across the surface, and the underside is a mottled deep gold when you lift a corner, about two minutes.

  6. Flip once and cook until the second side is browned and the middle springs back under a fingertip rather than feeling wet and dense. If the centre sinks when pressed, give it another thirty seconds.

  7. Serve immediately, or hold on a rack in a low oven. Butter and syrup, or a spoon of thick yoghurt and berries, which suits the sourness better than syrup does.

Notes

  • Hydration varies. A stiff discard needs more milk, a slack one needs less. Judge by the ribbon off the spoon, not by the numbers here.

  • They taste soapy: too much baking soda for the acidity of your discard. A very young or recently fed starter is not sour enough to neutralise a full teaspoon. Cut it to half and lean on the baking powder.

  • Discard that has been in the cold for weeks and has a layer of grey liquid on top is fine — pour the liquid off if you want them less sour, stir it in if you want them more so. Discard that smells of acetone or shows fuzzy colour goes in the bin.

Keeping it

Unleavened batter keeps cold for three days and gets sourer each day. Once the soda goes in, use it within about ten minutes. Cooked pancakes freeze between sheets of parchment for two months and toast well from frozen.
