Fish & Shellfish

Whole Baked Steelhead with Brown Butter

Leaner than salmon, less forgiving, and better than either when it is caught right.

Serves 6
Makes
20 minutes
Hands on
50 minutes
Start to finish

Steelhead is a rainbow trout that went to sea and came back with opinions. It looks like salmon, it prices like a bargain, and it cooks faster because it carries less fat. That last part is the whole story: the buffer that lets a fatty king coast through an extra four minutes is not there, so a steelhead side goes from perfect to dry while you are washing a bowl.

So this is a moderate oven, a probe left in, and a brown butter poured over at the end to put back some of the richness the fish does not have on its own.

What you need

Fish

  • 1 steelhead side, about 2.5 lb (1.1 kg), skin on, pin bones out
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp olive oil

Brown butter

  • 6 tbsp butter
  • 2 tbsp capers, drained
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 3 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 pinch chile flakes

How

  1. Take the fish out of the fridge 20 minutes before it goes in. A side straight from cold cooks unevenly, thin end first, and steelhead has no margin for that.

  2. Heat the oven to 325°F (165°C). Lower than you would use for salmon, because the goal is to narrow the gap between the surface and the middle.

  3. Dry the fillet, salt and pepper it, rub with oil, and lay it skin down on a parchment-lined sheet pan. Tuck the thin tail end under itself so the whole side is roughly one thickness.

  4. Bake until a probe in the thickest part reads an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C) and the flesh is opaque, firming under a fingertip, and just beginning to separate along the muscle lines. In a moderate oven this is roughly 20 to 25 minutes for a side of this size, but the probe decides, not the clock.

  5. If you like a translucent centre, cooks routinely pull at 125 to 130 degrees Fahrenheit (52 to 54°C). That is below the FDA figure and it is not the right call for anyone pregnant, immunocompromised, elderly or very young.

  6. While it bakes, melt the butter in a small pale-coloured pan over medium heat. Let it foam, then keep going, swirling, until the foam falls, the milk solids on the bottom turn the colour of a hazelnut shell and the whole kitchen smells like toffee. That happens fast and it burns two seconds later.

  7. Pull the pan off the heat immediately and add the capers, which will spit, then the lemon juice, parsley and chile.

  8. Rest the fish 5 minutes, pour the brown butter over the whole side, and serve straight off the pan.

  9. What goes wrong: treating steelhead like a king salmon. Same shape, half the fat, two thirds the cooking time. If your last one was dry, the fish was not the problem.

Notes

  • Hatchery steelhead and wild steelhead cook the same. What you may keep and where is an Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife question and the regulations move, so check with ODFW rather than a recipe page.
  • The same treatment works for a large lake trout or a big rainbow, at the same temperature, checked earlier.

Keeping it

Fridge three days. Cold steelhead flakes into a salad better than it reheats, and reheating is what dries it out the rest of the way.
