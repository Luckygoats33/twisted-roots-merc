The Crab Melt

An open-faced sandwich that has no business being as good as it is.

4 open-faced sandwiches
Makes
15 minutes
Hands on
25 minutes
Start to finish

This is what happens to the crab that did not get eaten standing over the sink. It is a counter lunch, not a dinner party, and it is quietly one of the best uses of Dungeness there is, because the cheese and the toast take a small amount of crab a very long way.

The only real technique is keeping it under the broiler rather than in the oven. You want the top browned and bubbling before the bread underneath has time to go soft, which is a matter of about four minutes of close attention and one open oven door.

What you need

Crab mix

  • 12 oz (340 g) picked crab meat, drained
  • 1/3 cup mayonnaise, full fat
  • 2 tbsp sour cream
  • 2 tbsp finely diced celery
  • 2 tbsp thinly sliced scallion
  • 1 tsp lemon zest
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt

Assembly

  • 4 thick slices sourdough or a split ciabatta
  • 3 tbsp softened butter
  • 1 clove garlic, halved
  • 6 oz (170 g) sharp cheddar, grated
  • 2 oz (55 g) gruyere, grated
  • 8 thin slices tomato, optional

How

  1. Set an oven rack about six inches under the broiler element and turn the broiler on to preheat. A cold broiler browns nothing and just dries the sandwich out.

  2. Fold the crab mix ingredients together lightly, keeping the lumps whole. Taste it. It should be a shade under-seasoned, because the cheese is bringing salt.

  3. Butter the bread on both sides and toast it in a dry skillet over medium until the underside is golden and crisp and the slice sounds hollow when tapped. Rub the toasted face once with the cut garlic.

  4. Lay the toast on a sheet pan, toasted face up. Add tomato slices if you are using them, then pile the crab mix on and spread it to the edges. Bare bread at the edge burns before the middle heats.

  5. Cover generously with both cheeses, all the way to the crust.

  6. Broil, with the door open and your eyes on it, until the cheese has melted, pooled and browned in blisters across the top and the crab underneath is steaming. It goes from pale to perfect to ruined in under a minute at the end.

  7. If you want to be sure the middle is hot, slide a probe in from the side; an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C) means it is heated through, which matters more if the crab came out of the fridge cold.

  8. Let it settle a minute before cutting, or the whole topping slides off in one piece and lands on the board.

  9. What goes wrong: too much mayonnaise. It loosens under heat and runs out from under the cheese, and you end up with a soggy raft. Keep the mix stiff.

Notes

  • Shrimp works, canned salmon works, hot-smoked salmon works if you cut it with something plain. This is a template.
  • Sourdough holds up under a wet topping far better than a soft sandwich loaf, which turns to pulp.

Keeping it

Assembled melts do not keep. The crab mix keeps two days in the fridge and is also a fine thing to eat with crackers while deciding whether to bother with the toast.
