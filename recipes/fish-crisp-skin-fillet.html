Fish & Shellfish

Crisp-Skin Salmon in a Pan

Skin like a potato chip, and the fish underneath still translucent in the middle.

4 fillets
Makes
20 minutes
Hands on
45 minutes
Start to finish

Crisp skin is not a technique so much as a water problem. Skin is wet, and wet things steam. Everything below is about removing water before the pan does, and then not moving the fish while the pan finishes the job.

The second half of the trick is that you barely cook the top. Set the fillet skin down in a hot pan, leave it alone for most of the time, and let heat climb up through the fillet. Flipping early gives you two grey faces and a soggy skin, which is what most people get and quietly accept.

What you need

Fish

  • 4 salmon fillets, skin on, about 6 oz (170 g) each, pin bones out
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil with a high smoke point

Pan

  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1 clove garlic, crushed
  • 1 lemon, halved

How

  1. Salt the skin side and set the fillets skin up on a rack over a plate. Leave them uncovered in the fridge 30 minutes, longer if you have it. The salt draws water out of the skin and the cold air takes it away. This step does more than the pan does.

  2. Just before cooking, blot the skin again and scrape the flat of a knife across it from head end to tail. You will be surprised what comes off. Salt the flesh side now, not earlier.

  3. Heat a heavy skillet over medium-high with the oil until it shimmers and the first wisp of smoke lifts off the surface. A pan that is not hot enough at the start is why skin sticks.

  4. Lay the fillets in skin down, away from you, and press each one flat with a spatula for about 20 seconds. Fillets curl as the skin contracts, and a curled fillet only touches the pan in the middle.

  5. Now leave them. Do not slide them, do not peek under. Watch the side of the fillet: opacity creeps up from the bottom like a tide line. When it has climbed roughly two thirds of the way and the skin has stopped sizzling frantically, you are close.

  6. Drop in the butter, thyme and garlic, tilt the pan and spoon foaming butter over the top for a minute. The top face goes from raw to just set without ever touching metal.

  7. Flip, count to 30, and take them out. Fully cooked is an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C) at the thickest point, where the flesh flakes firmly and looks the same colour throughout. Cooks who prefer a translucent centre pull at 125 to 130 degrees Fahrenheit (52 to 54°C), which is below the FDA figure and not for anyone pregnant, immunocompromised, elderly or very young.

  8. Rest skin up on a warm plate, never skin down on anything, and squeeze lemon over the flesh only. Lemon juice on the skin undoes twenty minutes of work in four seconds.

  9. What goes wrong: crowding. Two fillets in a small pan drop the temperature, water pools, and you have poached fish with leathery skin. Cook in two batches and keep the first warm in a low oven.

Notes

  • Nonstick works but never gets the same crackle. Carbon steel or cast iron, properly hot, is the tool for this.
  • Chalky salmon is salmon that went past temperature. The proteins squeeze out water and there is no getting it back. Buy a thermometer before you buy better fish.

Keeping it

Best eaten immediately, because skin softens within minutes of resting. Leftovers keep in the fridge three days and are worth flaking cold into a salad rather than reheating.
