Razor Clams, Cleaned and Fried

Two minutes in the pan, and the cleaning is the whole job.

Serves 4
Makes
45 minutes, most of it cleaning
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

A razor clam is the reason people stand on a cold beach at night with a flashlight and a tube. It does not taste like a steamer clam and it does not cook like one. The meat is tender, sweet and almost creamy, and it goes from that to a piece of belt leather in roughly ninety seconds of unnecessary heat.

The cleaning is the part that puts people off, and it is genuinely a bit of work the first few times. After that it takes about a minute a clam. The frying is barely a recipe: hot fat, a light crumb, one turn, out.

What you need

Clams

  • 12 razor clams, in the shell
  • 8 cups boiling water for the dip
  • 8 cups ice water

Coating

  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tbsp milk
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1.5 cups fine cracker crumbs or panko
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne

Pan

  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 lemons, in wedges

How

  1. Dip the clams, a few at a time, into boiling water for five to ten seconds, just until the shells crack apart, then straight into ice water. Any longer and you have begun cooking them, which you will regret at the pan.

  2. Slide the shells off and the clam out. Cut off the tip of the neck and the dark tip of the digger foot.

  3. Snip the clam open lengthwise from the neck through the body so it opens flat like a book. Cut away the gills, the dark gut running along the body, and the tough zipper-like piece near the base.

  4. Rinse each cleaned clam under cold running water, rubbing the surface with your thumbs to get every trace of sand from the folds. Sand is the entire reason a fried razor clam ever disappoints anybody.

  5. Dry the cleaned clams hard between towels. Wet clams will not hold a crumb and will spit in the fat.

  6. Set up three shallow dishes: flour with the salt, pepper and cayenne; eggs beaten with the milk; crumbs. Coat each clam flour, egg, crumb, pressing the crumbs on, and lay them on a rack.

  7. Heat the butter and oil in a wide skillet over medium-high until the butter foam has subsided and a dropped crumb browns within a few seconds. This has to be hot; a cool pan means a longer cook and a tough clam.

  8. Fry in batches, no crowding, until the underside is golden and the edges of the clam have just started to curl, roughly a minute, then turn once and give the second side about the same. Cooked razor clam is opaque white and still yielding; if it has shrunk noticeably and gone rigid, it is past.

  9. If you want the number, seafood is fully cooked at an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C), but a razor clam is thin enough that it arrives there almost immediately. Time and appearance are the practical guide here.

  10. Drain on a rack, salt lightly, and serve with lemon and nothing else that needs cooking.

Notes

  • Razor clam beaches close for domoic acid, sometimes for long stretches. Check the current closures with the Oregon Department of Agriculture before you dig, every single time. Cooking does not destroy the toxin and there is no way to see it.
  • Digging requires a licence and there are daily limits, including the rule that you keep the first clams you dig regardless of size. That is ODFW's rule, not ours, and it is worth reading before you go.

Keeping it

Cleaned raw clams keep a day in the fridge and freeze well, laid flat in a bag with the air pressed out, for three months. Fried clams keep for about as long as it takes to carry the plate to the table.
