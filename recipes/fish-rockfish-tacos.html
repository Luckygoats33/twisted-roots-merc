Fish & Shellfish

Rockfish Tacos

The cheapest good fish on this coast, in a hot pan, with cabbage and lime.

12 tacos, serves 4
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
45 minutes
Start to finish

Rockfish is what people bring back from a bottom fishing trip out of Depoe Bay when the tuna did not happen and the halibut season was not open. It is firm, mild, white, cheap, and it holds together in a pan better than cod does, which makes it the right fish for a taco.

These are pan-seared rather than fried, mostly because a taco already has enough going on and partly because nobody wants to heat eight cups of oil on a Tuesday. The slaw is doing the work that a batter would otherwise do: crunch, acid, and something cold against something hot.

What you need

Fish

  • 1.5 lb (680 g) rockfish fillets, skin off, cut in 1 inch strips
  • 2 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp dried oregano
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 lime, halved

Slaw

  • 4 cups shredded green cabbage
  • 1/2 red onion, sliced paper thin
  • 1/3 cup chopped cilantro
  • 3 tbsp lime juice
  • 1 tsp honey
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt

Crema

  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise, full fat
  • 1 tbsp lime juice
  • 1 chipotle in adobo, minced
  • 1 pinch salt

To serve

  • 12 corn tortillas
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • 2 limes, in wedges

How

  1. Toss the cabbage and onion with lime juice, honey and salt and let it sit while you do everything else. Twenty minutes in acid takes the raw edge off the onion and wilts the cabbage just enough to fold into a tortilla.

  2. Stir the crema ingredients together and taste for heat. One chipotle is mild in this quantity; two is not.

  3. Mix the spices with the salt and toss the fish strips in it, coating all sides. Do this right before cooking, because salt sitting on cut fish draws water and you will be searing wet fish.

  4. Warm the tortillas first, directly over a gas flame or dry in a hot skillet, until they puff slightly and take a few dark spots. Stack them in a folded towel to steam soft.

  5. Heat the oil in a heavy skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Lay the strips in one layer with space between them and do not touch them.

  6. Sear until the underside has a real brown crust and the strips release from the pan on their own, then turn each one. Rockfish is done when it is opaque to the centre, flakes at a fork, and reaches an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C). In strips this size that is a couple of minutes a side.

  7. Squeeze lime over the fish in the pan off the heat, and break the strips into rough chunks with a spatula.

  8. Build: tortilla, crema, fish, drained slaw, avocado, more lime. Drain the slaw or the tortilla goes to pieces before it reaches your mouth.

  9. What goes wrong: overcooking, which turns rockfish stringy and dry rather than chalky the way salmon goes. Pull it a shade early and let the residual heat finish it in the pan off the burner.

Notes

  • Lingcod, true cod, sablefish and halibut trim all work here. So does the awkward tail end of a salmon fillet if that is what is in the fridge.
  • Doubling the spice mix and keeping it in a jar makes this a fifteen minute dinner the second time.

Keeping it

The slaw and crema keep three days. Cooked fish keeps two, and is better folded into a cold taco than reheated into a hot one.
