Fish & Shellfish

Salmon Cakes That Hold Together

The binder is the whole argument, and most recipes get the ratio wrong in favour of bread.

8 cakes
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 30 minutes with the chill
Start to finish

A salmon cake is a way of turning the far end of a fillet, or last night's leftovers, into something people are pleased to see. The failure is always the same: too much filler and it eats like stuffing, too little and it collapses in the pan into an expensive hash.

The fix is not more breadcrumbs. It is a small amount of binder, a proper chill so the fat sets, and gentle handling that keeps recognisable flakes intact instead of mashing everything into paste. You want to see the fish in the finished cake.

What you need

Cakes

  • 1 lb (450 g) cooked salmon, flaked, skin and bones removed
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 tbsp mayonnaise, full fat
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 3/4 cup panko breadcrumbs
  • 3 tbsp finely diced shallot
  • 2 tbsp chopped dill or parsley
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Coating and frying

  • 1 cup panko for the outside
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tbsp butter

Sauce

  • 1/2 cup mayonnaise, full fat
  • 2 tbsp chopped cornichons
  • 1 tbsp capers, chopped
  • 1 tsp lemon zest

How

  1. If you are starting from raw fish, cook it first: bake portions at 350°F (175°C) until they flake apart easily and reach an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C), then cool completely. Warm fish will not bind.

  2. Flake the salmon into a bowl in pieces roughly the size of a thumbnail. Do not shred it. Pull out any pin bones and the grey layer under the skin, which is oily and strong and does the finished cake no favours.

  3. Whisk egg, mayonnaise, mustard and lemon juice together separately, then fold that into the fish with the shallot, herbs, salt, pepper and three quarters cup of panko. Fold, do not stir. Twelve strokes, not forty.

  4. Squeeze a small handful. It should just hold. If it slumps, add panko a tablespoon at a time; if it feels dry and crumbly, another spoon of mayonnaise. This is the only judgement call in the recipe.

  5. Shape eight cakes about three quarters of an inch thick, set them on a lined tray, and refrigerate at least an hour. The chill is not optional. It sets the fat and the egg, and it is the difference between a cake and a scramble.

  6. Press both faces into the remaining panko. Heat oil and butter in a heavy skillet over medium until the butter foam subsides and a crumb dropped in sizzles steadily rather than violently.

  7. Fry in batches, without crowding, until the underside is deep golden and a spatula slides under cleanly, then flip once. They are hot through when the centre steams as you press the side and a probe reads 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C).

  8. Drain on a rack, not on paper. Paper traps steam and softens the crust you just spent ten minutes building.

  9. Stir the sauce ingredients together and serve alongside with lemon.

  10. What goes wrong: flipping too early. If you have to force a spatula under, the crust has not formed and it will tear. Wait for it to release.

Notes

  • Canned or hot-smoked salmon both work. Smoked is stronger; cut it with an equal weight of plain cooked fish or the whole plate tastes like a campfire.
  • The cakes freeze well raw. Freeze on the tray, bag them, and fry from frozen over lower heat with a lid on for the first few minutes.

Keeping it

Shaped raw cakes hold two days in the fridge. Cooked cakes keep three days and reheat best in a hot oven on a rack, never in a microwave.
