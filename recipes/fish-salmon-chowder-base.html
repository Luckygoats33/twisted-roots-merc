Fish & Shellfish

A Salmon Chowder Base

Build the base right and the fish goes in last, for four minutes, off the boil.

About 3 quarts, serves 6 to 8
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes
Start to finish

Most bad chowder is bad because the fish was in the pot the entire time. Salmon goes from silky to woolly in about ninety seconds of hard simmering, and once it has gone there is no route back. So the base is built and seasoned as a complete thing on its own, and the fish is stirred in at the end and barely cooked by residual heat.

This makes about three quarts of base. It freezes, which means a pot on a Sunday turns into four fast dinners on wet weeknights when nobody wants to think.

What you need

Base

  • 4 oz (110 g) bacon, diced
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 1 leek, white and pale green, sliced and washed
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 6 cups fish or chicken stock
  • 2 lb (900 g) waxy potatoes, in 3/4 inch dice
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp thyme leaves
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp white pepper

Finishing, per pot

  • 1.5 lb (680 g) salmon, skin off, in 1 inch cubes
  • 1.5 cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup corn kernels
  • 2 tbsp chopped dill
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

How

  1. Render the bacon in a heavy pot over medium heat until the fat has run out and the pieces are browned at the edges but not brittle. Lift the bacon out and set it aside; leave the fat.

  2. Add the butter, then onion, celery and leek. Cook until everything has slumped and gone translucent and the onion smells sweet rather than sharp, with no colour on it.

  3. Stir in the garlic for a minute, then the flour. Keep it moving until the raw flour smell is gone and the vegetables are coated in a pale paste. Undercooked flour tastes exactly like undercooked flour and it will follow you all the way to the bowl.

  4. Pour in the stock a cup at a time, whisking each addition smooth before the next. Add potatoes, bay, thyme, salt and pepper.

  5. Bring it to a bare simmer and hold it there until the potatoes give way completely to a knife tip with no resistance in the middle, roughly 20 minutes depending on the variety. Fish out the bay leaves.

  6. Crush a few potatoes against the side of the pot with a spoon. That starch is your thickener, and it beats adding more flour.

  7. Stop here if you are freezing it. Cool the base fast, in a shallow pan, and freeze in quart portions. Cream and fish go in at serving time, never before.

  8. To finish a pot: bring the base back to a gentle simmer, stir in the cream and corn and let it come back up to steaming. Then take the pot off the heat entirely.

  9. Slide the salmon cubes in, stir once, and put the lid on. They cook in the residual heat in about 4 minutes, until they turn opaque and break apart at a nudge from a spoon, reaching an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C). If your pieces are large, check one with a probe.

  10. Add dill, lemon juice and the reserved bacon. Taste for salt now and not before, because the bacon and stock have both been quietly seasoning it.

Notes

  • Hot-smoked salmon instead of raw makes a different and very good soup. Use half the weight and add it with the dill, since it is already cooked.
  • Waxy potatoes hold their shape. Russets disintegrate into the broth, which some people want and most do not.

Keeping it

Finished chowder keeps two days in the fridge and does not freeze well once the fish is in. Plain base freezes three months.
