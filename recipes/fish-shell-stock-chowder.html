Fish & Shellfish

Chowder Built on Shells

The bones and shells you were about to throw out are the ingredient.

About 3 quarts, serves 6
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
2 hours
Start to finish

Every crab you pick and every fish you fillet leaves behind the part that has the most flavour in it. Shells, heads and frames are where the gelatin and the sweetness live, and forty minutes of gentle simmering pulls it all into water for nothing. A chowder built on that base tastes like the ocean; the same chowder built on bought stock tastes like a soup with fish in it.

Two rules. Do not simmer fish bones for hours, because they turn bitter and glue-like after about forty minutes. And roast crab shells first, because raw shell stock tastes flat and roasted shell stock tastes like a bisque.

What you need

Shell stock

  • 4 crab bodies and shells, or 2 fish frames and heads, gills removed
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 onion, quartered
  • 2 carrots, chopped
  • 2 stalks celery, chopped
  • 1 fennel top, chopped
  • 1 cup dry white wine
  • 10 cups cold water
  • 8 peppercorns
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 6 parsley stems

Chowder

  • 4 oz (110 g) salt pork or bacon, diced
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 2 lb (900 g) waxy potatoes, in 3/4 inch dice
  • 1 tsp thyme leaves
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp white pepper

To finish

  • 1.5 cups heavy cream
  • 1 lb (450 g) picked crab, clams or flaked cooked fish
  • 3 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

How

  1. For crab shells, heat the oven to 400°F (205°C), toss the shells with the oil, and roast until they have darkened to a deep red-orange and smell sweet and toasty rather than raw. Fish frames skip this step; they are better unroasted.

  2. Put the shells or frames in a stockpot with the vegetables, wine, cold water and aromatics. Start from cold water, which draws more out than dropping bones into hot water does.

  3. Bring it slowly up until the surface just trembles, skimming the grey foam off as it rises. Never let it boil. A boiling stock emulsifies fat and scum back into itself and comes out cloudy and muddy.

  4. Simmer crab shells 45 minutes and fish frames no more than 30. Fish bones give up everything they have in half an hour and turn bitter shortly after. Strain through a fine sieve and press gently; do not force it.

  5. Render the salt pork in a heavy pot over medium heat until the fat runs and the pieces are golden and crisp at the edges. Lift them out and reserve.

  6. Add the butter, onion and celery and cook until soft and translucent with no colour, then stir in the flour and cook it until the raw smell is gone and it coats everything in a pale paste.

  7. Whisk in 8 cups of the shell stock a little at a time, then add the potatoes, thyme, salt and pepper. Simmer until the potatoes surrender completely to a knife tip and the broth has thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon.

  8. Crush a few potato cubes against the side of the pot to release their starch, which thickens more cleanly than adding flour a second time.

  9. Stir in the cream and bring it back to steaming, then take it off the heat before adding the seafood. Picked crab and cooked fish need warming, not cooking; give them 3 minutes off the burner under a lid, and check that anything raw reaches an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C).

  10. Finish with parsley, lemon juice and the reserved salt pork. Taste for salt at the very end, since the stock, the pork and the shellfish have all been seasoning it behind your back.

Notes

  • Freeze shells and frames as you generate them, in a bag in the freezer, until there is enough for a pot. They keep three months without much loss.
  • Gills must come out of a fish head before it goes in the pot. They hold blood and grit and they are the main reason people think fish stock tastes muddy.

Keeping it

Strained stock keeps four days in the fridge and six months frozen. Finished chowder keeps two days and does not freeze once the cream and seafood are in.
