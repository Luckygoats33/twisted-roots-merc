Fish & Shellfish

A Whole Fish Baked on a Bed of Potatoes

The potatoes cook in the drippings, which is the entire reason to do it this way.

Serves 4
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes
Start to finish

People are shy about whole fish because of the head, which is silly, because a whole fish is easier to cook well than a fillet and costs less per pound of edible meat. Bones conduct heat inward and the skin keeps water in, so the margin is generous.

Baking one over sliced potatoes solves the other problem, which is that a whole fish gives up a lot of good liquid and it normally runs into a sheet pan and burns. Here it runs into starch instead. Rockfish, black bass, a small lingcod or a sea bream all work; what matters is that it fits your dish.

What you need

Base

  • 2 lb (900 g) waxy potatoes, sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 1 large onion, sliced thin
  • 4 cloves garlic, sliced
  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 1.5 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 cup dry white wine
  • 1 tsp dried oregano

Fish

  • 1 whole rockfish, 2.5 to 3 lb (1.1 to 1.4 kg), scaled, gutted, gills out
  • 1 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 lemon, half sliced and half in wedges
  • 6 sprigs parsley
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

How

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the potatoes, onion and garlic with the oil, salt, pepper and oregano and spread them in a baking dish just large enough to hold the fish on top.

  2. Pour the wine over and bake the potatoes on their own until the edges are starting to colour and a knife goes into a slice with only slight resistance. They need this head start because the fish will be done long before raw potatoes would be.

  3. Meanwhile prepare the fish. Check for scales the fishmonger missed by running a thumb from tail to head. Rinse the cavity, dry the fish inside and out, and slash both sides down to the bone twice.

  4. Salt the fish inside and out, stuff the cavity with lemon slices and parsley, and rub the outside with oil.

  5. Lay it on the potatoes, slide the dish back in, and bake until the skin has blistered and browned in patches and the flesh at the shoulder is opaque to the bone. A probe at the thickest point should read an internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit (63°C).

  6. The old test still works if you have no probe: push a thin knife into the thickest part behind the head and hold it there for five seconds, then touch it to your lip. Hot means done. Cool means keep going.

  7. Rest the dish on the counter 5 minutes. Serve the fish straight from the dish, lifting the top fillet away from the backbone, then removing the whole skeleton before you work on the underside.

  8. Squeeze lemon over and spoon the potatoes out from underneath, along with whatever has collected in the bottom of the dish. That liquid is the best thing on the table.

  9. What goes wrong: an undercooked potato layer, because the fish went in at the same time. Give the base its head start and check that a slice from the middle of the dish is tender before the fish ever touches it.

Notes

  • The cheeks, behind and slightly below the eye, are two coin-sized pieces of the best meat on the fish. Somebody in the kitchen should get them as payment.
  • A fish that will not fit the dish can have its head removed. It is not traditional and nobody at the table will care.

Keeping it

Fridge, three days, fish picked off the bone and stored separately from the potatoes. The potatoes reheat well in a hot oven; the fish does not.
