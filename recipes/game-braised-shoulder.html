Game & The Freezer

Slow-Braised Shoulder

The cut nobody wants, cooked the only way it was ever going to work.

Serves 6 to 8
Makes
40 minutes
Hands on
5 hours
Start to finish

A shoulder is a working muscle group laced with connective tissue, and every attempt to treat it like a steak ends the same way. Under long wet heat, though, that same connective tissue turns to gelatin, and gelatin is what makes a braise feel rich even though there is almost no fat in the animal.

The requirement is time at a low temperature. A braise held at a hard boil tightens the muscle fibres and squeezes them dry, so you end up with stringy meat sitting in good sauce. Barely a bubble, lid on, several hours, and do not go poking at it.

What you need

Meat

  • 4 lb (1.8 kg) venison or elk shoulder, boned, in large chunks
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 3 tbsp beef tallow or neutral oil

Braise

  • 2 onions, sliced thick
  • 3 carrots, in chunks
  • 3 stalks celery, in chunks
  • 6 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 cups red wine
  • 4 cups beef or game stock
  • 4 sprigs thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 8 juniper berries, crushed
  • 1 strip orange peel

To finish

  • 2 tbsp cold butter
  • 1 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 3 tbsp chopped parsley

How

  1. Trim the shoulder hard. Every bit of waxy fat and every sheet of silverskin you leave will float to the top of the braise as a greasy film and taste of the freezer. Connective tissue between muscles can stay; that is the good kind.

  2. Season the chunks, dust them with flour, and shake off the excess. Heat the tallow in a heavy Dutch oven until it shimmers.

  3. Brown the meat in batches, without crowding, until each piece has a genuinely dark crust on at least two sides and the bottom of the pot has gone brown and sticky. Pale meat gives a pale braise; this step is the flavour.

  4. Set the meat aside, lower the heat, and cook the onions, carrots and celery in the fat until softened and browning at the edges. Add garlic and tomato paste and cook, stirring, until the paste darkens from red to brick and smells sweet rather than tinny.

  5. Pour in the wine and scrape everything off the bottom with a wooden spoon. Reduce it by half, which takes the raw edge off and concentrates it.

  6. Return the meat, add stock to come about three quarters of the way up, then the thyme, bay, juniper and orange peel. Bring it just to a simmer on the stove.

  7. Cover and move it to a 300°F (150°C) oven. Check after 30 minutes: the liquid should be barely trembling, with a bubble breaking every second or two. If it is bubbling steadily, drop the oven by 25 degrees Fahrenheit.

  8. Cook until a fork pushed into a chunk twists freely with no resistance and the meat begins to fall apart at the seams under its own weight. That is roughly three and a half to four and a half hours, and it is a texture, not a time.

  9. Braising takes the meat far past any internal temperature target, which is the point; connective tissue only converts to gelatin somewhere above 160°F (71°C) and it needs hours there. Doneness here is texture, not a number on a probe.

  10. Lift the meat out, strain the liquid, and skim the fat off the top. Reduce the strained liquid hard on the stove until it coats a spoon and leaves a clear line when you drag a finger across the back of it.

  11. Off the heat, swirl in the cold butter and the vinegar. The vinegar is not optional; it cuts the richness and makes the whole pot taste like it has seasoning rather than just salt.

  12. Return the meat to the sauce, warm it through, and scatter parsley over. Serve on mashed potatoes, polenta or noodles.

  13. What goes wrong: a boil. If the meat comes out stringy and dry inside a great sauce, the pot was too hot. Same recipe, lower oven, next time.

Notes

  • Better the second day, genuinely. The gelatin sets overnight and the fat lifts off the top in a solid disc, which is the easiest skimming you will ever do.
  • Neck meat is the same proposition as shoulder and often better. Do not grind either of them if you have the time to braise.

Keeping it

Fridge four days, freezer three months in its sauce. Freeze the meat submerged in liquid or the exposed edges dry out and go woolly.
