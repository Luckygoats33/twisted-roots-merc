Game & The Freezer

Grinding and Packing the Freezer

An afternoon of tedium that determines what the next twelve months taste like.

One animal, packaged
Makes
3 to 5 hours
Hands on
An afternoon
Start to finish

Grinding day is not glamorous and it is where most of the flavour of an entire animal is won or lost. Warm meat, dull plates and sloppy wrapping produce a freezer full of packages nobody is excited to open. Cold meat, sharp equipment and tight packages produce burger that tastes like the animal did.

Plan the whole thing before you cut anything. Decide how much goes to grind, what package sizes your household actually cooks, and how much fat you are adding, and then work through it without stopping to think.

What you need

Equipment

  • 1 meat grinder with a coarse and a fine plate
  • 2 sharp grinder knives
  • 4 large trays that fit in the freezer
  • 1 vacuum sealer or 2 rolls of freezer paper and tape
  • 1 permanent marker that writes on plastic
  • 2 boxes nitrile gloves

Meat

  • 40 lb (18 kg) trimmed venison or elk destined for grind
  • 8 lb (3.6 kg) beef fat trim or pork fat, at 20 percent by weight
  • 2 gallons ice for chilling bowls

How

  1. Trim first, grind second, and never blur the two. Every scrap of silverskin, sinew and waxy game fat that goes into the hopper either wraps around the auger and stops the machine or ends up as gristle in a burger.

  2. Cut everything into strips that fit the throat of your grinder without forcing. Forcing is what dulls the blade and warms the meat.

  3. Chill hard. Trays of cubed meat go in the freezer until the edges are crunchy, roughly 30 to 45 minutes. Grinder head, auger, blade and plate go in with them. Warm equipment smears fat and smeared fat means greasy, crumbly burger.

  4. Decide the fat ratio before you begin. Ten percent added fat makes a lean grind suitable for chili and sauces; twenty percent makes burger patties that behave. Weigh it rather than guessing.

  5. Grind once through the coarse plate, alternating handfuls of meat and fat so the mix is even without a second pass. For burger, follow with a fine plate; for chili and sausage, coarse is better.

  6. Watch what comes out of the plate. It should be distinct strands. The moment it turns into a paste or starts oozing rather than extruding, stop, clean the blade and plate, and chill everything again. Something is dull or something is warm.

  7. Package in the sizes you actually cook. A pound for a household of two, two pounds for a family, and nothing bigger, because the leftover half of a thawed package is the thing that gets forgotten.

  8. Vacuum seal if you have the machine. If you are wrapping in paper, press the package flat and thin, force every bit of air out, wrap tightly with the shiny side in, and tape all the seams. Flat packages freeze faster, stack better and thaw more evenly.

  9. Label every single package with species, cut and weight. Do not put a date on the paper if you would rather not, but write something that tells you which animal it came from, or you will be defrosting mysteries in the middle of winter.

  10. Freeze in a single layer first, spread out, and only stack once everything is solid. A freezer loaded with a warm stack of packages takes a day to catch up and everything in it suffers.

  11. Wash the grinder immediately and dry the metal parts completely. Carbon steel plates rust overnight and a rusted plate is a ruined plate.

  12. Remember that ground meat has a higher food safety floor than whole muscle. Everything you just made gets cooked until it is browned through with no pink left and a probe reads an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C), because grinding takes the surface of the meat and puts it through the middle.

Notes

  • Freezing does not sterilise meat and it is not a substitute for testing an animal. For anything to do with chronic wasting disease, sampling, or an animal that did not look right, go to ODFW and your current state hunter guide.
  • A cheap grinder will do one deer a year unhappily. If you are doing an elk, borrow or buy something with a real motor and a number 22 head.

Keeping it

Ground game holds its quality about four months at 0 degrees Fahrenheit (-18°C), whole-muscle cuts about a year, and vacuum-sealed packages roughly double both. It stays safe indefinitely at that temperature; what degrades is how it tastes.
