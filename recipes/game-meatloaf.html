Game & The Freezer

Venison Meatloaf

Free-form on a sheet pan, glazed twice, and never pressed into a loaf tin.

Serves 8
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 40 minutes
Start to finish

A loaf tin steams the sides of a meatloaf and leaves it sitting in its own grease, which is why tin-baked meatloaf is pale and slightly boiled at the edges. Shaped free-form on a sheet pan, the whole outside browns, the fat runs off, and there is surface area for a glaze to caramelise on.

Venison needs the same two supports as every other lean ground preparation here: added fat and a panade. Get those right and this is better than the beef version, because the meat itself has more flavour to begin with.

What you need

Panade and aromatics

  • 1.5 cups fresh breadcrumbs
  • 3/4 cup whole milk
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 stalks celery, finely diced
  • 1 carrot, grated
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced

Meat

  • 2 lb (900 g) ground venison
  • 12 oz (340 g) ground pork
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1.5 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/3 cup chopped parsley

Glaze

  • 3/4 cup ketchup
  • 3 tbsp brown sugar
  • 2 tbsp cider vinegar
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp hot sauce

How

  1. Soak the breadcrumbs in the milk and set aside. Melt the butter and cook the onion, celery and carrot until completely soft and sweet, with no crunch left, then add garlic for a minute and let it cool.

  2. Raw onion in a meatloaf never fully cooks and gives you hard hot bites. The five minutes this step costs is worth it.

  3. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and line a sheet pan with parchment.

  4. Combine the soaked crumbs, cooled vegetables, both meats, eggs, Worcestershire and seasonings. Mix with your hands, lifting and turning, only until it is uniform. Overworked meatloaf is springy and dense.

  5. Fry a small patty, taste it, and adjust the salt. It is the last chance you get.

  6. Shape the mix into a free-form loaf on the pan, roughly nine inches long and four wide, with the top gently domed. Smooth the surface so there are no cracks for fat to pool in.

  7. Whisk the glaze ingredients and brush a third of it over the loaf.

  8. Bake for about 40 minutes, then brush on another third and continue. Glazing in stages builds layers instead of one thin varnish that burns.

  9. Bake until the outside is deeply browned, the glaze has caught and darkened at the edges, and a probe in the very centre reads an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C). Ground meat of any kind gets that number, and a meatloaf is the definition of ground meat.

  10. Brush the last of the glaze on and rest the loaf on the pan for 15 minutes before slicing. Cut too early and it crumbles apart along the pan.

  11. What goes wrong: not enough fat. Straight venison with no pork produces a loaf that is crumbly and dry no matter how much bread is in it. The pork is structural.

Notes

  • Bacon draped over the top is traditional and works, but it shields the surface from browning. Weave it thin or skip it and rely on the glaze.
  • Cold meatloaf sandwiches on white bread with more ketchup are the actual reason to make a big one.

Keeping it

Fridge four days, freezer three months, sliced and wrapped in portions. Reheat slices in a covered pan with a splash of stock rather than in a microwave.
