Game & The Freezer

Pot Pie Filling, Made to Freeze

Build the filling now, put a crust on it some rainy evening later.

About 3 quarts, enough for three 9 inch pies
Makes
50 minutes
Hands on
3 hours
Start to finish

A pot pie is two separate projects that people insist on doing on the same night. Split them up. The filling is a braise that improves with a day in the fridge and freezes without complaint, and the pastry takes twenty minutes whenever you feel like it.

The one thing a filling for freezing needs that a filling for tonight does not is to be slightly thicker and slightly under-seasoned, because it will loosen as it thaws and concentrate as it bakes under a lid of pastry.

What you need

Meat

  • 3 lb (1.4 kg) venison or elk shoulder or neck, in 1 inch cubes
  • 2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1/3 cup all-purpose flour
  • 4 tbsp beef tallow or neutral oil

Braise

  • 2 onions, diced
  • 3 carrots, diced
  • 3 stalks celery, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 cup dry white wine
  • 5 cups beef or game stock
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 2 bay leaves

Late additions

  • 1 lb (450 g) waxy potatoes, in 1/2 inch dice
  • 8 oz (225 g) mushrooms, quartered and browned
  • 1 cup frozen peas
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 4 tbsp butter mashed with 4 tbsp flour
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar

How

  1. Season and flour the meat, then brown it hard in batches in the tallow until each piece has a dark crust and the bottom of the pot is brown and sticky. Crowding the pot steams the meat and you lose the flavour you came for.

  2. Set the meat aside and cook the onion, carrot and celery in the same fat until soft and lightly coloured, then add garlic and tomato paste and stir until the paste darkens to brick.

  3. Deglaze with the wine, scraping the pot clean, and reduce it by half.

  4. Return the meat, add stock, thyme and bay, and hold it at a bare simmer, covered, until a cube crushes easily against the side of the pot with a spoon. Expect two to two and a half hours; a boil at any point strings the meat out.

  5. Add the potatoes and cook until they are tender to a knife tip but not falling apart, about 20 minutes. They finish cooking again inside the pie, so stop a little short.

  6. Brown the mushrooms separately in a dry hot pan until they squeak and then colour, and stir them in. Raw mushrooms added to the pot just release water.

  7. Stir in the butter and flour paste a lump at a time and simmer until the filling thickens enough that a spoon dragged across the bottom leaves a track that closes slowly. It should be noticeably thicker than a stew.

  8. Add the cream, peas, parsley and vinegar off the heat. Taste for salt, and stop a shade under where you want it.

  9. Cool the filling completely, spread in a shallow pan so it drops through the warm range quickly, before it goes anywhere near a freezer. Portion into quart containers.

  10. To use: thaw in the fridge overnight, put it in a pie dish, top with pastry, cut vents, and bake at 400°F (205°C) until the pastry is deep golden and the filling is bubbling up visibly through the vents. A probe into the centre should read an internal temperature of at least 165°F (74°C) so you know the middle is properly hot, not just the edges.

  11. What goes wrong: a thin filling under a crust. It boils up, soaks the pastry from below, and you serve soup with a soggy lid. Thicker than feels right is the correct amount.

Notes

  • This is a braise, which means the meat goes far past any internal temperature threshold in the first hour and then spends two more converting connective tissue. The texture is the test, not the number.
  • Any tough cut works. Shanks, boned out, make the best version of this in the collection.

Keeping it

Fridge four days, freezer four months in sealed containers with as little headspace as you can manage. Label the container, because everything in a freezer looks like everything else.
