Game & The Freezer

A Venison Roast That Is Not Dry

A lean roast wants a low oven, a probe, and someone willing to take it out early.

Serves 6 to 8
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
2 hours 30 minutes
Start to finish

A whole-muscle roast off the hind quarter, the top round or the sirloin tip, is one of the best things on a deer and one of the most commonly ruined. It has no marbling and no fat cap, so there is nothing basting it from inside, and the difference between rosy and grey is about fifteen minutes in a conventional oven.

A low oven narrows that gap by cooking the whole roast at nearly the same rate rather than in layers. Add a fat cap you supply yourself, a probe left in the meat, and a proper rest, and it behaves.

What you need

Roast

  • 1 venison or elk roast, 3 to 4 lb (1.4 to 1.8 kg), silverskin removed, tied
  • 1 tbsp coarse salt
  • 2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper
  • 6 slices bacon or a sheet of pork back fat
  • 6 lengths kitchen twine

Rub and pan

  • 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp chopped rosemary
  • 1 tbsp chopped thyme
  • 2 tbsp beef tallow
  • 2 onions, thickly sliced
  • 3 carrots, in chunks

Gravy

  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup red wine
  • 2 cups beef or game stock
  • 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp cold butter

How

  1. Salt the roast all over and leave it uncovered on a rack in the fridge overnight if you can, or at least two hours. Dry brining seasons the interior and dries the surface so it browns.

  2. Tie the roast into an even cylinder with twine at inch intervals. An untied roast has a thin flapping end that is grey before the middle is warm.

  3. Heat the oven to 250°F (120°C). Smear the roast with mustard, garlic and herbs, then drape the bacon or fat over the top and tie it in place.

  4. Scatter onions and carrots in a roasting pan, set the roast on top, and push a probe into the very centre along the long axis.

  5. Roast slowly. On a roast this size in a low oven, expect somewhere between an hour and a half and two hours, but the probe is the only thing that decides. The exterior will look pale for most of that time and that is correct.

  6. Pull the roast at an internal temperature of 125 to 128 degrees Fahrenheit (52 to 53°C) if you want medium rare after carryover, or 135 degrees Fahrenheit (57°C) for medium. Whole-muscle venison and elk take the same latitude as whole-muscle beef; only ground meat needs 160°F (71°C).

  7. Rest the roast, loosely tented, for a full 20 minutes. It will climb five to eight degrees as it sits, and the juices redistribute instead of running out onto the board.

  8. Optional and worth it: while it rests, crank the oven to 500°F (260°C), then return the roast for four or five minutes to blister the surface. A quick blast at the end browns without adding meaningful heat to the middle.

  9. For the gravy, spoon most of the fat out of the pan, mash the vegetables slightly, and stir in flour over medium heat until it smells toasted. Add wine, scrape the pan, reduce until nearly dry, then add stock and simmer until it coats a spoon.

  10. Strain the gravy, finish with vinegar and cold butter off the heat, and carve the roast across the grain in thin slices.

  11. What goes wrong: an oven at 350°F (175°C) and no thermometer. That combination cooks a two inch band of grey meat around a raw core and there is no way to fix it after the fact.

Notes

  • Cuts from the round can be slightly tough even at the right temperature. Slice them thinner and across the grain and they eat like a much more expensive piece of meat.
  • If the animal was old or hard hunted, braise the roast instead. No oven temperature makes a tough muscle tender in two hours.

Keeping it

Fridge four days. Cold roast sliced paper thin is the best sandwich in this collection. Reheat only in gravy, gently, or not at all.
