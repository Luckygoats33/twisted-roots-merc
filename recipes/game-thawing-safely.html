Game & The Freezer

Thawing Meat Without Ruining It

The counter is not a thawing method. It is a bacteria incubator with a nice view.

One package, thawed properly
Makes
5 minutes
Hands on
20 minutes to 3 days depending on method
Start to finish

Thawing on the counter is the most common food handling mistake in home kitchens, and it is easy to see why: it is free and it works. The problem is that the outside of a roast sits in the range where bacteria multiply fastest for hours while the middle is still frozen solid, and you cannot cook that away from a whole muscle you are planning to serve pink.

Three methods that work, in order of how much they cost you in planning. There is no fourth one, and the microwave barely counts.

What you need

What you need

  • 1 tray or sheet pan to catch drips
  • 1 refrigerator running at 40 degrees Fahrenheit (4°C) or below
  • 1 large bowl or a clean sink for the water method
  • 1 probe thermometer
  • 2 zip bags in case a package leaks

How

  1. The fridge method, which is the best one. Move the package to a tray on the bottom shelf and leave it. Figure roughly a day for every five pounds, so a small roast is overnight and a turkey-sized piece of elk is three days. It is slow, it is safe, and the meat loses the least liquid.

  2. Put it on a tray. Packages leak, and raw meat drip on a shelf above vegetables is how a small mistake becomes a bigger one.

  3. The cold water method, when you forgot. Keep the meat sealed in a watertight bag, submerge it fully in cold tap water, and change the water every 30 minutes. A pound takes about an hour. The bag must not leak, or you are washing the meat and the sink at the same time.

  4. Never use warm or hot water. It thaws the surface into exactly the temperature range you are trying to avoid while the core is still solid, and it starts cooking the outer layer into a grey rim.

  5. The straight from frozen method, which is underrated. Ground meat, stew cubes and thin steaks can go into a hot pan or a braise frozen. It takes about half again as long and it works, particularly for anything that was going to be cooked through anyway.

  6. Whatever thaws in cold water or a microwave gets cooked immediately, not put back in the fridge for tomorrow. Both methods leave parts of the meat warm enough that the clock has already started.

  7. Meat thawed properly in the fridge, still cold to the touch, can be refrozen. You will lose some texture to a second round of ice crystals but it is safe. Meat thawed on a counter should not be refrozen.

  8. Cook it to the right number for what it is. Whole-muscle venison and elk follow steak rules at an internal temperature of 130 to 135 degrees Fahrenheit (54 to 57°C) for medium rare. Anything ground goes to 160°F (71°C) without exception, because grinding puts the surface through the middle.

  9. Judge whether it is fully thawed by pressing the centre. It should give evenly with no hard core. A roast with a frozen middle roasts into a grey shell around a raw centre, which is the other reason to plan ahead.

  10. If a package thawed and you are not sure how long it sat out, the honest answer is that you cannot cook your way back from several warm hours on whole muscle. Use judgement, and when in doubt do not serve it to people who did not get a vote.

Notes

  • Vacuum sealed packages thaw faster in water than paper wrapped ones, because the plastic sits right against the meat and conducts.
  • Frozen meat that has partly thawed and refrozen shows up as a large ice crystal layer and a stained package. It is a quality problem rather than an automatic safety one, but it is worth knowing what you are looking at.

Keeping it

Thawed raw whole-muscle game keeps three to five days in the fridge, ground meat one to two. Cook or refreeze within that window.
