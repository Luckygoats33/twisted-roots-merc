Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Apple Butter

Hours of nothing happening, then suddenly it is dark and thick and smells like the whole house.

About 6 half-pint jars
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
8 hours, mostly unattended
Start to finish

Apple butter is apple sauce cooked until the sugars caramelise and the water is gone, which takes far longer than seems reasonable and cannot be rushed with higher heat without scorching. There is no butter in it. The name refers to the texture, which should be spreadable and completely smooth with no grain at all.

Apples are high-acid fruit, so a boiling water bath is the correct process. Low-acid foods, meaning plain vegetables, meats, fish and soups, are never water bath safe and require a pressure canner. Fruit butters sit safely on the right side of that line, which is why this one can be canned at all.

What you need

Apple butter

  • 6 lb (2.7 kg) apples, cored and chopped, peels on
  • 2 cups apple cider or water
  • 2 cups granulated sugar
  • 1 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/4 cup bottled lemon juice
  • 1 tbsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp ground allspice
  • 1/2 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt

Equipment

  • 1 heavy pot or a slow cooker
  • 1 food mill or an immersion blender
  • 6 half-pint jars with new lids
  • 1 boiling water canner with a rack
  • 1 copy of the current USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning, or OSU Extension

How

  1. Get the tested processing time before you begin, from the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning or from OSU Extension. It depends on jar size and altitude, and this page does not print one.

  2. Note the altitude question. Siletz is near sea level and the base figures apply here, but water boils cooler as you climb and the tested tables add time accordingly. Use the line for your own elevation, not somebody else's.

  3. Use a mix of apples. Tart ones for flavour, sweet ones for body. Leave the peels on while cooking; they carry pectin and colour and they come out in the mill.

  4. Cook the apples with the cider, covered, over medium-low heat until they have completely collapsed and a spoon meets no resistance at all, about 40 minutes.

  5. Pass everything through a food mill to remove peel and any remaining core, or blend it smooth if you peeled the apples first. The texture should be completely without grain.

  6. Return the puree to the pot with both sugars, lemon juice, spices and salt, and stir until the sugar has dissolved.

  7. Now cook it down, uncovered, over the lowest heat that keeps it moving, stirring often and scraping the bottom. This takes four to six hours on a stove or overnight in a slow cooker with the lid propped. It will spit; a splatter screen is worth it.

  8. It is done when the volume has roughly halved, the colour has gone from pale to a deep mahogany brown, and a spoonful mounded on a cold plate holds its shape with no ring of liquid seeping out around it. That ring test is the whole doneness cue and it is more reliable than any timing.

  9. A thermometer is of limited use here. Fruit butter finishes below the 220°F (105°C) gel stage of a jam because it is thickened by evaporation rather than by sugar concentration, so the plate test is what you go by.

  10. Taste and adjust the spice at the end. Flavours concentrate enormously during the long cook and a mixture that seemed well spiced at the start can be overwhelming by the finish.

  11. Ladle boiling hot into hot jars, leaving a quarter inch of headspace, remove bubbles, wipe the rims until they squeak, and fit lids and rings finger tight.

  12. Process in the boiling water canner for the tested time, with jars covered by at least an inch of water, timed from a full rolling boil. Rest, lift out, cool overnight, and check every seal the next day.

  13. What goes wrong: scorching. Once apple butter catches on the bottom of the pot, the burnt flavour goes through the entire batch within minutes and cannot be stirred out. Low heat and a heavy pot are not suggestions.

Notes

  • A slow cooker with the lid set slightly ajar is the low-effort version and it is genuinely good. It just takes all night.
  • Nobody here has cooked your batch. Use a tested process, and if a jar looks or smells wrong on opening, dispose of it without tasting.

Keeping it

Sealed jars, rings removed, a year in a cool dark cupboard. Opened, refrigerate and use within about a month. Unprocessed apple butter is a fridge product only and keeps three weeks.
