Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Apple Pie

Two kinds of apple, a cooked filling, and no gap between the fruit and the lid.

One 9 inch double-crust pie
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
5 hours including cooling
Start to finish

The classic apple pie failure is the dome: a beautifully browned top crust arched over an inch of empty air, with the fruit collapsed at the bottom. It happens because raw apples are mostly water held in rigid cells, and those cells give way all at once in the oven, after the crust has already set in the shape you built it.

Precooking the filling solves it. The apples release their water on the stove where you can see it, the pieces firm up, and what goes into the shell is very close to what comes out. It also lets you thicken the juice accurately instead of guessing.

What you need

Filling

  • 3 lb (1.4 kg) apples, half firm tart and half sweet, peeled and sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • 3 tbsp cornstarch mixed with 3 tbsp water
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1.5 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Pastry

  • 1 batch pie dough, two discs, chilled
  • 1 large egg beaten with 1 tbsp cream
  • 2 tbsp coarse sugar

How

  1. Use two kinds of apple. A firm tart one that holds its shape, such as Granny Smith or a Gravenstein, and a softer sweet one that collapses into sauce and fills the gaps. All of one kind gives you either apple gravel or apple sauce.

  2. Melt the butter in a wide pan, add the apples, both sugars, lemon, spices and salt, and cook over medium heat, stirring gently.

  3. Cook until the apples have released a good pool of liquid, softened at the edges, and a slice bends without snapping but still holds together when you lift it. That is roughly 8 to 10 minutes and it is a texture, not a time.

  4. Push the apples to one side, stir the cornstarch slurry into the liquid, and cook until it turns from cloudy to glossy and thickens enough to coat the fruit. Stir in the vanilla off the heat.

  5. Cool the filling completely, spread on a sheet pan to speed it up. Warm filling melts the bottom crust before it ever reaches the oven, and this is the most common reason for a soggy base.

  6. Line the pan with the bottom crust and chill it. Fill with the cold apples, packing them in and pressing down so there are no voids, and pour any remaining thickened juice over.

  7. Lay on the top crust, seal, crimp, and cut four generous vents. Freeze the pie 20 minutes, then brush with egg wash and sugar it.

  8. Bake at 425°F (220°C) on a preheated sheet pan on the lowest rack for 20 minutes, then drop to 375°F (190°C).

  9. Continue until the crust is a deep even brown, including the base, which you can check by lifting the pie and looking, and juice is bubbling slowly through the vents. Thick lazy bubbles, not thin fast ones.

  10. An instant-read thermometer in the centre should reach about 200°F (93°C). Because the filling went in precooked, this happens sooner than it would otherwise, and the crust is the limiting factor rather than the fruit.

  11. Cool at least four hours before cutting. The starch is still setting while it cools and a warm slice will slump.

  12. What goes wrong, besides the dome: watery apples like Red Delicious, which turn to mush and give up more liquid than any amount of starch will hold. Buy apples that are meant for baking.

Notes

  • A grating of sharp cheddar into the top crust dough is an old habit in this part of the country and it is very good.
  • Cooking the filling the day before makes this a much easier project. It keeps three days in the fridge.

Keeping it

Two days at room temperature under a cloth, three more in the fridge. Warm slices briefly in a 325°F (165°C) oven rather than a microwave, which turns the crust to leather.
