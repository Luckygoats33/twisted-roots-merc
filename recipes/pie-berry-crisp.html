Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Berry Crisp

Oats, brown sugar, cold butter, and no pastry to worry about at all.

One 9 by 13 inch pan, serves 10
Makes
20 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes
Start to finish

A crisp is what you make when you want the fruit without the commitment. There is no dough to chill, no lattice to weave, and it works with any berry, any stone fruit and most combinations of the two. The topping is a streusel with oats in it, which is what makes it crisp rather than merely crumbly.

The one place people go wrong is the ratio of topping to fruit. Too thin and it disappears into the juice; too thick and the middle never crisps because it is steaming from below. About three quarters of an inch, rubbled rather than spread, is the right amount.

What you need

Fruit

  • 8 cups mixed berries, about 1.1 kg, fresh or frozen
  • 2/3 cup granulated sugar
  • 3 tbsp cornstarch
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1/4 tsp fine sea salt

Topping

  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, 130 g
  • 1 cup rolled oats, not instant
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 cup chopped hazelnuts or walnuts
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 170 g cold butter, in cubes

How

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C). Butter a 9 by 13 inch dish and set it on a rimmed sheet pan to catch the overflow, because there will be some.

  2. Toss the berries with sugar, cornstarch, lemon and salt and spread them in the dish. If the fruit is frozen, use it frozen and add 10 minutes to the bake.

  3. Combine the flour, oats, brown sugar, nuts, cinnamon and salt in a bowl and add the cold butter.

  4. Rub the butter in with your fingertips until the mixture holds together in clumps when you squeeze a handful, with pieces ranging from crumbs to hazelnut-sized lumps. Uniform crumbs bake into a sandy layer; irregular ones bake into something with texture.

  5. Scatter the topping over the fruit in rubble rather than pressing it into a lid, leaving a few gaps for steam.

  6. Bake until the topping is deep golden and firm to a fingertip, the nuts smell toasted, and the fruit is bubbling thickly up around the edges and through the gaps.

  7. If you want the number, an instant-read thermometer pushed into the fruit should read close to 200°F (93°C), which is where cornstarch finishes thickening. Below that the juices stay thin no matter how brown the top looks.

  8. If the topping browns before the fruit is bubbling, tent it loosely with foil and keep going. Brown topping over cold fruit is the most common way this comes out wrong.

  9. Rest 20 minutes before serving. It sets noticeably as it cools and it is far too hot to eat straight out of the oven anyway.

Notes

  • The topping keeps in a bag in the freezer for three months and goes straight from frozen onto fruit, which makes this a fifteen minute dessert.
  • Hazelnuts are the local nut and the better choice here. Toast them first if you want the flavour to carry.

Keeping it

Covered at room temperature two days, four in the fridge. Reheat in a hot oven to bring the topping back; a microwave makes it soggy in seconds.
