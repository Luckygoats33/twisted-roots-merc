Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

A Lattice Top

Twenty minutes of weaving, and the vents are the functional part.

One 9 inch lattice top
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
1 hour with the chills
Start to finish

A lattice is not only decoration. A wet berry pie under a solid lid traps steam, the filling boils rather than reduces, and the top crust steams from below into something pale and damp. A lattice lets water leave, which concentrates the filling and browns the strips from both sides.

The weaving is easier than it looks and impossible if the dough is warm. Everything here is a rhythm of roll, chill, weave, chill, and the chilling is not padding.

What you need

What you need

  • 1 disc pie dough, chilled
  • 1 filled pie shell, cold
  • 2 tbsp flour for the counter
  • 1 large egg beaten with 1 tbsp cream
  • 2 tbsp coarse sugar

Tools

  • 1 ruler or a straight-edged pastry cutter
  • 1 rimmed baking sheet lined with parchment
  • 2 sheets of parchment for chilling the strips

How

  1. Roll the dough into a rectangle a little larger than the pie, about an eighth of an inch thick. Slide it, on parchment, onto a sheet pan and refrigerate 15 minutes until firm.

  2. Cut strips with a ruler and a sharp knife or a fluted wheel. Anywhere from half an inch to an inch wide, but pick one width and stay with it; uneven strips look accidental rather than rustic.

  3. Chill the cut strips again on the tray. Warm strips stretch as you lift them and end up thin and torn.

  4. Lay every second strip across the filled pie in one direction, evenly spaced, and leave the alternates aside.

  5. Fold back every other strip halfway. Lay one strip across the pie perpendicular to them, then unfold the folded strips back over it.

  6. Now fold back the strips that were underneath, lay the next perpendicular strip, and unfold. Repeat outward from the middle in both directions. That alternating fold is the entire technique, and once your hands do it once they remember.

  7. Trim the ends to about half an inch beyond the pan, then fold the bottom crust overhang up over them and crimp the whole rim together. That seal is what keeps the filling from escaping under the strips.

  8. Chill the assembled pie in the freezer for 20 minutes. This is the step that separates a crisp defined lattice from one that spreads and merges into a lid.

  9. Brush with egg wash, avoiding puddles in the gaps, and scatter coarse sugar over.

  10. Bake starting hot, around 425°F (220°C), until the lattice is set and beginning to colour, then drop to 375°F (190°C) and continue until the strips are a deep golden brown and the filling is bubbling thickly through the gaps in the centre of the pie, not just around the rim.

  11. If you want a number, an instant-read thermometer in the middle of the filling should be near 200°F (93°C), which is where fruit starch has finished doing its work.

  12. What goes wrong: weaving on a warm counter in a warm kitchen. If the strips start feeling greasy and stretchy at any point, put the whole tray in the fridge and go do something else for ten minutes.

Notes

  • A woven lattice looks better than strips simply laid over each other, and the difference is about four minutes of work.
  • For a shortcut, cut wide strips and lay them in a simple over-and-under grid. Nobody has ever complained about a pie because of the weave.

Keeping it

An assembled unbaked lattice pie freezes solid on a tray, then wraps and keeps three months. Bake it from frozen, adding time, and it is arguably better than one baked fresh.
