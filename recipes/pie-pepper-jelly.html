Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Pepper Jelly

Sweet, hot, and acid enough for a water bath because of the vinegar, not in spite of it.

About 6 half-pint jars
Makes
50 minutes
Hands on
About 3 hours
Start to finish

Pepper jelly is a jelly made from peppers, sugar, pectin and a serious quantity of vinegar, and that vinegar is not a flavouring decision. Peppers on their own are a low-acid vegetable and are not safe in a boiling water bath. The added vinegar is what pushes the finished product into the high-acid range where water bath processing works.

Which means the one thing you must never do is reduce the vinegar because the jelly tastes sharp. Adjust the sugar, adjust the peppers, adjust the heat, but leave the acid alone and use a tested recipe's ratio.

What you need

Jelly

  • 2 cups finely chopped red and green bell peppers, seeds out
  • 1/2 cup finely chopped jalapenos or hotter chiles, to taste
  • 1.5 cups cider vinegar of at least 5 percent acidity
  • 6 cups granulated sugar
  • 1 packet liquid pectin, 3 oz (85 g)
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tbsp butter, optional, for foam

Equipment

  • 1 heavy wide pot, at least 6 quarts
  • 6 half-pint jars with new lids and sound rings
  • 1 boiling water canner with a rack
  • 1 jam thermometer
  • 2 pairs of gloves for handling chiles
  • 1 copy of the current USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning, or your OSU Extension office

How

  1. Get the tested processing time before you start, from the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning or from OSU Extension, for your jar size. It also varies by altitude, and this page prints no number.

  2. Note your elevation. Siletz is near sea level so the base figures apply here, but water boils cooler higher up and the tested tables add time to compensate. Look yours up rather than borrowing somebody else's.

  3. Understand the acid. Peppers are low-acid and would need a pressure canner on their own. The vinegar at the stated strength and quantity is what makes this water bath safe, so use vinegar labelled at 5 percent acidity and do not dilute or reduce it.

  4. Wear gloves to chop the chiles and keep your hands away from your face. This is obvious advice that people ignore once each.

  5. Chop the peppers very fine, by hand or with a few pulses in a food processor, stopping well short of a puree. You want visible flecks suspended in clear jelly.

  6. Combine peppers, vinegar, sugar and salt in the pot and bring it to a boil over high heat, stirring until the sugar has completely dissolved and the liquid runs clear rather than cloudy off a spoon.

  7. Boil hard for 5 minutes, stirring, then add the liquid pectin all at once and return it to a full rolling boil that cannot be stirred down. Boil exactly one minute and take it off the heat.

  8. Skim the foam. At this point the jelly will be very loose and that is correct; liquid pectin sets as it cools rather than by boiling down, so do not keep cooking it hoping for a gel stage at 220°F (105°C) the way you would with jam.

  9. Let the pot stand 5 minutes and stir once. This stops all the pepper floating to the top of every jar, which is the classic cosmetic failure of pepper jelly.

  10. Ladle into hot jars leaving a quarter inch of headspace, debubble, wipe the rims until they squeak, and fit lids and rings finger tight.

  11. Process in the boiling water canner for the tested time, jars covered by an inch of water, timed from the return to a full rolling boil. Rest 5 minutes, lift out, and leave them overnight.

  12. Check every seal the next day and refrigerate anything that failed. The jelly firms fully over about two days, so do not panic on the first night.

  13. What goes wrong: a jelly that stays syrupy. Usually the pectin was old, or it was boiled for longer than a minute after the pectin went in, which breaks it down.

Notes

  • Never describe a home canning recipe as safe on the strength of one batch. Use tested recipes, and dispose of any jar that is bulging or smells wrong without tasting it.
  • Over cream cheese with crackers is the standard use, and it is standard because it works.

Keeping it

Sealed jars, rings off, a year in a cool dark cupboard. Opened, refrigerate and use within about two months.
