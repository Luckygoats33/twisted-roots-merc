Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Quick Pickled Onions

Ten minutes of work, an hour of waiting, and they go on everything for the next month.

2 pint jars
Makes
10 minutes
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

Pink pickled onions are the single highest return per minute of anything in this collection. They cut through the fat in a taco, a crab melt, a venison sandwich and a bowl of chowder, and they take ten minutes of actual work. The colour comes from the onion reacting with the acid, and it happens on its own within the hour.

Be clear about what this is: a refrigerator pickle. It is not processed, it is not shelf stable, and it lives in the fridge. That is not a shortcut, it is a different category of preserve with its own honest shelf life.

What you need

Onions

  • 3 large red onions, sliced paper thin
  • 2 cups boiling water for blanching, optional

Brine

  • 1.5 cups white wine vinegar or cider vinegar at 5 percent acidity
  • 1.5 cups water
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1 tbsp fine sea salt

Aromatics

  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp black peppercorns
  • 2 tsp coriander seed
  • 4 cloves garlic, halved
  • 2 dried chiles, optional
  • 1 tsp dried oregano

How

  1. Slice the onions as thinly as you can manage, ideally on a mandoline. Thick slices stay harsh and crunchy in the middle and never take the brine evenly.

  2. If you want a milder result, pour boiling water over the sliced onions in a sieve and drain them immediately. This knocks the raw sulphurous edge off without cooking them.

  3. Pack the onions into two clean pint jars along with the bay, peppercorns, coriander, garlic, chiles and oregano, pressing them down firmly.

  4. Combine the vinegar, water, sugar and salt in a small pan and heat, stirring, until the sugar and salt have completely dissolved and the liquid is steaming but not yet at a rolling boil.

  5. There is no internal temperature to chase here. The brine only has to be hot enough to dissolve the salt and sugar and to soften the onion slightly; it is the acid, not the heat, doing the preserving.

  6. Pour the hot brine over the onions to cover them completely. Anything sticking above the liquid discolours and goes soft.

  7. Press the onions down with a spoon to release trapped air, let the jars cool on the counter for about an hour, and watch the colour go from purple to bright pink.

  8. Lid them and refrigerate. They are edible after an hour, good after four, and best after a day.

  9. On canning these: it is possible to process pickled onions in a boiling water bath, because added vinegar puts them in the high-acid range. But do that only with a tested recipe, with its stated vinegar strength, brine ratio, jar size and processing time, from the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning or OSU Extension. Do not water bath this refrigerator recipe on the assumption that it is close enough.

  10. Altitude affects any water bath process, and the tested tables account for it. Siletz is near sea level; a great many readers are not, and the correct time is the one for your own elevation.

  11. What goes wrong: a weak brine. Cutting the vinegar with more water because the smell is strong gives you a pickle that goes slimy in the fridge in a fortnight.

Notes

  • This is a refrigerator pickle and it has not been processed. It is not shelf stable and it must not be stored in a cupboard.
  • The leftover brine is excellent in a vinaigrette, and it will pickle a second, smaller batch of onions before it gives up.

Keeping it

In the fridge, sealed, about a month. They soften gradually after two weeks and are still perfectly good, just less crisp. Discard if the brine turns cloudy or ropey.
