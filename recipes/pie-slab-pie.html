Pie, Cobbler & Preserves

Slab Pie

Pie for twenty, in a sheet pan, cut in squares like a sensible person.

One half sheet pan, 24 squares
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
5 hours including cooling
Start to finish

A slab pie is a pie rolled thin in a rimmed sheet pan, and its whole advantage is ratio. Because the layer of fruit is shallow, every square has considerably more crust per bite than a wedge from a deep pie, which is either the best or the worst thing about it depending on which part you like.

It also feeds a crowd from one oven, cuts into neat squares, and travels to a potluck without anybody having to think about a pie server. The shallow filling cooks faster and thickens more reliably, which makes it a more forgiving project than a deep dish pie despite looking like more work.

What you need

Pastry

  • 1 double batch pie dough, made as two large discs
  • 3 tbsp flour for rolling
  • 1 large egg beaten with 2 tbsp cream
  • 3 tbsp coarse sugar

Filling

  • 10 cups berries or sliced fruit, about 1.4 kg
  • 1.25 cups granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup cornstarch
  • 3 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon, for apple versions
  • 2 tbsp butter, in small pieces

Optional glaze

  • 1.5 cups powdered sugar
  • 3 tbsp milk
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

How

  1. Roll the first disc into a rectangle about eighteen by thirteen inches, which is bigger than it sounds and needs a clear counter. Roll it around the pin to move it, and unroll it over a half sheet pan.

  2. Ease it into the corners without stretching, letting the excess hang over the rim, and chill the lined pan while you make the filling.

  3. Toss the fruit with sugar, cornstarch, lemon and salt. Because the layer is shallow, you need proportionally more starch than a deep pie, which is why the amount looks high.

  4. Spread the fruit in an even layer right to the corners and dot the butter over it. An even layer is what gives you squares that all set to the same firmness.

  5. Roll the second disc to the same size, lay it over, trim to about half an inch beyond the pan, and fold the two edges together and crimp against the rim.

  6. Cut vents generously across the whole top, at least a dozen, or lay a lattice. A large surface area needs a lot of vents.

  7. Freeze the assembled pan 20 minutes, then egg wash and sugar the top.

  8. Bake at 400°F (205°C) until the crust is deep golden brown across the whole surface and the filling is bubbling thickly through the vents in the middle of the pan, not just at the ends. Expect somewhere near 45 minutes and check the middle, which is always last.

  9. An instant-read thermometer in the centre of the filling should read close to 200°F (93°C), which is where the cornstarch has finished setting.

  10. Cool completely in the pan on a rack, at least three hours. A slab pie cut warm slides apart, and the whole point is neat squares.

  11. Glaze if you like, then cut into squares with a bench scraper rather than a knife, pressing straight down.

  12. What goes wrong: an uneven fruit layer. Thin patches burn, thick patches stay loose, and it shows immediately when you cut it.

Notes

  • A quarter sheet pan version halves everything and fits a smaller oven and a smaller gathering.
  • This is the correct format for a bake sale, a wake, or anything else where somebody needs to feed a room without plates.

Keeping it

Covered at room temperature two days, four in the fridge. It travels better than any other pie in this collection and it is the only one you can stack.
