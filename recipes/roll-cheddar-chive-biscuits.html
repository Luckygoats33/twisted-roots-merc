Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Cheddar And Chive Biscuits

Sharp cheddar cut in with the butter, salt dialled back, baked cooler so the cheese does not burn.

Eight to nine biscuits
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
50 minutes
Start to finish

Cheese is about a third fat and carries a fair amount of salt, so a cheddar biscuit made by adding cheese to a plain recipe comes out heavy and over-seasoned. Two adjustments fix it: less butter, because the cheese brings its own, and less salt for the same reason. Grate the cheese coarse and cut it in with the butter rather than folding it in at the end, so it is distributed as pieces that melt into pockets. Bake it at 425 rather than 450 — exposed cheese scorches and turns bitter well before a biscuit is done at the higher heat.

What you need

Dough

  • 420 g all-purpose flour (about 3 1/2 cups)
  • 16 g baking powder (about 3 1/2 tsp)
  • 2 g baking soda (about 1/3 tsp)
  • 7 g fine salt (about 1 1/4 tsp)
  • 140 g cold unsalted butter, cubed (about 10 tbsp)
  • 170 g sharp cheddar, coarsely grated (about 1 3/4 cups)
  • 15 g chives, chopped (about 3 tbsp)
  • 290 g cold buttermilk (about 1 1/4 cups)

To finish

  • 20 g melted butter (about 1 1/2 tbsp)
  • 1/2 tsp cracked black pepper

How

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Whisk the flour, baking powder, soda and salt.

  2. Cut in the cold butter until the pieces are hazelnut sized and irregular.

  3. Toss through the grated cheddar and chives so every shred is coated in flour. Coated cheese stays where you put it; uncoated cheese clumps and runs out the side during the bake.

  4. Add the buttermilk and stir with a fork until just combined. Fat to flour here is 1:3 before the cheese and closer to 1:2.1 once you count the cheese fat, which is why the butter is down from the house recipe.

  5. Pat out to 2 cm on a floured counter, fold in thirds twice, then pat to 2.5 cm.

  6. Cut with a floured cutter pressed straight down, and set them on parchment with the sides just touching. Pick off any cheese sitting on the outside — it will scorch and stick.

  7. Brush with melted butter and grind pepper over the tops.

  8. Bake 18 to 22 minutes, until well risen, deep gold, and the cheese that has leaked at the edges is browned and lacy rather than black. An instant-read reads 205°F (96°C).

  9. Cool five minutes on the tray so the melted cheese sets and they lift off cleanly.

  10. If they came out squat and greasy, the cheese was soft or finely grated. Use it cold and coarse.

Notes

  • An aged cheddar gives more flavour per gram than a mild one, which means you use less of it and the dough stays light.
  • Spring onion greens work if there are no chives. Anything wetter than that — grated courgette, fresh tomato — will not.

Keeping it

Two days in a tin. Split and toast them cut side down in a dry pan until the cheese sizzles again.
