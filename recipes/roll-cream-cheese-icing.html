Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Cream Cheese Icing

The icing for the cinnamon rolls, with the temperature rules that decide whether it melts in or sits on top.

About 670 g, enough for two pans of rolls
Makes
10 minutes
Hands on
10 minutes
Start to finish

There are two icings, and which one you want depends on when you put it on. Applied to a roll straight from the oven it melts and soaks in and becomes part of the thing, sweet and glossy and invisible. Applied to a cold roll it sits on top in a thick pale layer and stays there. Both are legitimate and the recipe is the same; the only variable is the temperature of what is underneath. The mistake is doing it at the temperature in between, where it half-melts and slides off the sides into a puddle.

What you need

Icing

  • 225 g full-fat cream cheese, at cool room temperature (about 8 oz)
  • 115 g unsalted butter, soft (about 8 tbsp)
  • 300 g icing sugar, sifted (about 2 1/2 cups)
  • 30 g sour cream (about 2 tbsp)
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • A pinch of fine salt

How

  1. Get the temperatures right first. Both the butter and the cream cheese should be around 18°C — soft enough to dent under a finger, firm enough to hold a shape. An instant-read pushed into the middle of the block is the least argumentative way to know. Cold cream cheese gives you lumps that never beat out; warm cream cheese gives you a soup that no amount of sugar will thicken.

  2. Beat the butter alone for a minute until it is pale and smooth. Doing the butter first is what stops the lumps.

  3. Add the cream cheese and beat just until combined, 30 seconds or so. Cream cheese thins when it is over-beaten, because the emulsion breaks. This is the single most common way this icing is ruined.

  4. Add the sifted icing sugar in two lots on low speed, then the sour cream, vanilla and salt. Beat 20 seconds more, no longer.

  5. Check the consistency. It should fall off a spoon in a thick ribbon that holds for a second before flattening. Too stiff, add sour cream 10 g at a time. Too loose, chill it for 20 minutes rather than adding more sugar, which only makes it sweeter and stickier.

  6. For icing that melts in: spread it on rolls that have rested 10 minutes out of the oven, still hot enough to steam. For icing that sits on top: wait until they are completely cool, and use it straight from the fridge.

  7. The pinch of salt and the sour cream are both there to cut the sweetness. Leave them out and it tastes like frosting from a tub.

Notes

  • Full-fat block cream cheese only. The spreadable kind in a tub has added water and gums and it will not set.
  • For a thinner glaze for scones or buns, use 100 g cream cheese, 200 g icing sugar and 40 g milk instead.

Keeping it

Five days in the fridge in a sealed tub. Bring it back to about 18°C and beat it briefly before using, or it will tear the surface of whatever you spread it on.
