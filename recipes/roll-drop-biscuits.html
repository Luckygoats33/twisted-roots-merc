Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Drop Biscuits

No cutter, no folding, no counter. Twenty-five minutes from cupboard to table.

Ten biscuits
Makes
10 minutes
Hands on
25 minutes
Start to finish

A drop biscuit is a wetter dough spooned straight onto a tray, and it trades layers for speed and a craggy top that catches butter. Because there is no rolling, there is no gluten development to worry about and no reason to keep the fat in pieces, so this version uses melted butter, which makes it a one-bowl job with a fork. It is the thing to make when soup is nearly ready and there is no bread in the house, which on a wet evening is most evenings.

What you need

Dough

  • 300 g all-purpose flour (about 2 1/2 cups)
  • 12 g baking powder (about 2 3/4 tsp)
  • 2 g baking soda (about 1/3 tsp)
  • 6 g fine salt (about 1 tsp)
  • 8 g sugar (about 2 tsp)
  • 115 g unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly (about 8 tbsp)
  • 280 g cold buttermilk (about 1 1/8 cups)

To finish

  • 20 g melted butter (about 1 1/2 tbsp)
  • A pinch of flaky salt, or 1 tsp cracked black pepper

How

  1. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C) and line a tray with parchment.

  2. Whisk the dry ingredients together in a large bowl.

  3. Stir the melted butter into the cold buttermilk. It will seize into small lumps and clumps almost immediately. That is what you want — those lumps are pieces of butter and they behave like cut-in butter once they hit the flour.

  4. Pour the buttermilk mixture into the flour and stir with a fork until just combined, about ten strokes. The dough should be wet and rough, close to 93% hydration, and it should hold its shape on a spoon.

  5. Drop heaped tablespoons, about 70 g each, onto the tray, leaving four centimetres between them. Do not smooth them; the rough peaks brown and crisp and that is the appeal.

  6. Brush the tops with melted butter and season.

  7. Bake 12 to 16 minutes, until the peaks are well browned, the bases are golden when you lift one with a spatula, and an instant-read reads 205°F (96°C).

  8. Serve hot. These stale faster than a cut biscuit because of the larger surface area, so time them to come out when the food does.

  9. If they spread into flat pancakes, the butter was too hot when it met the buttermilk and dissolved into it instead of clumping, or the dough sat too long before baking.

Notes

  • Add 100 g grated sharp cheddar and a teaspoon of garlic powder for the version that goes with chowder.
  • The dough does not keep. Mix it when the oven is already hot.

Keeping it

Eat them the same hour. They reheat acceptably for four minutes at 350°F (175°C) until warm through, and no longer than that.
