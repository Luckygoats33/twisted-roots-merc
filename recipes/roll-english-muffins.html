Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

English Muffins

An 80% dough griddled slowly in rings, split with a fork, full of nooks.

Eight muffins
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
3 hours 30 minutes
Start to finish

The nooks come from two things and neither is magic. The dough is wet, at eighty percent hydration, so the bubbles it makes are large and irregular. And it is cooked slowly on a griddle rather than baked, so the inside stays open and slightly under-set where a hot oven would dry it into a crumb. Split them with a fork, never a knife: the fork tears along the existing holes and leaves the ragged surface that catches butter, and a knife cuts straight through them and gives you two smooth discs.

What you need

Dough

  • 400 g bread flour (about 3 1/4 cups)
  • 260 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 1 cup plus 1 tbsp)
  • 60 g water, lukewarm (about 1/4 cup)
  • 10 g honey (about 1/2 tbsp)
  • 8 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 5 g instant dried yeast (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 20 g unsalted butter, soft (about 1 1/2 tbsp)

For cooking

  • 40 g fine semolina or cornmeal (about 1/4 cup)
  • 10 g butter for the griddle (about 2 tsp)

How

  1. Mix the flour, salt and yeast, add the milk, water and honey, and stir to a very wet, sticky dough. It is closer to a thick batter than a bread dough and it is meant to be.

  2. Beat it with a wooden spoon or a mixer for four minutes, then work in the soft butter. Hydration is 80% on the flour, so do not try to knead this on a counter.

  3. Bulk covered 90 minutes to 2 hours, with one fold in the bowl at the halfway mark, until doubled and full of bubbles.

  4. Scatter semolina thickly over a tray. Scoop the dough into eight pieces of about 95 g with a wet scoop, drop each onto the semolina, and roll them over so they are coated all round. Flatten each gently to about 8 cm across.

  5. Prove 45 to 60 minutes uncovered, until visibly puffed and wobbling when the tray is nudged.

  6. Heat a heavy skillet or griddle over low heat with a smear of butter. Low is the whole trick — an instant-read laid on the surface should read around 150°C. Too hot and the outside is dark before the middle is cooked, which is the classic English muffin failure.

  7. Cook them in rings if you want tidy sides, or free-form if you do not mind them irregular. Give them 7 to 9 minutes on the first side, until the underside is evenly pale gold and the sides look dry and set about a third of the way up.

  8. Turn and cook another 7 to 9 minutes, until the second side matches and an instant-read through the side reads 200°F (93°C). If they are browning in under five minutes, the pan is too hot; take it off the heat for a minute and start again.

  9. Cool completely on a rack before splitting. Warm muffins tear badly and lose their structure.

  10. Split with a fork: press the tines in all the way round the equator, working around twice, then pull apart with your thumbs.

  11. If the insides are gummy and raw, the pan was too hot. If they are dense with small even holes, the dough was too dry or under-proofed.

Notes

  • The same dough made with 100 g of levain in place of the yeast and bulked overnight is better still, and takes a day.
  • Metal crumpet rings or clean tuna tins with the ends cut out both work, and both need buttering.

Keeping it

Three days in a bag. They are made to be toasted, so day two is not a real loss.
