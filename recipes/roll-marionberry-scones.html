Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Marionberry Cream Scones

Frozen berries folded in hard and fast, a rich cream dough, cut in wedges and frozen before baking.

Eight wedges
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

Marionberries are wetter and softer than blueberries and they will happily turn a scone dough purple and slack if you let them. Two rules keep that from happening: use them frozen, straight from the freezer with no thawing, and fold them in with the fewest possible movements. This is the American style of scone, richer than the plain kind and closer to a biscuit in ratio at one part butter to two point eight parts flour, with cream doing the work of the milk. Freezing the cut wedges before baking keeps them tall.

What you need

Dough

  • 420 g all-purpose flour (about 3 1/2 cups)
  • 18 g baking powder (about 4 tsp)
  • 70 g caster sugar (about 1/3 cup)
  • 7 g fine salt (about 1 1/4 tsp)
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 150 g cold unsalted butter, cubed (about 10 1/2 tbsp)
  • 240 g heavy cream, cold (about 1 cup)
  • 1 large egg, about 50 g
  • 200 g frozen marionberries, unthawed (about 1 1/2 cups)

To finish

  • 2 tbsp heavy cream for brushing
  • 20 g demerara sugar (about 1 1/2 tbsp)

How

  1. Whisk the flour, baking powder, sugar, salt and lemon zest together, rubbing the zest through the sugar first so it does not clump.

  2. Cut in the cold butter until the largest pieces are pea-sized. Put the bowl in the freezer for 10 minutes while you get everything else ready.

  3. Beat the egg into the cream. Pour it into the cold flour mixture and stir with a fork about eight times — it should still look dry and unpromising.

  4. Tip the frozen berries in and fold four or five times with a scraper, no more. There will be dry patches. Bring it together on the counter with two or three presses and accept that it looks rough. Every additional movement crushes another berry.

  5. Pat into a single round about 20 cm across and 3 cm thick. Cut into eight wedges with a floured knife, pressing straight down.

  6. Space them on a parchment-lined tray and freeze the whole tray for 15 minutes. Meanwhile heat the oven to 400°F (205°C). Cold dough into a hot oven is what keeps the butter in pieces long enough to steam.

  7. Brush the tops with cream and scatter demerara over.

  8. Bake 22 to 26 minutes, until risen and deep golden at the edges, the berry juice is bubbling dark at the seams, and the bottoms are browned when you lift one. An instant-read in the centre of a wedge reads 205°F (96°C).

  9. Cool 10 minutes on the tray before moving them, or the hot berry pockets tear straight through.

  10. If the dough went lavender and sticky, the berries thawed. It will still bake, it will just be a berry cake rather than a scone.

Notes

  • Blackberries, blueberries and chopped frozen strawberries all work. Fresh berries do not, unless you freeze them first on a tray.
  • Marionberry is a blackberry cross bred here in Oregon and it runs juicier than most, which is why the freezing rule matters more with these than with anything else.

Keeping it

Best the day they are baked. Unbaked wedges freeze for a month and bake from frozen with five minutes added.
