Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Morning Buns

A quick-laminated dough with three folds, cinnamon sugar and orange zest, baked in a muffin tin.

Twelve buns
Makes
1 hour
Hands on
About 6 hours, or overnight for the dough
Start to finish

A morning bun is a croissant that got rolled in cinnamon sugar and baked in a muffin tin, which fixes the two hardest things about croissants: shaping, and the way a pastry that size goes stale by noon. The lamination is a simplified three-fold version — enough butter layers for a proper shatter, not so many that a home kitchen has to keep everything at pastry-room temperature for an afternoon. The muffin tin does real work: it traps the butter as it renders, so the outside fries and caramelises instead of leaking away.

What you need

Dough

  • 400 g bread flour (about 3 1/4 cups)
  • 200 g whole milk, cold (about 3/4 cup plus 1 tbsp)
  • 40 g water, cold (about 3 tbsp)
  • 40 g caster sugar (about 3 tbsp)
  • 8 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 6 g instant dried yeast (about 2 tsp)
  • 30 g unsalted butter, soft (about 2 tbsp)

Butter block

  • 200 g cold unsalted butter (about 14 tbsp)

Filling and finish

  • 150 g caster sugar (about 3/4 cup)
  • 8 g ground cinnamon (about 1 tbsp)
  • Zest of 1 orange
  • 40 g caster sugar for rolling the hot buns (about 3 tbsp)

How

  1. Mix the dough ingredients and knead 5 minutes to a smooth, firm dough. Do not over-develop it; a very strong dough fights the rolling later. Flatten to a rectangle, wrap, and refrigerate at least 2 hours or overnight.

  2. Make the butter block. Beat the cold butter between two sheets of parchment with a rolling pin into a 15 by 15 cm square, then chill it 15 minutes. It should be pliable and cold — if it cracks it is too cold, if it shines it is too warm.

  3. Roll the dough to 30 by 15 cm, lay the butter block over the bottom half, and fold the top down. Seal the edges by pressing with the pin.

  4. Roll out to about 45 by 20 cm and give it a letter fold. Wrap and chill 30 minutes. Repeat twice more, for three folds total, chilling between each. If butter breaks through the surface at any point, patch it with a dusting of flour and get it back in the fridge.

  5. Mix the filling sugar, cinnamon and orange zest, rubbing the zest through the sugar with your fingers until it smells strongly of orange.

  6. Roll the dough out to about 30 by 45 cm and 5 mm thick. Scatter the filling evenly over the whole surface and press it lightly in with the pin so it does not all fall out when you roll.

  7. Roll up from the long side into a tight log and cut twelve pieces. Set each cut side up in a well-buttered muffin tin, pressing down gently.

  8. Prove at cool room temperature, around 21°C, for 90 minutes to 2 hours, until visibly puffed and jiggly and roughly half again as big. Warmer than that and the butter melts out before it ever gets to the oven, which is the classic way to lose a morning's work.

  9. Heat the oven to 400°F (205°C). Bake 22 to 28 minutes, until the tops are deep brown and the sugar at the edges has caramelised to a dark lacquer. Pale morning buns are underbaked and doughy in the spiral.

  10. Lift them out of the tin immediately with a fork — waiting means the caramel sets and they stay in — and roll the hot buns in the extra caster sugar.

  11. Cool five minutes and eat them warm. If a pool of butter collected in each cup, the dough was proved too warm or the lamination broke down. It still tastes good and it is not the pastry you were aiming for.

Notes

  • This lamination is a rough one on purpose. If you want proper croissant layering, do six folds and expect to spend a day on it.
  • Cardamom in place of half the cinnamon is a good change, and it goes better with the orange.

Keeping it

Day one only, honestly. They are 60% air and butter and by tomorrow they are a biscuit.
