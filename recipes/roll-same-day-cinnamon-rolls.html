Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Same-Day Cinnamon Rolls

Two and a half hours start to finish, with the yeast doubled and the dough kept warm.

Twelve rolls in a 9 x 13 inch pan
Makes
40 minutes
Hands on
2 hours 30 minutes
Start to finish

Sometimes the decision to make cinnamon rolls is made at nine in the morning and there is no overnight to work with. This is that version: more yeast, warmer liquid, a slightly leaner dough that develops faster, and rolls cut a little smaller so they bake through in half an hour. They are not quite as good as the overnight ones — a fast dough tastes mostly of butter and sugar, where a slow one tastes of bread as well — but the difference is smaller than the two-hour gap suggests, and nobody has ever handed one back.

What you need

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 250 g whole milk, warmed to about 35°C (about 1 cup)
  • 1 large egg, about 50 g
  • 50 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 8 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 11 g instant dried yeast (about 3 1/2 tsp)
  • 70 g unsalted butter, soft (about 5 tbsp)

Filling

  • 140 g unsalted butter, very soft (about 10 tbsp)
  • 170 g dark brown sugar (about 3/4 cup packed)
  • 16 g ground cinnamon (about 2 tbsp)
  • A pinch of fine salt

How

  1. Warm the milk to about 35°C — comfortably warm on the inside of a wrist, not hot. Anything above about 50°C starts killing yeast, and the difference is not visible, only later and in the pan.

  2. Mix flour, sugar, salt and yeast, add the milk and egg, and bring to a rough dough. Knead 8 minutes until smooth, then work in the soft butter in two lots until glossy.

  3. Bulk somewhere genuinely warm, around 80 degrees Fahrenheit (27°C), for 45 to 55 minutes, until doubled and domed. An oven with the light on and a tray of hot water in the bottom is the reliable trick in a cold house.

  4. Beat the filling to a smooth paste while it rises.

  5. Roll the dough into a rectangle about 35 by 45 cm, spread the filling to the edges leaving one clean long strip, and roll up firmly toward the clean edge.

  6. Cut twelve rolls with dental floss and set them cut side up in a buttered 9 x 13 inch metal pan.

  7. Prove warm 35 to 45 minutes, until the rolls have swollen into each other and a light poke leaves a dent that fills back slowly. Under-proofed rolls bake up tight and bready, and this is the step people rush.

  8. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and bake 26 to 32 minutes, until the tops are deep gold, the filling bubbles at the edges, and an instant-read in the middle roll reads 190°F (88°C).

  9. Rest 10 minutes, then ice while warm.

  10. If the crumb is coarse and yeasty-tasting, that is the cost of the fast schedule and 2.2% yeast. Dropping the yeast to 8 g and adding 40 minutes closes most of the gap.

Notes

  • A hot kitchen makes this schedule shorter still. Judge by the dough, not the clock, particularly in summer.
  • The dough can be made in a stand mixer in 6 minutes on medium, which is worth doing here since speed is the point.

Keeping it

Two days covered. Warm briefly before eating; cold cinnamon rolls are a different and lesser thing.
