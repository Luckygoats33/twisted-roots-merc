Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Shortcake For Berries

A sweet biscuit that splits clean, built to sit under fruit and cream without going to mush.

Eight shortcakes
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

Sponge cake under strawberries is a compromise nobody asked for. Shortcake proper is a sweet biscuit: sturdy enough to hold up under macerated fruit and a pile of cream, tender enough not to fight the fork, and split horizontally so the juice soaks into the cut faces rather than sitting on top. Fat to flour is a shade under one to three, and the sugar is kept moderate because the fruit and cream bring plenty. Make them the same day, and macerate the fruit while they bake.

What you need

Shortcakes

  • 400 g all-purpose flour (about 3 1/4 cups)
  • 16 g baking powder (about 3 1/2 tsp)
  • 60 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 6 g fine salt (about 1 tsp)
  • 140 g cold unsalted butter, cubed (about 10 tbsp)
  • 200 g heavy cream, cold (about 3/4 cup plus 1 tbsp)
  • 1 large egg, about 50 g

To finish and fill

  • 2 tbsp cream for brushing
  • 20 g demerara sugar (about 1 1/2 tbsp)
  • 700 g strawberries or marionberries, hulled and sliced (about 5 cups)
  • 50 g caster sugar for the fruit (about 1/4 cup)
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 300 g heavy cream for whipping (about 1 1/4 cups)
  • 15 g icing sugar (about 2 tbsp)

How

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F (205°C). Whisk the flour, baking powder, sugar and salt.

  2. Cut in the cold butter until the largest pieces are pea to hazelnut sized.

  3. Beat the egg into the cream and stir it in with a fork until the dough just holds together. Total liquid runs at about 62% of the flour, firmer than a biscuit, because these have to stand up to wet fruit.

  4. Pat out to 2 cm, fold in thirds once, then pat to 3 cm thick.

  5. Cut eight rounds with a floured 3 inch cutter, straight down and no twist, and space them on a tray. Brush the tops with cream and scatter demerara over — the sugar crust is doing work here, both texture and a barrier against the juice.

  6. Bake 18 to 22 minutes, until well risen, deep gold on top and browned underneath, and an instant-read reads 205°F (96°C).

  7. While they bake, toss the fruit with the caster sugar and lemon juice and leave it at room temperature. It is ready when the sugar has dissolved and there is a pool of dark syrup in the bowl, 20 to 30 minutes.

  8. Whip the cream with the icing sugar to soft peaks — it should hold a shape and still fold over at the tip. Stiff cream sits on top like a hat; soft cream settles into the fruit.

  9. Split the shortcakes horizontally while still slightly warm. Spoon fruit and syrup over the bottom half, add cream, and set the top back on at an angle.

  10. Assemble at the table, not in advance. Ten minutes after they are built, the bottom half is soup.

Notes

  • Macerating longer than an hour breaks the fruit down into jam. Half an hour is the sweet spot for strawberries.
  • A splash of the berry syrup brushed onto the cut faces before the fruit goes on is a good idea if the shortcakes have cooled.

Keeping it

The shortcakes keep a day in a tin and reheat well. Assembled shortcake keeps for about as long as it takes to carry it to a chair.
