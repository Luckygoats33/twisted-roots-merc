Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Soft Dinner Rolls

A dozen pull-apart rolls with a milk and butter dough, brushed with more butter on the way out.

Twelve rolls in a 9 x 13 inch pan
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
3 hours
Start to finish

These are the rolls that go next to a pot of something, get pulled apart at the table, and disappear before anybody remembers to put them on a plate. The dough is enriched with milk, butter, egg and a little sugar, which gives a fine, soft, slightly sweet crumb that stays tender for two days. They are baked touching so the sides stay pale and pillowy and only the tops brown. Straightforward yeasted dough, no preferment, no overnight, and about the most reliable thing in this section.

What you need

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 300 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 1 1/4 cups)
  • 1 large egg, about 50 g
  • 40 g caster sugar (about 3 tbsp)
  • 9 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 7 g instant dried yeast (about 2 1/4 tsp)
  • 60 g unsalted butter, soft (about 4 tbsp)

To finish

  • 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp milk, for the wash
  • 30 g melted butter for after baking (about 2 tbsp)
  • A pinch of flaky salt

How

  1. Mix flour, sugar, salt and yeast, add milk and egg, and bring together. Knead 8 minutes until smooth, then work in the soft butter until the dough is glossy and stretches thin without tearing.

  2. Bulk covered until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes in a warm room. The dough should hold a dent when poked rather than springing straight back.

  3. Divide into twelve pieces of about 80 g. Shape each by flattening it, folding the edges to the centre, then rolling it under a cupped hand on an unfloured patch of counter until the surface is taut and smooth. Flour under your hand stops it gripping, and without grip you cannot build tension.

  4. Set them in a buttered 9 x 13 inch pan in three rows of four, evenly spaced with small gaps.

  5. Prove covered 45 to 60 minutes, until they have grown into each other, filled the pan, and a light poke leaves a shallow dent that fills slowly.

  6. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) and brush the tops gently with egg wash.

  7. Bake 22 to 28 minutes, until the tops are deep golden brown, the rolls have risen into a single slab, and an instant-read into a middle roll reads 190°F (88°C).

  8. Brush with melted butter the moment they come out and scatter flaky salt over. The steam softens the crust as they cool, which is the texture you want.

  9. Cool 15 minutes in the pan before pulling them apart. If the middle rolls are doughy while the outer ones are done, the pan was crowded or the oven runs cool — check the centre one and bake to temperature, not to time.

Notes

  • The dough shapes into hot dog buns, cloverleaf rolls or a plain loaf without changing anything but the tin.
  • Made a day ahead: shape, refrigerate overnight, and prove for two hours from cold in the morning.

Keeping it

Three days in a sealed bag. Refresh at 300°F (150°C) for five minutes until warm and soft again.
