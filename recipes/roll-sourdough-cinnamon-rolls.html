Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Sourdough Cinnamon Rolls

Naturally leavened, bulked overnight, with enough tang to argue with the sugar.

Nine rolls in a 9 x 13 inch pan
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
About 18 hours
Start to finish

The case for making these with a starter rather than yeast is not that it is more virtuous. It is that a cinnamon roll is an enormous amount of sugar and butter, and a little acidity underneath it stops the whole thing tasting like a dessert at eight in the morning. A naturally leavened enriched dough is slow — sugar and fat both hold yeast back, and wild yeast is not fast to begin with — so this runs overnight at room temperature and gets shaped in the morning, which is the opposite way round from the yeasted version.

What you need

Levain

  • 20 g mature starter
  • 50 g bread flour (about 6 tbsp)
  • 50 g whole milk (about 3 tbsp)

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 220 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 7/8 cup)
  • 2 large eggs, about 100 g
  • 60 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 9 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 80 g unsalted butter, soft (about 5 1/2 tbsp)
  • 120 g levain, all of it

Filling

  • 150 g unsalted butter, very soft (about 10 1/2 tbsp)
  • 180 g dark brown sugar (about 3/4 cup packed)
  • 18 g ground cinnamon (about 2 1/4 tbsp)
  • A pinch of fine salt

How

  1. Build the levain on milk in the morning and use it at peak, six to eight hours later, when it is domed and smells sour and sweet at once.

  2. In the evening, mix flour, sugar, milk, eggs and levain to a rough dough and rest 30 minutes. Add the salt and knead 8 minutes to smooth.

  3. Work in the soft butter in three lots. Enriched sourdough is slack and takes longer to come back together than a yeasted dough; keep going until it is glossy and stretches thin.

  4. Bulk overnight at cool room temperature, around 20°C, for 10 to 14 hours. It should rise by about three-quarters and be soft and airy. In a warm house use the fridge for the second half of the night or it will be over the top by morning.

  5. In the morning, beat the filling to a paste. Roll the dough on a floured counter to about 35 by 45 cm.

  6. Spread the filling to the edges leaving one clean long strip, roll up firmly, and cut nine rolls with dental floss.

  7. Set them cut side up in a buttered 9 x 13 inch metal pan and prove 2 to 4 hours at room temperature, until visibly swollen, touching, and holding a slow-filling dent. Sourdough enriched dough proves slowly and this is not a step to hurry — under-proofed rolls come out tight and heavy and taste sour rather than complex.

  8. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake 30 to 36 minutes, until deep gold, bubbling at the edges, and an instant-read in the middle roll reads 190°F (88°C).

  9. Rest 10 minutes and ice while warm.

  10. If they taste flatly sour instead of tangy, the levain was past peak or the bulk ran long in a warm room. If they are dense, the prove was short.

Notes

  • Discard will not do this. It needs a levain at peak, and the dough needs the lift more than any lean bread does.
  • For a morning bake, shape at night and cold proof 10 to 12 hours, then give the pan two hours at room temperature before it goes in.

Keeping it

Two days covered. Naturally leavened rolls stale slightly slower than yeasted ones, which is a small consolation for the extra day.
