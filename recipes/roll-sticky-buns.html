Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Pecan Sticky Buns

A caramel goo in the bottom of the pan, buns baked on top of it, and the whole thing inverted while hot.

Twelve buns in a 9 x 13 inch pan
Makes
50 minutes
Hands on
About 4 hours, or overnight
Start to finish

A sticky bun is a cinnamon roll baked upside down on top of a caramel. The goo is butter, brown sugar and syrup, and it does two jobs: it caramelises into a sauce under the buns, and it steams up through them so the crumb stays wet. The critical part is inverting the pan while everything is still hot enough to flow. Leave it five minutes too long and the caramel sets into toffee, welds itself to the pan, and you will be chiselling pecans out of it while everyone watches.

What you need

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 250 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 1 cup)
  • 1 large egg, about 50 g
  • 50 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 9 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 7 g instant dried yeast (about 2 1/4 tsp)
  • 70 g unsalted butter, soft (about 5 tbsp)

Goo

  • 170 g unsalted butter (about 12 tbsp)
  • 200 g dark brown sugar (about 1 cup packed)
  • 120 g honey or golden syrup (about 1/3 cup)
  • 60 g heavy cream (about 1/4 cup)
  • 4 g fine salt (about 3/4 tsp)
  • 150 g pecan halves (about 1 1/2 cups)

Filling

  • 80 g unsalted butter, very soft (about 6 tbsp)
  • 120 g light brown sugar (about 1/2 cup packed)
  • 10 g ground cinnamon (about 1 1/4 tbsp)

How

  1. Mix and knead the dough as for any enriched dough: everything but the butter first, 8 minutes to smooth, then the soft butter worked in until glossy and windowpaning. Bulk until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes warm.

  2. Make the goo. Melt the butter with the brown sugar, honey, cream and salt over medium heat, stirring, until it is smooth, glossy, and just beginning to bubble at the edges — about three minutes. Do not boil it hard; you are making a sauce, not a candy, and boiling drives it toward toffee.

  3. Pour the goo into a buttered 9 x 13 inch metal pan and scatter the pecans over it evenly, flat side down.

  4. Beat the filling to a paste. Roll the dough to a 35 by 45 cm rectangle, spread the filling to the edges bar one clean strip, and roll up firmly.

  5. Cut twelve buns with floss and set them cut side up on the goo, leaving space between them.

  6. Prove 45 to 60 minutes at room temperature, until swollen and touching and a poke leaves a slow-filling dent. Or cover and refrigerate overnight, then bring back to room temperature for an hour in the morning.

  7. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Put the pan on a foil-lined tray, because the goo will boil over and burnt caramel on an oven floor is a genuinely unpleasant afternoon.

  8. Bake 32 to 38 minutes, until the tops are deep brown, the caramel is bubbling thickly up the sides, and an instant-read in the centre bun reads 190°F (88°C). Underbaked sticky buns are gummy in the middle and the caramel never sets properly.

  9. Rest exactly five minutes — long enough for the caramel to stop being molten, short enough that it still flows. Run a knife round the edge, put a tray over the pan, and invert the whole thing in one confident movement.

  10. Leave the pan sitting on top for a minute so the last of the caramel runs down, then lift it off and scrape any stragglers back onto the buns.

  11. If the caramel set hard in the pan, put it back in the oven for three minutes until it loosens, then invert again. If it ran thin and soaked away, the goo was boiled too long and lost water.

Notes

  • Toasting the pecans first at 350°F (175°C) for 7 minutes, until fragrant, is worth the extra tray.
  • Golden syrup gives a cleaner caramel flavour than honey. Honey gives a floral note that some people like and some do not.

Keeping it

Two days at room temperature under a cloth. Reheat for six minutes at 300°F (150°C) until the caramel loosens again.
