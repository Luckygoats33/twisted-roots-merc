Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

The Twisted Root

The overnight cinnamon roll: enriched dough shaped at night, proofed cold, baked at six.

Nine large rolls in a 9 x 13 inch pan
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
About 14 hours, most of it in the fridge
Start to finish

These are proofed overnight in the fridge, which is not a shortcut so much as a rearrangement: the fiddly rolling and cutting happens at a civilised hour, and the morning job is turning an oven on. Cold proofing also firms the butter in the filling so it stays put instead of running out into the pan, and it gives the dough enough time to develop some flavour of its own rather than tasting only of cinnamon and sugar. They come out roughly the size of a hubcap, which is the size a cinnamon roll should be.

What you need

Dough

  • 600 g bread flour (about 4 3/4 cups)
  • 300 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 1 1/4 cups)
  • 2 large eggs, about 100 g
  • 60 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 10 g fine salt (about 1 3/4 tsp)
  • 7 g instant dried yeast (about 2 1/4 tsp)
  • 85 g unsalted butter, soft (about 6 tbsp)

Filling

  • 170 g unsalted butter, very soft (about 12 tbsp)
  • 200 g dark brown sugar (about 1 cup packed)
  • 20 g ground cinnamon (about 2 1/2 tbsp)
  • A pinch of fine salt

How

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, salt and yeast, keeping salt and yeast apart until they meet the liquid. Add the milk and eggs and mix to a rough dough.

  2. Knead 8 minutes until smooth and elastic, then add the soft butter in three lots, kneading each in fully. It will go slippery and refuse to hold together, then come back. Finished dough should be glossy and pull into a thin windowpane.

  3. Bulk in a covered bowl until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes in a warm kitchen. If your house is cold, this is where the schedule slips — give it the time rather than more yeast.

  4. Beat the filling ingredients to a smooth spreadable paste. Very soft butter, not melted: melted butter runs out of the roll and pools under it, then burns.

  5. Roll the dough on a floured counter into a rectangle about 40 by 50 cm and 5 mm thick. Spread the filling right to the edges on three sides, leaving a clean 2 cm strip along the far long edge.

  6. Roll up from the long side toward the clean strip, firmly but not so tight that the middle telescopes out when it proofs. Pinch the seam shut and lay it seam down.

  7. Cut nine rolls with a length of unflavoured dental floss or a very sharp knife — slide the floss under, cross the ends over the top and pull. A knife squashes the spiral and floss does not.

  8. Set them cut side up in a buttered 9 x 13 inch pan with room between them, cover tightly, and refrigerate 8 to 14 hours.

  9. In the morning, take the pan out and let it stand at room temperature 60 to 90 minutes, until the rolls have puffed, grown into each other, and a gentle poke leaves a dent that fills slowly. Cold rolls put straight in the oven will be dense in the middle.

  10. Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C). Bake 28 to 34 minutes, until the tops are deep gold, the filling is bubbling at the edges, and an instant-read into the doughy centre of a middle roll reads 190°F (88°C). The centre roll is always the last to be done and it is the one to test.

  11. Rest 10 minutes before icing, so the icing softens into the surface without disappearing entirely. Ice them warm, not hot, and not cold.

  12. If the middles rose up and popped out like a chimney, the roll was too tight. If the filling ran out and welded them to the pan, the butter was melted or the pan was underbuttered.

Notes

  • Cream cheese icing is the standard here. There is a recipe for it in this section and it makes about twice what one pan needs, which nobody has ever objected to.
  • The pan should be metal. Glass insulates and the bases come out pale and doughy.

Keeping it

Two days covered at room temperature. Reheat individually for 20 seconds in a microwave, or eight minutes at 300°F (150°C) until warmed through.
