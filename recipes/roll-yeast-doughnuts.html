Biscuits, Rolls & Buns

Yeast Doughnuts

An enriched dough fried at 350°F (175°C), turned once, glazed while hot.

Twelve doughnuts and their holes
Makes
50 minutes
Hands on
3 hours 30 minutes
Start to finish

Frying is a temperature problem with a dough attached. Too cool and the doughnut sits in the oil absorbing it, and comes out heavy and greasy. Too hot and the outside is dark before the middle has cooked, which is worse because it looks finished. Three hundred and fifty is the number, and it drops every time you put dough in, so you fry in small batches and let it recover between them. A thermometer clipped to the pan is not optional here — this is the one recipe in this section where guessing has a real cost.

What you need

Dough

  • 500 g bread flour (about 4 cups)
  • 240 g whole milk, lukewarm (about 1 cup)
  • 2 large eggs, about 100 g
  • 60 g caster sugar (about 1/4 cup)
  • 9 g fine salt (about 1 1/2 tsp)
  • 8 g instant dried yeast (about 2 1/2 tsp)
  • 60 g unsalted butter, soft (about 4 tbsp)

For frying

  • 2 litres neutral oil — canola, sunflower or peanut (about 8 cups)

Glaze

  • 300 g icing sugar, sifted (about 2 1/2 cups)
  • 60 g whole milk (about 1/4 cup)
  • 2 tsp vanilla extract
  • A pinch of fine salt

How

  1. Mix the flour, sugar, salt and yeast, add milk and eggs, and knead 8 minutes to a smooth dough. Work in the soft butter until glossy and elastic.

  2. Bulk covered until doubled, 60 to 90 minutes. Or refrigerate overnight, which makes the dough much easier to cut and gives a better flavour.

  3. Roll out on a floured counter to 1.5 cm thick — no thinner, or they fry through before they puff. Cut with a 3 inch cutter and a 1 inch cutter for the middles.

  4. Set each on its own small square of parchment on a tray. The parchment lets you lower a proofed doughnut into the oil without deflating it, and then floats free.

  5. Prove 45 to 60 minutes, until visibly puffed and light, and a very gentle poke leaves a dent that fills slowly. Over-proofed doughnuts collapse in the oil; under-proofed ones stay dense and do not get the pale ring around the middle.

  6. Heat the oil in a heavy deep pan to 350°F (175°C), with a thermometer clipped to the side. Fill the pan no more than half way.

  7. Fry two or three at a time, parchment side down, peeling the paper off with tongs after a few seconds. Fry 60 to 90 seconds, until the underside is an even deep gold and a pale band has formed around the middle where the dough sat above the oil.

  8. Turn once and fry another 60 to 90 seconds, until the second side matches. Check the first one of each batch: an instant-read into the side should read 190°F (88°C). Turning them more than once makes them absorb oil.

  9. Lift onto a rack over a tray, never onto paper towel, which traps steam against the base and turns it soggy.

  10. Let the oil come back up to 350°F (175°C) between batches. It will have dropped 15 to 20 degrees Fahrenheit and frying at that lower temperature is exactly how greasy doughnuts happen.

  11. Whisk the glaze ingredients smooth. Dip each doughnut while still warm, top side down, lift, twist, and set back on the rack to drip and set.

  12. Eat them within a couple of hours. A doughnut is at its best somewhere around the fifteen minute mark and it is genuinely not the same thing by tomorrow.

Notes

  • The holes fry in about 45 seconds a side and are the cook's payment.
  • Used frying oil, cooled and strained through a coffee filter, is good for one or two more rounds. Discard it once it smells sharp or foams.

Keeping it

Same day only, and preferably within the hour. There is no honest way around this.
