Soup, Chowder & Stew

Broccoli Cheddar Soup

Grate your own cheese, take the pot off the heat before it goes in, and it will not turn grainy on you.

6 bowls, about 2.5 litres
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
45 minutes
Start to finish

Cheese soup breaks for reasons worth understanding. Cheese is protein and fat held in an emulsion, and heat above roughly 180 degrees Fahrenheit (82°C) tightens those proteins into strings and squeezes the fat out as an oily film. Pre-grated cheese makes it worse, because the anti-caking starch on it turns the soup slightly gritty. So the rules are: grate your own from a block, take the pot off the heat before the cheese goes in, add it a handful at a time, and never let it boil afterwards. The roux is there to help too, since starch in the liquid physically gets between the proteins and slows them clumping.

What you need

Base

  • 60 g butter
  • 1 large onion, finely diced
  • 2 carrots, grated
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 50 g all-purpose flour
  • 750 ml vegetable or light stock
  • 500 ml whole milk, warmed
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/4 teaspoon grated nutmeg
  • 1/2 teaspoon dry mustard powder

Broccoli

  • 700 g broccoli, florets in small pieces, stems peeled and diced

Cheese and finishing

  • 300 g sharp cheddar, grated from a block
  • 50 g parmesan, grated
  • 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce, optional
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • Fine salt, to taste

How

  1. Melt the butter in a heavy pot over medium heat and cook the onion and grated carrot until the onion is translucent and soft, about eight minutes. Keep the heat moderate; this soup should stay pale, and browned onion turns it a dull khaki.

  2. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, about thirty seconds. Sprinkle over the flour and stir constantly, cooking until it smells like toasted crackers rather than raw dough and has gone pale straw in colour, about two minutes. Under-cooked roux is why so many cheese soups have a chalky background taste.

  3. Add the stock a ladle at a time, stirring smooth after each addition, then the warmed milk. Work the lumps out while the mixture is thick; they are permanent once it thins.

  4. Add the salt, pepper, nutmeg and mustard powder and bring to a gentle simmer. Cook for five minutes, stirring, until it has thickened enough to coat a spoon.

  5. Add the diced broccoli stems first and cook three minutes, then the florets, and simmer only until the florets are tender at the stalk when pierced and still bright green, four to five minutes more. Overcooked broccoli goes olive-drab and sulphurous, and it happens fast.

  6. Blend a third of the soup and stir it back in if you want it thicker and more uniform, or leave it entirely chunky. Do this before the cheese goes in — blending a cheese soup at speed risks breaking the emulsion.

  7. Take the pot completely off the heat and wait a minute. Then add the cheddar and parmesan a large handful at a time, stirring until each addition has fully melted before adding the next. The soup should stay glossy and smooth.

  8. Finish with the Worcestershire and lemon juice and taste for salt, keeping in mind that the cheese has already brought a lot. Serve immediately, in warm bowls.

  9. If it needs to be held, keep it below a simmer over the lowest possible heat, stirring now and then. If it must be reheated later, do so gently and stop as soon as it steams.

Notes

  • It went grainy: too hot, too fast, or pre-grated cheese. Sometimes a broken cheese soup will come back if you blend it hard with a splash of cold milk, but do not count on it.

  • Sodium citrate, a quarter teaspoon per 100 g of cheese, makes the emulsion essentially unbreakable and is what processed cheese uses. It is not cheating, it is chemistry, and it is worth having if you make this often.

  • Very aged cheddar tastes better and breaks more easily, because its proteins are already broken down. A medium-sharp cheddar of about a year is the practical compromise.

Keeping it

Three days cold, and it is at its best the day it is made. Reheat over low heat, stirring, and never let it simmer. It does not freeze — the cheese emulsion separates on thawing and cannot be brought back.
