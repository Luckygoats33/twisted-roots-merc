Soup, Chowder & Stew

Chicken and Rice Soup

Thighs, not breast. Rice cooked separately, so the leftovers are not a bowl of porridge.

6 bowls, about 3 litres
Makes
30 minutes
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

Two decisions make this soup better than the version most people have had. The first is thighs instead of breast. Breast meat has almost no connective tissue and no fat, so by the time it has flavoured a broth it is dry and stringy; thigh meat stays succulent through a long simmer and gives the pot far more. The second is cooking the rice separately and adding it to the bowl. Rice left in soup keeps absorbing liquid all night, and what was a soup becomes a solid mass by morning. Cook it apart, store it apart, and you have soup on the second day instead of a casserole.

What you need

Chicken

  • 900 g bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs, about 6
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil

Soup

  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 3 carrots, sliced 1 cm thick
  • 3 ribs celery, sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 litres chicken stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 3 sprigs thyme
  • 1 strip lemon zest

Rice and finishing

  • 200 g long grain white rice
  • 500 ml water
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt for the rice
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 3 tablespoons chopped parsley or dill
  • Fine salt and black pepper, to taste

How

  1. Season the chicken thighs on both sides and let them sit while you get everything else ready. Pat the skin dry before it goes in the pan — wet skin steams and will not brown.

  2. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high and lay the thighs in skin side down. Leave them without moving until the skin is deep golden and releases from the pot on its own, six to eight minutes. Turn and give the other side two minutes. Lift them out. They are not cooked through yet and are not meant to be.

  3. Pour off all but about 2 tablespoons of fat. Add the butter, onion, carrot and celery and cook until the onion is translucent and the vegetables have softened, about eight minutes, scraping up the browned bits from the chicken as they loosen. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, thirty seconds.

  4. Add the stock, bay, thyme and lemon zest, and return the thighs to the pot along with any juice on the plate. Bring to a bare simmer — surface barely moving. A boil will toughen the meat and cloud the broth.

  5. Simmer for about twenty-five minutes, until the meat pulls easily from the bone. Chicken is safe at an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C); check the thickest part of a thigh against the bone with an instant-read thermometer, and note that thigh meat is actually better a good deal past that, at around 175°F (79°C), where the connective tissue has broken down.

  6. While that happens, cook the rice separately: rinse it until the water runs clear, then simmer covered in the water with the salt until the grains are tender and the water has been absorbed, about fifteen minutes. Fluff and set aside.

  7. Lift the chicken out, let it cool enough to handle, then pull the meat off in large pieces and discard the skin and bones. Do not shred it to threads; pieces you have to chew are the point.

  8. Return the meat to the pot, pull out the bay, thyme stems and zest, and add the lemon juice and herbs. Taste for salt — this is the step that decides whether the soup tastes finished or flat, and it usually needs more than you expect.

  9. To serve, put a scoop of rice in each bowl and ladle the soup over it. Warm the whole thing through until steaming and serve.

Notes

  • If you must use breast, poach it whole in the finished broth off the heat until the centre reaches 165°F (74°C), then slice it. Simmering breast meat for twenty-five minutes gives you sawdust.

  • Lemon and dill take this in a Greek direction; ginger, a spoon of white miso and green onion take it somewhere else entirely. The base does not care.

  • The zest strip goes in whole and comes out whole. Grated zest left in turns bitter over a long simmer.

Keeping it

Broth and meat hold five days cold and freeze for three months. Rice holds four days cold and freezes acceptably. Kept separate, this reheats into something that still looks like soup; kept together, you will be eating chicken rice pilaf, which is not a disaster but is not what you made.
