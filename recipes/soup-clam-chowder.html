Soup, Chowder & Stew

Clam Chowder, Thickened Properly

A blond roux, clam liquor, and dairy that goes in at the end and never boils. Coastal Oregon, not a paste.

6 bowls, about 2.5 litres
Makes
45 minutes
Hands on
1 hour
Start to finish

The chowder in most bowls on this coast has been thickened with cornstarch until a spoon will stand up in it, which hides that there is not much clam in there. This one is thickened with a blond roux — equal weights of butter and flour, cooked two minutes until it smells of toasted crackers — at a ratio that gives a soup you drink rather than a pudding you chew. The dairy goes in at the very end, off the boil, because a chowder that boils after the cream is added will break into grains and greasy pools. And the clams go in last of all, because a clam has about ninety seconds before it turns to rubber.

What you need

Clams

  • 1.5 kg fresh clams in the shell, scrubbed, or 400 g chopped clam meat
  • 250 ml water or dry white wine, for steaming

Base

  • 150 g thick-cut bacon, diced
  • 1 tablespoon butter, if the bacon is lean
  • 2 medium onions, diced
  • 3 ribs celery, diced
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves
  • 50 g butter
  • 50 g all-purpose flour
  • 900 g Yukon gold potatoes, cut in 1.5 cm dice
  • 750 ml clam liquor topped up with water or stock

Finishing

  • 375 ml half and half or whole milk, warmed
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons coarse black pepper
  • Fine salt, to taste
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 1 teaspoon cider vinegar

How

  1. If you are using clams in the shell, put them in a wide pot with the water or wine, cover, and steam over high heat until the shells have opened, five to eight minutes. Pull them out as they open rather than waiting for the last stubborn ones — overcooked clams are rubber. Discard any that stay shut.

  2. Strain the liquid left in the pot through a cloth or a fine sieve to catch the sand, and keep it. That is clam liquor, and it is most of the flavour of this soup. Pull the meat from the shells and chop it roughly.

  3. Render the bacon in a heavy pot over medium heat until the fat has run out and the pieces are browned and crisp at the edges. Lift them out with a slotted spoon and set aside, leaving the fat behind.

  4. Cook the onion and celery in the bacon fat with the bay and thyme until the onion is completely soft and translucent with no crunch left, about eight minutes. Do not brown them; this is a white soup and browned onion turns it grey.

  5. Add the 50 g of butter and let it melt, then sprinkle over the flour. Stir constantly and cook until the mixture smells like toasted crackers rather than raw dough and has gone the colour of pale straw, about two minutes. Stop there. A darker roux tastes better and thickens worse, and this soup needs the thickening power.

  6. Add the clam liquor a ladle at a time, stirring smooth after each addition. Getting the lumps out while it is still thick is easy; once it is thin they are permanent.

  7. Add the potatoes, bring to a gentle simmer, and cook until a knife slides into a cube with no resistance, about fifteen minutes. The potato starch that leaches out here adds body on top of the roux, which is why this recipe uses less flour than you might expect.

  8. Take the pot off the heat. Stir in the warmed dairy. From this point the soup must never boil — above about 180°F (82°C) the milk proteins tighten and curdle and the fat separates out, and a broken chowder does not come back together.

  9. Return to low heat, stir in the clams and the bacon, and warm through only until steam rises and the surface shivers, two or three minutes. The clams are already cooked; you are heating them, not cooking them.

  10. Season with the pepper, salt to taste, parsley and the vinegar. Taste before salting — clam liquor and bacon have already brought a great deal. The vinegar is not there to be tasted, it is there to stop the cream and starch tasting heavy.

Notes

  • It broke: it boiled after the dairy went in, or the dairy was cold and hit a very hot pot. Warm the dairy first, add it off the heat, and keep the finished soup below a simmer.

  • Razor clams, if you dug them, are better here than anything you will buy — clean them, use only the tender parts and chop the tougher neck fine, and add them at the same point.

  • The roux ratio is 50 g flour and 50 g butter to about 1.1 litres of total liquid, which gives a chowder that coats a spoon and still runs off it. Double the flour and you have gravy with potatoes in it.

Keeping it

Four days cold and it is better on the second day, once the potato starch has fully hydrated and the thyme has spread out. Reheat gently over low heat, stirring, and never let it boil. It does not freeze — dairy and potato both come out of the freezer with the texture wrecked. If you want to freeze ahead, make everything up to the point before the dairy goes in, freeze that, and finish it fresh.
