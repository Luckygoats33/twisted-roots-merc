Soup, Chowder & Stew

Corn Chowder, Thickened Without Flour

The cobs make the stock, the corn starch does the thickening, and there is no roux anywhere in it.

6 bowls, about 2.5 litres
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 10 minutes
Start to finish

This chowder has no roux in it and does not need one. Corn kernels are full of starch, and blending a portion of the finished soup releases enough of it — along with the starch from the potatoes — to give real body. The other half of the technique is the cobs. Scrape them hard with the back of a knife after cutting off the kernels and you get a thick, sweet, milky liquid that is the most concentrated corn flavour available; then simmer the stripped cobs in the stock for half an hour and they give up more. Skip both and you have a potato soup with corn in it.

What you need

Corn and its stock

  • 8 ears sweet corn, husked
  • 1.25 litres water or light vegetable stock
  • 2 bay leaves

Base

  • 120 g thick-cut bacon, diced, or 3 tablespoons butter for a meatless pot
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 2 ribs celery, diced
  • 1 red bell pepper, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 700 g Yukon gold potatoes, cut in 1.5 cm dice
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Finishing

  • 250 ml whole milk or half and half, warmed
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice or cider vinegar
  • 3 tablespoons sliced green onion
  • A pinch of cayenne

How

  1. Stand each ear in a wide bowl and cut the kernels off. Then take the back of the knife and scrape down each stripped cob, hard, from top to bottom. A thick white liquid comes out. That is corn milk, it is loaded with starch and sugar, and it is the reason this recipe works without flour. Keep it with the kernels.

  2. Break the bare cobs in half and simmer them in the water or stock with the bay leaves for thirty minutes, until the liquid smells sweetly of corn and has taken on a faint body. Pull the cobs out and discard them. This is your chowder liquid.

  3. Meanwhile, render the bacon in a heavy pot over medium heat until browned and crisp at the edges and the fat has run. Lift the pieces out and set them aside. If you are going meatless, melt the butter instead.

  4. Cook the onion, celery and bell pepper in the fat until soft, translucent and just beginning to catch gold at the edges, about eight minutes. Add the garlic and paprika and cook until fragrant, thirty seconds.

  5. Add the potatoes, the corn stock, the salt and pepper. Simmer until the potatoes give no resistance to a knife, about fifteen minutes.

  6. Add the kernels and all the scraped corn milk and simmer another five minutes, until the kernels are tender but still pop between the teeth. Longer and they go flabby and lose the texture that makes this worth eating.

  7. Ladle about a third of the soup into a blender and puree until completely smooth, then stir it back in. It should visibly thicken as it returns, going from watery to something that coats a spoon. If it is still thin, blend another ladleful. If it is too thick, add stock.

  8. Off the heat, stir in the warmed milk, the lime juice and the cayenne, and taste for salt. Warm through gently and stop when steam rises — this soup will curdle if boiled after the dairy, just like any other chowder.

  9. Serve topped with the bacon and green onion. Keeping the bacon out until serving means it is still crisp instead of soft and pale.

Notes

  • Frozen corn works out of season, but you lose the cobs and the corn milk, which is most of the point. Compensate by blending half the soup instead of a third, and add a knob of butter at the end.

  • It is bland: corn needs acid and salt more than most vegetables. The lime at the end is doing real work, and under-salting a corn soup makes it taste like sweet water.

  • Vegan version: butter to oil, dairy to unsweetened oat milk or coconut milk. Coconut and corn are a better match than they sound, especially with the cayenne turned up.

Keeping it

Four days cold and good on the second day. It freezes better than most chowders if you leave the dairy out — freeze the blended base for three months and stir in milk when you reheat. With the dairy already in, expect separation on thawing.
