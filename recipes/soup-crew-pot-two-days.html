Soup, Chowder & Stew

A Pot That Feeds a Crew for Two Days

Twelve litres of white bean and sausage soup. Scaled honestly, with the parts that do not scale called out.

About 12 litres, 24 large bowls
Makes
1 hour
Hands on
3 hours
Start to finish

Most recipes lie about scaling. Multiply a soup by four and several things stop behaving: browning takes far longer because the pan is crowded and the moisture has nowhere to go, seasoning does not scale linearly and salt in particular needs adding by taste rather than by multiplication, and a twelve litre pot takes hours to cool, which is a food safety problem rather than an inconvenience. This is a soup built for that scale on purpose — beans, sausage, greens and stock, nothing that breaks or goes grainy — with the awkward parts flagged. It feeds a working crew twice and is better the second day.

What you need

Beans

  • 1.4 kg dried cannellini or great northern beans
  • 6 litres water, for soaking
  • 2 tablespoons fine salt, for the soaking water

Pot

  • 1.8 kg Italian sausage, casings removed
  • 4 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 large onions, diced
  • 6 carrots, diced
  • 6 ribs celery, diced
  • 12 cloves garlic, minced
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 tablespoon fennel seed, crushed
  • 2 teaspoons red pepper flakes
  • 2 tins diced tomatoes, about 800 g each
  • 6 litres chicken or vegetable stock
  • 4 bay leaves
  • 2 parmesan rinds

Late and finishing

  • 900 g kale or chard, stemmed and chopped
  • 3 tablespoons red wine vinegar
  • 3 tablespoons fine salt, to taste
  • 150 g grated parmesan, for the table
  • 125 ml good olive oil, for drizzling

How

  1. Soak the beans overnight in the water with the salt. Salted soaking water gives beans that cook more evenly with skins that stay intact, which is the opposite of the old advice and is what actually happens. Drain them before cooking.

  2. Brown the sausage in your largest pot in four batches, not one. This is the step that does not scale: a crowded pot steams the meat grey and you lose the browning that carries the whole soup. Each batch wants a hot pan and space. Ground meat has to reach an internal temperature of 160°F (71°C), so check the thickest crumbles with an instant-read thermometer rather than judging by colour. Lift each batch out and keep the fat.

  3. Cook the onion, carrot and celery in the sausage fat and olive oil until softened and browning at the edges. At this volume expect twenty to twenty-five minutes rather than the ten a small pot takes, and stir often so the bottom does not scorch.

  4. Add the garlic, fennel seed and pepper flakes and cook until fragrant, then the tomato paste, cooking until it darkens to brick.

  5. Add the drained beans, tomatoes, stock, bay leaves and parmesan rinds. Return the sausage. Bring to a boil, then hold it at a low simmer.

  6. Simmer for about ninety minutes, stirring and scraping the bottom every twenty minutes. Beans sink and a twelve litre pot scorches easily. The soup is ready when a bean crushes smoothly against the roof of your mouth with no chalk at the centre — test five, since one soft bean proves nothing.

  7. Add the greens and cook until they are tender and dark, five to ten minutes for kale and one or two for chard.

  8. Now season, off the heat, in stages. Add half the salt, stir thoroughly, wait two minutes and taste. A pot this size takes several minutes to distribute salt, and tasting too soon leads to over-salting. Add the vinegar at the end.

  9. Cooling is the part people get wrong. A full pot left on the stove stays in the temperature range where bacteria multiply for hours. Divide it into shallow containers no more than 8 cm deep, or set the pot in a sink of ice water and stir it down, and get it cold within a couple of hours.

  10. To serve on the second day, reheat only what you need, bringing each batch to a full steaming simmer. Serve with parmesan, olive oil and bread.

Notes

  • Feeding twenty-four: plan 500 ml a head for a working crew and 350 ml for a mixed room. Add a loaf of bread per six people and it stretches further than the numbers suggest.

  • What does not scale: browning, seasoning, and cooling. Everything else multiplies cleanly. Budget more time than the arithmetic suggests for the first and taste your way through the second.

  • Meatless version: leave out the sausage, double the fennel seed, add 700 g of mushrooms browned hard, and use vegetable stock. It is a good soup and it needs more salt than the meat version.

Keeping it

Five days cold in shallow containers, and better on the second day. It freezes for four months and portions well into 1 litre containers. The greens go slack in the freezer, so if you plan to freeze most of the pot, add the greens by the batch when reheating instead.
