Soup, Chowder & Stew

Mushroom Soup, Dried and Fresh Together

Fresh mushrooms give texture, dried ones give the flavour. Using only fresh is why most mushroom soup tastes of cream.

6 bowls, about 2.5 litres
Makes
35 minutes
Hands on
55 minutes
Start to finish

Fresh mushrooms are about ninety percent water. That water has to be driven off before browning starts, and even when it has, what is left behind is texture more than flavour. Dried mushrooms are the concentrated version — the drying process converts compounds that make them far more savoury than their fresh equivalent — and their soaking liquid is close to a mushroom stock. Using both is the whole idea here: fresh for body and bite, dried and their liquor for depth. Sherry at the end is traditional and worth including, since mushrooms and a little oxidised wine are one of those pairings that works for reasons nobody needs to explain.

What you need

Dried

  • 30 g dried porcini, shiitake or a mixed pack
  • 500 ml boiling water

Fresh

  • 700 g fresh mushrooms — cremini, button or wild, sliced thick
  • 3 tablespoons butter
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 teaspoons fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons fine salt, plus more to taste

Soup

  • 35 g all-purpose flour
  • 1 litre vegetable or light stock
  • 60 ml dry sherry or madeira
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  • 180 ml heavy cream, warmed
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley

How

  1. Cover the dried mushrooms with the boiling water and leave them until fully soft, about twenty minutes. Lift them out, chop them, and keep the liquid. Pour the liquid off carefully and leave the last spoonful behind, since grit settles at the bottom.

  2. Heat the butter and oil in a heavy pot over medium-high and add the fresh mushrooms with a pinch of salt. They will release a great deal of water and appear to boil. Keep going.

  3. Cook until the liquid has gone entirely, the pot has begun to hiss rather than bubble, the mushrooms have shrunk to a third of their volume and are browned at the edges, and a sticky brown film has built on the pot bottom. Ten to twelve minutes. Stopping short of this is the single most common reason mushroom soup tastes of nothing.

  4. Set aside a large handful of the browned mushrooms for garnish. Add the onion to the pot and cook until soft and golden, then the garlic and thyme until fragrant.

  5. Sprinkle over the flour and stir it in. Cook until it smells like toasted crackers rather than raw dough, about two minutes. This is a light roux — 35 g of flour for 1.5 litres of total liquid — which gives body without making a sauce of it.

  6. Add the sherry and scrape the pot bottom until it is clean. Let it reduce until the alcohol smell has gone, about a minute.

  7. Add the stock, the strained mushroom liquor, the chopped soaked mushrooms and the soy sauce. Simmer for twenty minutes, until the flavours have pulled together and the soup has thickened enough to coat a spoon.

  8. Blend to whatever texture you want. Fully smooth makes it elegant; blending half and leaving the rest gives you something to chew. An immersion blender in short bursts is easiest.

  9. Off the heat, stir in the warmed cream, the pepper and the lemon juice. Bring it back to a steam over low heat, and stop there — boiling after the cream will split it.

  10. Serve with the reserved browned mushrooms and the parsley on top, and taste for salt one more time before it leaves the stove.

Notes

  • Chanterelles, if you have found any on this coast, want different handling: cook them dry in a hot pan first until their water has gone, then add butter. They are worth using as the garnish rather than blending into the soup.

  • Vegan version: olive oil for butter, and full-fat oat milk or a cashew cream for the dairy. The mushroom flavour carries it without difficulty.

  • The soaking liquid is the point. Throwing it out and using more stock costs you most of what the dried mushrooms were for.

Keeping it

Four days cold. It thickens overnight; thin with stock. Freeze it before the cream goes in for three months, then finish it fresh — mushroom soup with the cream already in comes out of the freezer separated and grainy.
