Soup, Chowder & Stew

Split Pea with a Smoked Hock

One hock, a bag of split peas, and four hours of doing nothing. The cheapest good pot of soup there is.

8 bowls, about 3.5 litres
Makes
25 minutes
Hands on
3 hours
Start to finish

This is a soup built on one cheap smoky ingredient and a bag of dried peas, and it asks for almost nothing except time. Split peas have had their skins removed, which is why they need no soaking and why they collapse entirely into the liquid instead of staying whole — that collapse is the thickening, and there is no flour or cream anywhere in it. A smoked hock brings salt, smoke and a great deal of gelatin from the skin and joint, and it is already fully cured and cooked when you buy it, so the long simmer is about extracting what it has rather than making it safe.

What you need

The pot

  • 1 smoked ham hock, about 700 g, or 2 smaller ones
  • 500 g green split peas, picked over and rinsed
  • 2 tablespoons butter or olive oil
  • 2 large onions, diced
  • 3 carrots, diced
  • 3 ribs celery, diced
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2.5 litres water or light stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Finishing

  • 1 tablespoon cider vinegar
  • Fine salt, to taste
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 60 g croutons per bowl, optional

How

  1. Melt the butter in a large heavy pot over medium heat and cook the onion, carrot and celery until soft and the onion is translucent, about ten minutes. Let them take a little colour at the edges; it costs nothing and adds depth. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, thirty seconds.

  2. Add the split peas, water, bay, thyme, pepper, and the hock, pushing it down so it is submerged. Do not add salt yet — the hock will release a lot of it and you have no way of knowing how much until later.

  3. Bring to a boil, then drop to a low simmer with the lid slightly ajar. Skim any foam that rises in the first fifteen minutes.

  4. Simmer for two to two and a half hours, stirring every half hour and scraping the bottom. Split peas sink and scorch, and scorched pea soup tastes burnt all the way through with no way to fix it. If the pot is getting thick and sluggish, add water.

  5. It is ready when the peas have completely disintegrated and the soup is thick, uniform and pale green, with no distinct pea shapes left. If you still see individual peas, it needs longer — old dried peas take considerably more time and some very old ones never soften at all.

  6. Lift the hock out and let it cool enough to handle. Pull off the meat, discard the skin, bone and any hard fat, and chop the meat. Stir it back into the pot. Some hocks give a generous amount of meat and some give almost none; either way the gelatin has already gone into the soup, which is the main job.

  7. Now season. Add the vinegar first, then salt in small increments, tasting each time. A smoked hock varies enormously and a soup this thick swallows salt without seeming to change until suddenly it is too much.

  8. If it is thicker than you want — and it will thicken further as it cools — loosen it with water or stock. If it is thin, simmer uncovered until it holds a spoon track for a second. Serve with parsley and croutons.

Notes

  • Meatless version: leave out the hock, add 2 teaspoons of smoked paprika and 1 tablespoon of soy sauce, and use a well-made vegetable stock. It is a genuinely good soup, not a consolation prize, though it will need more salt.

  • Blending: most of the time it is unnecessary — properly cooked split peas do it themselves. If you want it silky, an immersion blender for thirty seconds gets you there.

  • Yellow split peas behave the same way and taste slightly milder and earthier. They take about the same time and look better in a bowl, if that matters to you.

Keeping it

Five days cold, and it sets to something you could slice. Loosen it with water when reheating and expect to add salt again. It freezes very well for four months, which makes it worth doubling — this is one of the few soups where the second batch costs almost no extra work.
