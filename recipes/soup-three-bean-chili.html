Soup, Chowder & Stew

Three Bean Chili

No meat, no apology, and no soy crumbles. Depth comes from toasted chiles, mushrooms and a long simmer.

8 bowls, about 3.5 litres
Makes
40 minutes
Hands on
1 hour 45 minutes
Start to finish

A vegetarian chili fails when it is built as a meat chili with the meat removed. What is actually missing is browning, fat and savoury depth, so this recipe replaces each of them deliberately: mushrooms cooked hard until the pan goes brown and sticky, a toasted whole-chile paste rather than powder, and soy sauce for glutamates. Three kinds of bean is not a gimmick either — black beans hold their shape and stay firm, pintos break down and thicken the pot, and chickpeas keep a distinct bite. Together they give you three textures in a spoonful, which is what a chili without meat is otherwise short of.

What you need

Chile paste

  • 4 dried ancho chiles, stemmed and seeded
  • 2 dried guajillo chiles, stemmed and seeded
  • 500 ml boiling water
  • 1 tablespoon cumin seed
  • 2 teaspoons dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika

Base

  • 4 tablespoons olive oil
  • 450 g mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 2 large onions, diced
  • 2 bell peppers, diced
  • 6 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce

The pot

  • 1 tin diced tomatoes, about 800 g
  • 1 litre vegetable stock
  • 400 g cooked black beans, drained
  • 400 g cooked pinto beans, drained
  • 400 g cooked chickpeas, drained
  • 1 tablespoon cocoa powder
  • 2 teaspoons fine salt, to taste
  • 1 tablespoon cider vinegar
  • 200 g corn kernels, optional

The table

  • 120 ml sour cream or cashew cream
  • 100 g crumbled feta or grated cheddar
  • 4 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1 lime, cut in wedges

How

  1. Toast the chiles in a dry skillet over medium heat, a few seconds a side, until fragrant and slightly softened. Do not let them darken past a light toasting — scorched chile is bitter and there is no fixing a pot it has gone into.

  2. Soak them in the boiling water for twenty minutes until fully soft. Toast and grind the cumin seed while you wait. Blend the chiles with 250 ml of the soaking liquid, the cumin, oregano and paprika into a smooth paste.

  3. Heat 2 tablespoons of the oil in a large pot over medium-high and add the mushrooms. Cook well past the point where they look done: the water comes out, the pot starts to hiss instead of bubble, the mushrooms shrink and brown, and a sticky brown film builds on the bottom. Ten to twelve minutes. That film is the meaty depth this chili is otherwise missing.

  4. Add the rest of the oil, the onions and the peppers with a pinch of salt, and cook until softened and browning at the edges, about eight minutes. Add the garlic and cook until fragrant.

  5. Add the tomato paste and cook until it darkens to brick, about a minute, then the chile paste, and cook it in the hot oil, stirring, until it smells roasted and has deepened in colour, three or four minutes.

  6. Add the soy sauce, tomatoes, stock and cocoa. Scrape the bottom of the pot clean and bring to a simmer.

  7. Simmer uncovered for forty-five minutes, until the liquid has reduced, the colour has gone from bright red to a deep brick, and the raw tomato taste is gone. Stir occasionally and scrape the bottom; a thick pot scorches easily.

  8. Add all three beans and the corn and simmer another twenty minutes, until the pintos have started to break down and thicken the chili and the black beans and chickpeas are hot through but still whole.

  9. Off the heat, add the vinegar and salt to taste. Vegetarian chili almost always needs more salt than you expect, because there is no cured meat in it doing that job quietly in the background.

  10. Serve with sour cream, cheese, cilantro and lime. The dairy and the acid are not garnish here; they are what balances a pot that is otherwise all earth and smoke.

Notes

  • Dried beans cooked from scratch are better and give you a starchy cooking liquid to use in place of some of the stock. Use 200 g dried per 400 g cooked and season the cooking water.

  • To make it vegan, skip the dairy toppings and use a cashew cream. Everything in the pot is already vegan as written.

  • Mash a ladleful of the beans against the side of the pot if you want it thicker. It is a better tool than cornstarch, which gives chili a glossy, slightly artificial texture.

Keeping it

Five days cold and better on the second day. It freezes for six months and is one of the most useful things to have in a freezer, since it reheats into a full meal with nothing else needed. Thin with stock on reheating; the beans keep drinking liquid.
