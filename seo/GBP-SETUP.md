# Google Business Profile — Setup Checklist

For Carrie and Eric, or whoever sits down at the counter iPad to do this.

This is the single highest-return hour of work available to this business. In a town of about 1,200, the map listing is the front door. Most people who end up at the store will have looked at this before they ever look at the website.

Do it in order. It takes about an hour to set up and ten minutes a week after that.

---

## 1. The NAP — get this exact, everywhere, forever

NAP means name, address, phone. Google cross-checks it against every other place the business appears online. One inconsistency ("Ste." vs "Suite", a different phone number on the Facebook page) is the most common reason a small business ranks below a competitor two towns away.

```
Twisted Roots Merc
151 N Gaither St
Siletz, OR 97380
United States
(541) 555-0134
https://twistedrootsmerc.com/
```

Rules:

- **Name field is the name only.** `Twisted Roots Merc`. Not "Twisted Roots Merc — Mercantile, Bakery & Kitchen." Not "Twisted Roots Merc | Siletz Hardware & Bakery." Google suspends profiles for keyword stuffing in the name, and a suspension takes weeks to undo.
- Copy that block into a note and paste it every time — Facebook, Yelp, Apple Business Connect, the Chamber listing, the Siletz Tribal directory if they have one. Do not retype it.
- **The phone number above is a placeholder.** Do not publish this profile until the real number is in. Changing the phone on a live profile resets some of the trust signals and can trigger re-verification.
- If the business occupies both 101 and 151 N Gaither St, pick **one** as the mailing/map address and stay with it. Two addresses reads as two businesses to Google. Mention the second building in the description and the photos, not in the address field.

Verification: this will almost certainly be a postcard to the physical address. Order it early — it takes 5 to 14 days and nothing else on the profile counts until it lands.

## 2. Categories

Google only ranks you for what the **primary** category says you are. The rest help but carry much less weight. Pick the one that matches how a neighbor would describe the place.

**Primary category:**

- `General store`

That is the right one and it is not close. It covers the whole building, it is what the store actually is, and it has almost no competition within 25 miles. "Grocery store" would put the store in a fight with Newport supermarkets it cannot win, and "Hardware store" would hide the kitchen.

**Secondary categories** (add all of these — Google allows up to 9 additional):

- `Bakery`
- `Breakfast restaurant`
- `Coffee shop`
- `Hardware store`
- `Sandwich shop`
- `Lumber store`
- `Convenience store`
- `Gift shop`
- `Camping store`

Alternatives worth swapping in later if the reporting shows them pulling: `Home improvement store`, `Deli`, `Building materials supplier`, `Plumbing supply store`, `Fishing store`, `Espresso bar`, `Grocery store`.

Note on category names: Google's exact list shifts and some entries are country-specific. Type the first few letters into the category field and take whatever Google offers that matches the intent above. If `Lumber store` does not appear, use `Building materials supplier`. If `Coffee shop` does not appear, use `Cafe`.

**Check the categories once a quarter.** Google adds and retires categories without telling anybody, and a retired primary category quietly stops ranking.

| Saturday | 6:30 AM | 8:00 PM |
| Sunday | 7:00 AM | 4:00 PM |

**More hours** (Google offers separate hour sets once the food categories are on — use them, they show up in the food results):

- **Kitchen:** 6:00 AM – 2:00 PM, seven days
- **Breakfast:** 6:00 AM – 11:00 AM, seven days
- **Lunch:** 11:00 AM – 2:00 PM, seven days

Do not create an "hours" entry for the bakery rack. It sells out, it is not a schedule, and a profile that says a department is open when it is empty generates the one-star review that says "drove over, nothing there."

**Special hours:** enter Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas, New Year's Day and July 4th every January, for the whole year. A profile showing "Hours might differ" on a holiday costs more walk-ins than being closed does.

**Storm days:** when Storm Mode goes on and the store is staying open late or closing early, edit the hours that day. It takes thirty seconds and it is the single most useful thing the profile can do all winter.

## 4. Attributes

Attributes are the checkboxes under "More" in the profile editor. They turn into the little icons on the listing and they feed filtered searches ("open now," "wheelchair accessible," "takeout"). Available attributes depend on which categories are on the profile, so some of these will only appear after the food categories are added.

**Accessibility**
- [x] Wheelchair accessible entrance — ramp at the front door
- [x] Wheelchair accessible parking lot
- [ ] Wheelchair accessible restroom — check the actual door width before ticking this
- [x] Wheelchair accessible seating

**Service options**
- [x] In-store shopping
- [x] In-store pickup
- [x] Curbside pickup — this is what the Yard already is; tick it
- [x] Dine-in
- [x] Takeout
- [ ] Delivery — leave off. The site says plainly that delivery is not happening yet, and the profile has to match the site.
- [ ] Same-day delivery — off

**Amenities**
- [x] Restroom
- [x] Free parking lot
- [x] Free street parking
- [x] Wi-Fi (if there is guest Wi-Fi; if not, leave it off rather than annoy people)
- [x] Good for kids
- [x] Dogs allowed — there is a biscuit jar at the register, so this one is earned

**Payments**
- [x] Credit cards
- [x] Debit cards
- [x] NFC mobile payments
- [x] Cash

**Dining / offerings** (these come with the restaurant categories)
- [x] Breakfast
- [x] Lunch
- [x] Coffee
- [x] Quick bite
- [x] Comfort food
- [x] Vegetarian options
- [x] Counter service

**Planning**
- [x] Quick visit
- [x] Family-friendly
- [ ] Accepts reservations — off

**Identity attributes** — only tick what is true. `Identifies as women-owned`, `Identifies as veteran-owned`, `Identifies as Indigenous-owned` and similar are self-declared and they matter to people locally, but a false one is worse than none.

## 7. Photo shot list

Photos are the highest-leverage thing after categories. Profiles with 25 or more photos get meaningfully more calls and direction requests. Take them on a phone, in daylight, no filters. Shoot vertical for the profile and horizontal for the website — different crops.

**Non-negotiable, do these first:**

1. The storefront, straight on, in daylight, whole false front in frame. This is the cover photo and it is what somebody matches against when they pull up out front.
2. The storefront again on an overcast wet day. Most people arrive in that weather and it is what the place actually looks like.
3. The carved sign, close, filling the frame.
4. The front door with the hours decal readable.
5. The parking area, showing there is room and it is gravel.
6. Interior from the front door looking down the aisle — the "will I be able to find anything" shot.
7. Carrie and Eric behind the counter, together, looking at the camera. This is the photo the whole thing hangs on. It goes in the Team category.

**The departments — one wide and one detail each:**

8. Everyday aisle, wide.
9. The cooler, open, stocked.
10. Fix It wall, wide, showing the density of it.
11. Fix It detail — the fastener bins.
12. Plumbing repair section, close enough to read the flapper packaging.
13. The Yard, wide, showing the racks under cover.
14. The dry shed with the concrete bags stacked off the ground.
15. Somebody loading a 4x4 into a pickup.
16. The storm wall, fully stocked, wide. Retake this one every October.
17. Storm wall detail — batteries and lanterns.
18. Outdoors section, fishing gear.
19. Local shelf, wide, the honey and the pottery together.

**Food — shoot these on the same morning, they carry the most weight:**

20. The bakery case, full, at 6:15 AM.
21. The Twisted Root cinnamon roll, iced, one roll, close.
22. A breakfast plate at the counter with a coffee next to it.
23. The Millworker cut in half so the inside is visible.
24. Biscuits coming out of the oven.
25. Coffee being poured.

**Ongoing:**

26. Seasonal — the first storm, the racks in summer, whatever is running in the river.
27. A short video, 30 seconds, walking from the front door through to the yard. Video posts get shown more than stills right now.

Retake the storefront and the storm wall every year. Old photos of a stocked shelf that is no longer stocked are how you get a disappointed review.

## 8. Q&A seeds

Google lets the business ask and answer its own questions. Do it — otherwise the questions sit unanswered or get answered wrong by a stranger. Post them from a personal Google account, then answer from the business profile, then upvote the answer.

Write them the way a neighbor would type them, not the way a marketer would.

1. **Q:** What time do you open?
   **A:** 6am Monday through Friday, 6:30am Saturday, 7am Sunday. Coffee is on before the doors are unlocked either way.

2. **Q:** Do you have a bakery?
   **A:** Yes. The rack is baked every morning and it is usually thin by nine. The count is on our website so you are not driving over to an empty case.

3. **Q:** Do you sell lumber?
   **A:** Some. Framing lumber, pressure treated posts, fence pickets and concrete, out back in the yard, everything 8 foot or shorter so it fits in a pickup. For a real load or long boards, call Siletz River Lumber here in town.

4. **Q:** Are you open when the power goes out?
   **A:** As long as we have power we are open, and we post it on the website. When a storm is coming we stock the wall heavier and put the hours up front.

5. **Q:** Do you have hardware, like plumbing parts?
   **A:** Yes. Fasteners, plumbing repair, electrical, sealants, tools and tarps. About 300 repair items, picked to cover the everyday fix rather than everything.

6. **Q:** Is there parking for a truck and trailer?
   **A:** Yes. Gravel lot out front with room along the side for trailers, and the yard is around back — pull straight in and we will help you load.

7. **Q:** Can I order breakfast ahead?
   **A:** Yes, on the website or by calling. No app and no account. We bag it with your name on it and you pay here or at the counter, either is fine.

8. **Q:** Do you take cards?
   **A:** Cards, debit, tap and cash.

9. **Q:** Are dogs allowed?
   **A:** Yes, and there is a biscuit jar at the register for them.

10. **Q:** How far is it from Newport?
    **A:** About 27 minutes. Toledo is 21, Lincoln City is 34.

11. **Q:** Can you order something you do not carry?
    **A:** We write down every request. When enough people ask for the same thing it goes on the shelf permanently, for everybody. We do not do one-off special orders. Tell us at the counter or on the website.

12. **Q:** Is it wheelchair accessible?
    **A:** Ramp at the front door, single level inside, wide aisles.

## 9. Asking for reviews

Reviews are the second-biggest ranking factor in the map after categories and proximity. The number matters, the recency matters more than people think, and replies matter.

**The rules that are not optional:**

- Never offer anything for a review. No discount, no free coffee, no entry into a drawing. It violates Google's policy and it is also the fastest way to get a batch of reviews filtered out.
- Never review-gate — that is, never ask "how was it" first and only send the happy ones to Google. Ask everybody the same way.
- Never post reviews from the store's own devices. Google notices the IP.

**Getting the link:** in the profile dashboard, "Ask for reviews" gives a short link. Shorten it further if it helps, print it on the postcard and the counter card, and put the QR alongside it.

**What to say at the counter** — this is the whole script, and it works because it does not oversell:

> "If you have a second sometime, a review on Google helps people find us. There's a card by the register with the link on it, or I can write it down for you."

**Note in the pickup bag** (printed slip, plain):

> Thanks for coming in.
> If the place is useful to you, a review on Google is the thing that helps most — it's how the next person finds us.
> twistedrootsmerc.com/review — or just tell us at the counter and we'll write it down for you.
> — Carrie & Eric

**In the email confirmation of a pickup order**, three hours later, one line:

> If that was any good, a Google review helps more than you'd think. If it wasn't, tell us first and we'll fix it.

**Replying:** reply to every review, good and bad, within two days. Keep it short and in the store's voice.

- Good review reply: *"Thanks Dave. Tell your brother we finally got the gluten free bread in."*
- Middling review reply: *"Fair. The rack does get picked clean early and we're baking more. Call ahead and we'll set one aside."*
- Bad review reply: name the thing, say what changed, do not argue, do not write a paragraph. *"Sorry — that shouldn't have happened. Come find Carrie next time you're in and we'll make it right."*

**Target:** 25 reviews in the first 90 days, then a steady handful a month. Steady beats a burst — 40 reviews in one week looks bought, to Google and to people.

## 10. Posting cadence

Posts expire from the profile after about six months but they show while they are live, and a profile that posts is treated as a profile that is being maintained.

**Every week — one post, five minutes:**

- Monday or Tuesday: what is on the bakery rack this week, or what just landed on a shelf.

**Every two weeks — one extra:**

- What is running in the river, or what got stocked because somebody asked for it. This is the post people actually read.

**Event posts:**

- Holiday hours, two weeks ahead.
- Storm Mode — post the day the forecast turns, saying what is stocked and what the hours will be. This is the highest-value post the store will ever make and it takes one minute.
- Anything happening in town the store is part of.

**Offer posts:** sparingly, and only when there is a real one. A profile full of manufactured deals reads like every other business. This store's whole pitch is that it is not doing that.

**Product refresh:** quarterly, on the seasonal swap noted in section 6.

**Photo upload:** two or three new photos a month. Phone photos, no production.

**A note on the voice:** the profile is not a different brand. Same as the site — plain, dry, useful, no exclamation points. "The rack was gone by 8:30 today. Sorry. More tomorrow." is a better post than anything with the word "delicious" in it.

## 11. After Google — the other listings

Same NAP block, copied not retyped. In rough order of how much they matter here:

1. **Apple Business Connect** — free, and it is what CarPlay and iPhone Maps read. Almost nobody in a small town does this, which is exactly why it is worth an hour.
2. **Bing Places** — imports straight from the Google profile in a couple of clicks.
3. **Facebook page** — the address and hours must match exactly. This is the most commonly wrong one.
4. **Yelp** — claim it so nobody else does, fill it in, then largely ignore it.
5. **Lincoln County / Newport Chamber, Oregon Coast visitor directories, Siletz community listings** — real local links from real local sites, which is the kind of link that actually moves a small map listing.
6. **Nextdoor business page** — in a town this size, this is where the "is anybody open" conversation actually happens.

## 12. The ten-minute weekly routine

Once it is set up, this is the whole ongoing job:

- [ ] Reply to any new reviews
- [ ] Answer any new questions
- [ ] One post
- [ ] Check hours are right for anything unusual coming up this week
- [ ] Upload a photo if there is one worth uploading

Once a quarter, add: check the categories still exist, refresh the products, re-read the description.
