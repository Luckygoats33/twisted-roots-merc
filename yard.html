Small on purpose

Not a lumberyard. A project rescue.

Siletz River Lumber handles real lumber orders and does it properly. We're not competing with that. The Yard exists for one question:

“I'm halfway through this and I need one more thing — today.”

Eight to ten lumber SKUs. A dry shed for concrete, raised off the ground and under cover, because paper bags and Oregon Coast moisture do not get along.

Everything's 8 foot or shorter and fits in a pickup. If you need twelves, fourteens, sheet goods, siding or an actual load — call Siletz River Lumber. We'll give you the number.

Free advice, worth what you paid

Setting a post, the easy way

StepWhat you doWhat you need
1Dig the hole about three times the post width, and a third of the post deep.Post hole digger (we rent ours out, ask)
2Drop 4–6 inches of gravel in the bottom so water drains instead of sitting.Landscape gravel, 50 lb
3Set the post, brace it plumb. Check it twice — concrete does not negotiate.4×4×8 PT, torpedo level
4Pour fast-set in dry, then pour water on top. No mixing tub, no wheelbarrow.Fast-setting concrete, 50 lb
5Wait 40 minutes before you hang anything on it. Wait 4 hours before you lean on it.Coffee, $3, inside

One bag of fast-set does one 4×4 in most soil around here. Two if the hole got away from you, which it usually does.
